VSEPR Theory & Molecular Geometry Derivation

Rigorous derivation of Steric Number, lone-pair repulsion hierarchies, spatial domain geometry versus observed nuclear shape, and net vectorial dipole moment cancellation.

4-Step Geometry Derivation Protocol

  1. Calculate Steric Number (SN):
    Count the valence electrons on the central atom and sum the number of bonded atoms (\(\sigma\)-bonds) plus lone pairs:
    $$\text{SN} = (\text{Bond Pairs}) + (\text{Lone Pairs}) = \tfrac{1}{2} [V + M - C + A]$$
    Result: Fixes the parent electron-domain geometry (Linear, Trigonal Planar, Tetrahedral, Trigonal Bipyramidal, Octahedral).
  2. Identify Electron Domain Arrangement:
    Electron domains repel each other to maximize distance and minimize Coulombic repulsion in 3D space:
    • SN = 4 → Tetrahedral (109.5°)
    • SN = 5 → Trigonal Bipyramidal (Axial 180°, Equatorial 120°)
    • SN = 6 → Octahedral (All 90° angles)
  3. Crux Step · The Exam Trap
    Position Lone Pairs According to Repulsion Hierarchy:
    Repulsion order: Lone Pair - Lone Pair > Lone Pair - Bond Pair > Bond Pair - Bond Pair.
    • In Trigonal Bipyramidal (SN=5), lone pairs occupy equatorial positions to minimize 90° repulsions (e.g. SF₄ is See-saw).
    • In Octahedral (SN=6), two lone pairs occupy trans-axial (opposite 180°) positions to minimize repulsion, yielding a Square Planar molecular geometry (e.g. XeF₄).
  4. Sum Vectorial Bond Dipoles & Net Dipole Moment:
    A molecule has \(\mu_{\text{net}} = 0\) only if bond dipole vectors geometrically cancel:
    In \(\text{XeF}_4\), four equal \(\text{Xe–F}\) dipoles lie in the same plane at 90° pointing outward, completely canceling out (\(\sum \vec{\mu} = \vec{0}\)). In \(\text{SF}_4\), the see-saw geometry creates a persistent equatorial-axial vector sum (\(\mu_{\text{net}} \neq 0\)).

Watch One · Authentic JEE Main Exemplar

Problem: Predict the molecular geometry and net dipole moment of \(\text{XeF}_4\) and \(\text{SF}_4\) respectively.

  1. For \(\text{XeF}_4\): Xe has 8 valence electrons. With 4 F atoms, it forms 4 \(\sigma\)-bonds and retains 2 lone pairs. Steric Number \(= 4 + 2 = 6\) (Octahedral domain). The 2 lone pairs sit opposite each other (180°), leaving 4 F atoms in a single plane → Square Planar. Symmetrical dipole vectors cancel → \(\mu = 0\).
  2. For \(\text{SF}_4\): S has 6 valence electrons. With 4 F atoms, it forms 4 \(\sigma\)-bonds and retains 1 lone pair. Steric Number \(= 4 + 1 = 5\) (Trigonal Bipyramidal domain). The 1 lone pair occupies an equatorial position → See-saw geometry. Dipoles do not cancel → \(\mu \neq 0\).
  3. Conclusion: \(\text{XeF}_4\) is square planar and nonpolar (\(\mu = 0\)); \(\text{SF}_4\) is see-saw and polar (\(\mu \neq 0\)).
