Unit 1 of 4

Oxidation Number: Bookkeeping & Rule Priority Ladder

What is happening?

An oxidation number is a formal bookkeeping charge assigned to an atom by assuming all shared bonding electrons belong completely to the more electronegative element.

Key Idea

Never guess oxidation states! Always follow the strict rule priority ladder from highest precedence to lowest.

  • 1. Elemental Form: Any uncombined element in its allotropic state has $\text{ON} = 0$ (e.g. $\text{H}_2, \text{O}_2, \text{P}_4, \text{S}_8, \text{Fe}$).
  • 2. Monatomic Ions: $\text{ON} = \text{ionic charge}$ (e.g. $\text{Na}^+ = +1, \text{Cl}^- = -1, \text{Al}^{3+} = +3$).
  • 3. High Priority Elements: Fluorine is always $-1$. Group 1 alkali metals are always $+1$. Group 2 alkaline earth metals are always $+2$.
  • Crux Exceptions
    Hydrogen & Oxygen: $\text{H}$ is usually $+1$ (except in metal hydrides like $\text{NaH}$ where $\text{H} = -1$). $\text{O}$ is usually $-2$ (except peroxides $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ where $\text{O} = -1$, and $\text{OF}_2$ where $\text{O} = +2$).

Unit 2 of 4

Oxidation & Reduction: The Dual Perspective

What is happening?

Oxidation and reduction are complementary and simultaneous: electrons released by the reducing agent must be captured by the oxidizing agent.

Key Idea

OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss of electrons ($\text{ON} \uparrow$). Reduction Is Gain of electrons ($\text{ON} \downarrow$).

Agent Identification Model: $\text{Zn} + \text{CuSO}_4 \to \text{ZnSO}_4 + \text{Cu}$

  1. Assign oxidation states: $\text{Zn}^0 \to \text{Zn}^{+2}$, and $\text{Cu}^{+2} \to \text{Cu}^0$. $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ is a spectator ion ($\text{S} = +6, \text{O} = -2$).
  2. $\text{Zn}$ loses 2 electrons $\implies$ $\text{Zn}$ is oxidised $\implies$ $\text{Zn}$ is the Reducing Agent.
  3. $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ gains 2 electrons $\implies$ $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ (or $\text{CuSO}_4$) is reduced $\implies$ $\text{CuSO}_4$ is the Oxidising Agent.

Unit 3 of 4

Disproportionation & Comproportionation Reactions

What is happening?

In a disproportionation reaction, the same element in a single intermediate oxidation state is simultaneously oxidized to a higher state and reduced to a lower state.

Key Idea

For an element to disproportionate, it MUST exist in an intermediate oxidation state (capable of both rising and falling).

Worked Example: Thermal Decomposition of Hydrogen Peroxide

$$2\text{H}_2\text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{O}_2$$

  1. In $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$, Oxygen has oxidation state $-1$.
  2. In $\text{O}_2$, Oxygen has oxidation state $0$ ($\text{ON}$ rises by $1 \implies$ oxidation).
  3. In $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, Oxygen has oxidation state $-2$ ($\text{ON}$ falls by $1 \implies$ reduction).
  4. Since the single species $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ undergoes both oxidation and reduction, it is a classic disproportionation reaction.

Unit 4 of 4

Distinguishing Redox from Non-Redox Reactions

Not all chemical reactions involve electron transfer. Neutralisation and double displacement reactions are strictly non-redox.

1. Non-Redox: Double Displacement

$$\text{AgNO}_3 + \text{NaCl} \to \text{AgCl}\downarrow + \text{NaNO}_3$$

  • $\text{Ag}: +1 \to +1$ (no change)
  • $\text{N}: +5 \to +5$ (no change)
  • $\text{O}: -2 \to -2$ (no change)
  • $\text{Na}: +1 \to +1$ (no change)
  • $\text{Cl}: -1 \to -1$ (no change)

Zero change in oxidation states $\implies$ NON-REDOX.

2. Redox: Displacement

$$\text{Fe} + \text{CuSO}_4 \to \text{FeSO}_4 + \text{Cu}$$

  • $\text{Fe}: 0 \to +2$ (oxidised, $\text{ON} \uparrow$)
  • $\text{Cu}: +2 \to 0$ (reduced, $\text{ON} \downarrow$)
  • $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ remains static.

Electron transfer occurs $\implies$ AUTHENTIC REDOX.
