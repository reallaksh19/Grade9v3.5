Unit 1 · Distance vs. Displacement & Path Reversal

What is Happening?

A particle executes a sequence of movements along a straight axis. Distance accumulates every segment scalar length regardless of orientation. Displacement records only the net change from initial to final position: \(\Delta x = x_f - x_i\).

Key Idea

Reversal is the decisive criterion. Unidirectional motion preserves \(s = |\Delta x|\). The instant velocity reverses sign, \(s > |\Delta x|\). Distance is monotonically non-decreasing, while displacement can decrease or vanish.

4-Step Kinematic Setup Procedure

  1. Fix the origin and sign convention: Declare \(x=0\) and whether right/upwards is positive. Keep this choice invariant throughout the analysis.
  2. Identify all turning events: Set \(v(t) = 0\) to detect instants where motion changes direction.
  3. Segment the journey by turning points: Compute the scalar length \(|\Delta x_k|\) of each monotonic leg. Sum for distance: \(s = \sum |\Delta x_k|\).
  4. Vector subtraction for net displacement: Evaluate \(\Delta x = x_{\text{final}} - x_{\text{initial}}\). Verify that \(s \ge |\Delta x|\).
Watch Out (Common Misconception)

Never integrate \(\int v\,dt\) directly to find distance when \(v(t)\) changes sign! \(\int v\,dt\) yields displacement. Total distance requires integrating absolute speed: \(s = \int |v(t)|\,dt\).

Worked Example: Particle with Reversal

Problem: A particle starts at \(x = 0\), moves to \(x = +8\text{ m}\) in \(4\text{ s}\), then reverses to \(x = +2\text{ m}\) in \(2\text{ s}\). Find total distance, displacement, average speed, and average velocity.

1. Leg 1: \(\Delta x_1 = 8 - 0 = +8\text{ m}\), length \(= 8\text{ m}\).
2. Leg 2: \(\Delta x_2 = 2 - 8 = -6\text{ m}\), length \(= 6\text{ m}\).
3. Total Distance: \(s = 8 + 6 = 14\text{ m}\).
4. Net Displacement: \(\Delta x = x_f - x_i = 2 - 0 = +2\text{ m}\).
5. Total Elapsed Time: \(\Delta t = 4 + 2 = 6\text{ s}\).
6. Average Speed: \(v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{14\text{ m}}{6\text{ s}} = 2.33\text{ m/s}\).
7. Average Velocity: \(\vec{v}_{\text{avg}} = \frac{+2\text{ m}}{6\text{ s}} = +0.33\text{ m/s}\).
Self-Check Question: Round Trip Ratio
Question: An athlete runs around a circular track of circumference \(400\text{ m}\) in \(50\text{ s}\), returning to the starting block. What is the ratio of average velocity to average speed?

Answer: Zero. Displacement is \(\Delta \vec{x} = 0\), so \(\vec{v}_{\text{avg}} = 0\text{ m/s}\). Average speed is \(v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{400}{50} = 8\text{ m/s}\). Ratio \(= \frac{0}{8} = 0\).

Unit 2 · Average Speed: Equal-Time vs. Equal-Distance Weighting

What is Happening?

A car travels at speed \(v_1\) and then at speed \(v_2\). Whether average speed is the arithmetic mean or harmonic mean depends entirely on whether the journey was split equally in time or in distance.

Key Idea

Equal-time weighting biases toward the higher speed: \(v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{v_1 + v_2}{2}\). Equal-distance weighting biases toward the slower speed because more time is spent traversing the distance at the lower speed: \(v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{2 v_1 v_2}{v_1 + v_2}\).

Derivation: Equal-Distance Harmonic Mean

Let each half of the total distance \(2d\) be \(d\).
Time for first half: \(t_1 = \frac{d}{v_1}\).
Time for second half: \(t_2 = \frac{d}{v_2}\).
Total time: \(T = t_1 + t_2 = d\left(\frac{1}{v_1} + \frac{1}{v_2}\right) = d \frac{v_1 + v_2}{v_1 v_2}\).
\[v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{2d}{T} = \frac{2d}{d \frac{v_1 + v_2}{v_1 v_2}} = \frac{2 v_1 v_2}{v_1 + v_2}\] Notice: By Cauchy-Schwarz / AM-HM inequality, \(\frac{2 v_1 v_2}{v_1 + v_2} \le \frac{v_1 + v_2}{2}\), with equality iff \(v_1 = v_2\).

Unit 3 · The Constant-Acceleration Kinematic Hub

Every constant-\(a\) problem connects five parameters: \(u\) (initial velocity), \(v\) (final velocity), \(a\) (acceleration), \(s\) (displacement), and \(t\) (time). Selecting the right formula eliminates the unknown you do not need:

• Missing \(s\): \(v = u + at\)
• Missing \(v\): \(s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2\)
• Missing \(t\): \(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\)
• Missing \(a\): \(s = \frac{u + v}{2} t\)
• Distance in \(n\)-th second: \(s_n = u + \frac{a}{2}(2n - 1)\)
Watch Out (Sign Consistency Trap)

In vertical projection under gravity, if upwards is taken as positive, acceleration must be entered as \(a = -g = -9.8\text{ m/s}^2\). At the peak, \(v = 0\), but acceleration is still \(-g\), never zero!
