A crate rests on a rough horizontal floor. It is pulled by a force P at an upward angle theta. The coefficients mu_s and mu_k are known, but the statement does not say whether the crate slips. For a specified P, determine the crate's acceleration or justify that it remains at rest.

Builds on

  • A small block of mass m rests on a larger block of mass M. The floor under the larger…

Conditions

  • Resolve the applied force into horizontal and vertical components.
  • The crate remains in contact with the floor.
  • Do not assume a friction equality before deciding the contact state.

Commit to a model or representation and your first relation before opening support.

Review and solution

What changed

The learner must infer the contact state and choose the static-feasibility or kinetic-friction model instead of being told that the bodies are at impending slip or already sliding.

What stayed valid

The normal force from the actual force balance, the provisional no-slip requirement and its comparison with μ_s N all carry over; what changes is that the contact state is not given and must be decided.

Answer

First find N = mg - P sin(theta). Under a provisional no-slip state, the friction required is f_required = P cos(theta). If |f_required| <= mu_s N, the crate can remain at rest and static friction equals the required value. If that inequality fails, sliding occurs; then use kinetic friction mu_k N opposite the sliding and apply Newton II to find acceleration.

  1. The upward component of P changes the normal reaction, so N is not automatically mg.
  2. The coefficient data do not identify the contact state; test the static state first.
  3. Compare the no-slip friction requirement with the static limit.
  4. Only after the static state is impossible is the kinetic-friction model selected for the acceleration calculation.

Rubric

  • Computes the actual normal reaction before any friction limit.
  • Tests the no-slip friction requirement against mu_s N before choosing a kinetic model.
  • Uses kinetic friction only after static feasibility fails.

Independent check

At P=0, the method must allow friction to be zero rather than mu_s mg.

Repair

Revisit: Assume no relative sliding provisionally and solve the tangential Newton-II equation for the static friction value f_required that would make that motion possible.

Lineage check

Conditions

  • The track is frictionless.

Review and solution

What changed

The learner must choose and change system boundaries across a multi-body solution, rather than being handed separate-body versus whole-system equations.

What stayed valid

Maintained contact still gives one shared acceleration, and a contact force still belongs only in the equation of the body it acts on; what changes is that you choose which system to isolate.

Answer

Use A+B+C as one system for the acceleration: a = F/(3m). Then isolate C to find the requested contact force: N_BC = m a = F/3. The B-on-C force is internal only to the three-cart system; it is external to cart C and must remain in C's equation.

  1. Maintained contact gives the carts one common acceleration along the track.
  2. For the acceleration, the whole three-cart system removes internal contact forces from the summed equation.
  3. For the requested B-on-C contact force, isolate C because that interaction is external to C.
  4. Newton II on C gives N_BC = mF/(3m) = F/3.

  • Chooses the whole train for the acceleration calculation.
  • Switches to cart C for the requested contact force.
  • Keeps the same common acceleration across both equations.

Builds on

Conditions

  • 0 < theta < 90 degrees.
  • The block remains in contact with the floor.
  • Assume cos(theta)-mu_s sin(theta) > 0.

Commit to a model or representation and your first relation before opening support.

Review and solution

What changed

The learner must carry a force that changes both the tangential demand and the normal reaction through the static-friction threshold instead of treating mu_s mg as a fixed limit.

What stayed valid

Static friction is still at most μ_s N and equals it only at impending slip; what changes is that the push also presses the block into the floor, so N grows with P.

Answer

At impending slip, N=mg+P sin(theta) and P cos(theta)=mu_s N, so P_max = mu_s m g / (cos(theta)-mu_s sin(theta)).

  1. The downward component of P increases the normal reaction, so the vertical balance gives N=mg+P sin(theta).
  2. For a static state, friction must oppose the horizontal push; the maximum available value is reached only at impending slip.
  3. At the threshold, P cos(theta)=mu_s[mg+P sin(theta)].
  4. Rearranging gives P[cos(theta)-mu_s sin(theta)]=mu_s mg and hence the stated P_max.

Rubric

  • Computes N from the vertical force balance including the downward component of P.
  • Uses static-friction equality only at impending slip.
  • Derives the threshold expression with the force-angle term retained in both horizontal and vertical components.

Independent check

Setting theta=0 reduces the result to P_max=mu_s mg, the ordinary horizontal-push threshold.

Repair

Revisit: Build the chosen-body FBD and determine the normal reaction N from the force balance or acceleration perpendicular to the contact; do not assume N = mg.

Lineage check

Static friction is still at most μ_s N and equals it only at impending slip; what changes is that the push also presses the block into the floor, so N grows with P.

The learner must carry a force that changes both the tangential demand and the normal reaction through the static-friction threshold instead of treating mu_s mg as a fixed limit.

  • A block of mass m is on a smooth fixed plane that rises to the right at angle theta…

Conditions

  • 0 < theta < 90 degrees.
  • The applied force is horizontal.
  • Do not assume the static-friction direction in advance; use a signed required value.

Commit to a model or representation and your first relation before opening support.

Review and solution

What changed

The learner must integrate force decomposition, altered normal reaction, signed friction demand and static feasibility in dependency order rather than copying N=mg cos(theta) or f=mu_s N.

What stayed valid

The normal force from the perpendicular balance, the friction that rest requires, and the test against μ_s N all carry over; what changes is that a horizontal push alters both N and the required friction at once.

The deciding move

Use the sign of f_required to determine the required friction direction and test its magnitude against mu_s N.

Answer

With uphill positive, N=mg cos(theta)+P sin(theta). The static friction required by rest is f_required=mg sin(theta)-P cos(theta). Static equilibrium is feasible exactly when |mg sin(theta)-P cos(theta)| <= mu_s[mg cos(theta)+P sin(theta)].

  1. Resolve P into P cos(theta) uphill and P sin(theta) into the plane.
  2. The perpendicular rest condition gives N=mg cos(theta)+P sin(theta).
  3. The tangential rest equation P cos(theta)+f_s-mg sin(theta)=0 gives the signed required friction f_required=mg sin(theta)-P cos(theta).
  4. Do not choose a friction direction separately; the sign of f_required supplies it.
  5. The static model is feasible when the magnitude of that required value does not exceed mu_s N.

Rubric

  • Includes the inward perpendicular component P sin(theta) when finding N.
  • Solves a signed f_required from the tangential equilibrium equation.
  • Tests |f_required| against mu_s N instead of setting friction equal to its limit immediately.

Independent check

At P=0 the condition reduces to tan(theta)<=mu_s; if P cos(theta)=mg sin(theta), the required friction is zero.

Repair

Revisit: Build the chosen-body FBD and determine the normal reaction N from the force balance or acceleration perpendicular to the contact; do not assume N = mg.

Lineage check

Conditions

  • The block is on a smooth fixed incline.
  • Only gravity and the normal reaction act.

Review and solution

What changed

The learner must recognize the same force/component double-counting error after it moves from a drawn FBD into algebraic component equations.

What stayed valid

Weight is still one force described by two components, and the normal force is still the only other interaction; what changes is that the double count appears in equations rather than on a drawing.

Answer

The solution double-counts the same weight vector. In plane-aligned equations, gravity contributes mg sin(theta) down the plane and mg cos(theta) into the plane; the unresolved mg is not added again. Thus the tangential gravity term is mg sin(theta), and the perpendicular equation uses N-mg cos(theta)=0 for maintained contact.

  1. Weight mg is one physical force, not three.
  2. Resolving it replaces its representation in the chosen component equations.
  3. The original vector magnitude mg is not itself a signed scalar component along either tilted axis.
  4. Use the two appropriate components in the two equations and keep the normal as the separate contact force.

Rubric

  • Identifies double-counting of gravity as the error.
  • Uses mg sin(theta) only in the tangential gravity contribution.
  • Uses mg cos(theta) only in the perpendicular gravity contribution and N separately.

Independent check

The corrected equations account for exactly two physical interactions: Earth-on-block gravity and plane-on-block normal contact.

Repair

Revisit: When a physical force is resolved into components for chosen axes, keep the body-force inventory unchanged: use the components as a representation of that one force in the equations, not as additional interaction forces alongside the original vector.

Lineage check

A block starts from rest and travels the same distance s down the same incline of angle theta in two trials. On a smooth surface the time is t_0. On a rough surface the block slides throughout and the time is t > t_0. Assuming kinetic friction coefficient mu_k is constant, derive mu_k in terms of theta, t_0 and t.

Builds on

  • A block is already sliding down a rough fixed plane inclined at angle theta. The…

Conditions

  • Both trials start from rest at the same point.
  • The incline angle and travel distance are unchanged.
  • The rough-surface trial has established downward sliding with constant mu_k.

Commit to a model or representation and your first relation before opening support.

Review and solution

What changed

The learner must combine the established rough-incline dynamics with an independent timing representation to infer the friction coefficient, rather than being asked directly for acceleration.

What stayed valid

The rough-slope acceleration g(sin θ − μ_k cos θ) and the smooth-slope g sin θ still hold; what changes is that μ_k must be inferred from travel times instead of being used to find an acceleration.

Answer

Smooth acceleration is a_0=g sin(theta), rough acceleration is a=g[sin(theta)-mu_k cos(theta)]. Since s=(1/2)at^2 from rest, a/a_0=t_0^2/t^2. Therefore mu_k=tan(theta)[1-t_0^2/t^2].

  1. The smooth trial gives a_0=g sin(theta).
  2. The rough sliding trial gives a=g[sin(theta)-mu_k cos(theta)].
  3. Equal distance from rest gives s=(1/2)a_0 t_0^2=(1/2)a t^2, hence a/a_0=t_0^2/t^2.
  4. Substitute the two incline accelerations and solve for mu_k.
  5. This yields mu_k=tan(theta)[1-t_0^2/t^2].

Rubric

  • Builds the smooth and rough incline accelerations from the corresponding force models.
  • Uses equal-distance constant-acceleration kinematics to relate a/a_0 to t_0^2/t^2.
  • Solves to mu_k=tan(theta)[1-t_0^2/t^2] and checks its limiting sign.

Independent check

Because t>t_0, the bracket is positive. If t approaches t_0, the inferred mu_k approaches zero.

Repair

Revisit: Use |f_s| = mu_s N only at impending slip; if relative sliding is already established, use |f_k| = mu_k N with direction opposing the relative sliding.

Lineage check

The rough-slope acceleration g(sin θ − μ_k cos θ) and the smooth-slope g sin θ still hold; what changes is that μ_k must be inferred from travel times instead of being used to find an acceleration.

The learner must combine the established rough-incline dynamics with an independent timing representation to infer the friction coefficient, rather than being asked directly for acceleration.
