In Olympiad competitions like the International Mathematics Olympiad (IMO) and ExamSIDE Foundation, coordinate geometry questions never test isolated formulas. Instead, they require changed-demand transfer—applying coordinate invariants to solve multi-step geometric, algebraic, and transformational challenges:
1. Multi-Statement Truth Vectors Evaluating composite T/F statements across reflections, arbitrary lines \( x = k \), and area invariants.
2. Coordinate Reflections (Mirror Images) Reflecting geometric figures across axis lines and computing the area of resulting symmetric diamonds.
3. Equilateral & Special Shape Synthesis Constructing non-trivial equilateral polygons using Pythagorean distance relations on the axes.
4. Parametric Collinearity & Integer Loci Solving for unknown variables \( a, b, k \) constrained to linear proportionality and integer domains.
Challenge 1 IMO Achievers Signature
3 MARKS · HOTS
State 'T' for True and 'F' for False for the following four statements:
  1. If point \( P(a, b) \) lies strictly in Quadrant III, then its reflection across the y-axis followed by reflection across the x-axis lies strictly in Quadrant I.
  2. The perpendicular distance of any point \( P(x, y) \) from the vertical line \( x = k \) is given by \( |x - k| \).
  3. If opposite vertices of a square have coordinates \( (a, b) \) and \( (c, d) \), then the area of the square is given by \( \frac{1}{2}\left[(a - c)^2 + (b - d)^2\right] \).
  4. If the three distinct points \( (k, 2 - 2k) \), \( (1 - k, 2k) \), and \( (-4 - k, 6 - 2k) \) are collinear, then the unique value of \( k \) is \( -1 \).
Step-by-Step Proof of All 4 Statements:
  • Statement 1 is TRUE: In Quadrant III, \( a < 0 \) and \( b < 0 \). Reflecting across the y-axis negates the x-coordinate: \( (-a, b) \). Then reflecting across the x-axis negates the y-coordinate: \( (-a, -b) \). Since \( -a > 0 \) and \( -b > 0 \), \( (-a, -b) \) lies in Quadrant I.
  • Statement 2 is TRUE: The line \( x = k \) is vertical. The shortest distance from \( P(x, y) \) to the vertical line is horizontal, which is exactly the difference between their abscissae: \( |x - k| \).
  • Statement 3 is TRUE: The segment joining opposite vertices is a diagonal \( d \). By distance formula, \( d^2 = (a - c)^2 + (b - d)^2 \). For any square of side \( s \), \( d = s\sqrt{2} \implies s^2 = \frac{1}{2}d^2 \). Thus, \( \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\left[(a - c)^2 + (b - d)^2\right] \).
  • Statement 4 is TRUE: Equating step slopes: \[ m_1 = \frac{2k - (2 - 2k)}{(1 - k) - k} = \frac{4k - 2}{1 - 2k} = \frac{-2(1 - 2k)}{1 - 2k} = -2 \] Slope between second and third points: \[ m_2 = \frac{(6 - 2k) - 2k}{(-4 - k) - (1 - k)} = \frac{6 - 4k}{-5} \] Equating slopes: \( \frac{6 - 4k}{-5} = -2 \implies 6 - 4k = 10 \implies -4k = 4 \implies k = -1 \).
💡 Transfer Insight: Notice how Statement 4 yields a constant slope \( -2 \) independent of \( k \) for the first pair of points! This algebraic cancellation is a classic Olympiad trap designed to reward careful factoring over brute-force expansion.
Challenge 2 Geometric Reflection & Area
3 MARKS · HOTS
A triangle on the Cartesian plane has vertices \( A(1, 2) \), \( B(4, 2) \), and \( C(3, 5) \).
  1. The triangle is reflected across its horizontal base line \( y = 2 \) to produce triangle \( ABC' \). Find the coordinates of \( C' \).
  2. What is the precise geometric classification and total area of the combined quadrilateral \( ACBC' \)?
Mathematical Derivation:
  1. Reflection across \( y = 2 \): The x-coordinate remains unchanged (\( x = 3 \)). The distance of \( C \) from \( y = 2 \) is \( 5 - 2 = 3 \) units upward. The mirror image must lie 3 units downward: \( y' = 2 - 3 = -1 \). Thus, \( C'(3, -1) \).
  2. Quadrilateral Shape: Adjacent sides \( AC = AC' = \sqrt{(3-1)^2 + (5-2)^2} = \sqrt{4 + 9} = \sqrt{13} \).
    Adjacent sides \( BC = BC' = \sqrt{(4-3)^2 + (2-5)^2} = \sqrt{1 + 9} = \sqrt{10} \).
    Since two distinct pairs of adjacent sides are equal (\( \sqrt{13} \neq \sqrt{10} \)), \( ACBC' \) is strictly a Kite (not a rhombus).
  3. Area Calculation via Orthogonal Diagonals: Horizontal diagonal \( AB = 4 - 1 = 3 \).
    Vertical diagonal \( CC' = 5 - (-1) = 6 \).
    Since the diagonals intersect orthogonally at \( (3, 2) \): \[ \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times d_1 \times d_2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 3 \times 6 = 9\text{ sq. units}. \]
💡 Transfer Insight: In any reflection across a line containing one of the diagonals ($AB$), the line of reflection acts as an axis of symmetry, guaranteeing orthogonal diagonals ($AB \perp CC'$). The area is always half the product of the diagonals.
Challenge 3 ExamSIDE / PRMO Foundation
3 MARKS · HOTS
Two vertices of an equilateral triangle are \( O(0, 0) \) and \( A(2a, 0) \), where \( a > 0 \). An equilateral triangle \( \triangle OAB \) is formed in the upper half-plane (\( y > 0 \)), and a square \( OACD \) is constructed on side \( OA \) in the lower half-plane (\( y \le 0 \)).
Find the exact distance between vertex \( B \) and the center \( M \) of the square.
Geometric Coordinate Deduction:
  1. Coordinates of Vertex B: In equilateral \( \triangle OAB \), side length is \( 2a \). The altitude from \( B \) bisects base \( OA \) at \( x = a \).
    By Pythagorean theorem: \[ h = \sqrt{(2a)^2 - a^2} = \sqrt{4a^2 - a^2} = a\sqrt{3} \] Since \( B \) is in the upper half-plane (\( y > 0 \)), \( B = (a, a\sqrt{3}) \).
  2. Coordinates of Center M: The square has side \( 2a \) extending downward along \( x \in [0, 2a] \) and \( y \in [0, -2a] \).
    Its center \( M \) is the midpoint of diagonals: \[ x_M = \frac{0 + 2a}{2} = a, \quad y_M = \frac{0 + (-2a)}{2} = -a \implies M(a, -a). \]
  3. Distance \( BM \): Both points share identical abscissa \( x = a \). Hence, the distance is strictly vertical: \[ BM = y_B - y_M = a\sqrt{3} - (-a) = a(\sqrt{3} + 1). \]
Challenge 4 Algebraic Invariant / Diophantine
3 MARKS · HOTS
Three points on the Cartesian plane are given as \( P(a, 0) \), \( Q(0, b) \), and \( R(1, 1) \), where \( a, b \neq 0 \).
  1. Find the governing relation between \( a \) and \( b \) such that \( P, Q, R \) are strictly collinear.
  2. If \( a \) and \( b \) are constrained to be positive integers, find the total number of ordered pairs \( (a, b) \) that satisfy this condition.
Collinearity & Number-Theoretic Deduction:
  1. Collinearity Condition via Slopes: Slope \( PQ = \frac{b - 0}{0 - a} = -\frac{b}{a} \).
    Slope \( PR = \frac{1 - 0}{1 - a} = \frac{1}{1 - a} \).
    Equating slopes: \[ -\frac{b}{a} = \frac{1}{1 - a} \implies -b(1 - a) = a \implies -b + ab = a \implies ab = a + b. \] Dividing both sides by \( ab \): \[ \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} = 1. \]
  2. Positive Integer Solutions: Rearranging \( ab - a - b = 0 \): \[ ab - a - b + 1 = 1 \implies (a - 1)(b - 1) = 1. \] Since \( a, b \) are positive integers, \( a - 1 \) and \( b - 1 \) must be positive integer divisors of 1: \[ a - 1 = 1 \implies a = 2, \quad b - 1 = 1 \implies b = 2. \] Thus, there is exactly 1 pair: \( (2, 2) \).
