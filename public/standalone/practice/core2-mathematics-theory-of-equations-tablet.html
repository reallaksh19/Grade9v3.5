Q9 · Roots → Factors → Equation

Find an equation whose roots are \(0, 1, -3/2, 5/2\).

Small Clue
Each root \(r\) gives the factor \(x - r\). Watch the sign of \(-3/2\): \(x - (-3/2) = x + 3/2\). Pair \(x(x-1)\) and \((x + 3/2)(x - 5/2)\) before multiplying everything.
👁️ Reveal Worked Solution & Crux Move
Full Algebraic Derivation:
1. Form factors: \(P(x) = x(x - 1)(x + 3/2)(x - 5/2) = 0\).
2. Multiply fractional factors by 4 to get integer coefficients: \(4P(x) = x(x - 1)(2x + 3)(2x - 5) = 0\).
3. Expand in pairs:
   \(x(x - 1) = x^2 - x\)
   \((2x + 3)(2x - 5) = 4x^2 - 10x + 6x - 15 = 4x^2 - 4x - 15\)
4. Multiply the two quadratic trinomials:
   \((x^2 - x)(4x^2 - 4x - 15) = x^2(4x^2 - 4x - 15) - x(4x^2 - 4x - 15)\)
   \(= 4x^4 - 4x^3 - 15x^2 - 4x^3 + 4x^2 + 15x = 4x^4 - 8x^3 - 11x^2 + 15x = 0\).
Final Answer: \(4x^4 - 8x^3 - 11x^2 + 15x = 0\).
Key Move: Clearing denominators early into \((2x+3)(2x-5)\) prevents fractional arithmetic errors in the 4th-degree expansion.
Watch Out: Forgetting the root \(0\) which supplies the factor \(x\), reducing a quartic to a cubic.

C9-A · Form Quartic with Fractional & Negative Roots

Form an equation with integer coefficients whose roots are \(1/2, -1, 2\), and \(3\).

How Do I Start?
The fractional root \(1/2\) corresponds to the factor \((2x - 1)\). The integer roots give \((x + 1), (x - 2), (x - 3)\). Pair \((x + 1)(x - 2)\) and \((2x - 1)(x - 3)\).
👁️ Reveal Worked Solution & Crux Move
Full Algebraic Derivation:
1. Form factors: \(P(x) = (2x - 1)(x + 1)(x - 2)(x - 3) = 0\).
2. Pair \((x + 1)(x - 2) = x^2 - x - 2\).
3. Pair \((2x - 1)(x - 3) = 2x^2 - 7x + 3\).
4. Multiply the two quadratic factors:
   \((x^2 - x - 2)(2x^2 - 7x + 3) = x^2(2x^2 - 7x + 3) - x(2x^2 - 7x + 3) - 2(2x^2 - 7x + 3)\)
   \(= 2x^4 - 7x^3 + 3x^2 - 2x^3 + 7x^2 - 3x - 4x^2 + 14x - 6\)
   \(= 2x^4 - 9x^3 + 6x^2 + 11x - 6 = 0\).
Final Answer: \(2x^4 - 9x^3 + 6x^2 + 11x - 6 = 0\).
Key Move: Vieta check: \(\sum \alpha = 1/2 - 1 + 2 + 3 = 9/2 = -(-9)/2\). Product \(= (1/2)(-1)(2)(3) = -3 = -6/2\). Matches perfectly.

C9-B · Monic Reciprocal Quartic from Sum Pairs

A monic quartic has roots \(r, 1/r, s, 1/s\). You are told \(r + 1/r = 3\) and \(s + 1/s = 4\). Find the expanded monic quartic equation.

Olympiad Insight
Do NOT attempt to solve for \(r\) and \(s\) via the quadratic formula! The roots \(r\) and \(1/r\) are the roots of the quadratic \(t^2 - (r + 1/r)t + r(1/r) = t^2 - 3t + 1 = 0\). The entire quartic is simply the product of two reciprocal quadratics!
👁️ Reveal Worked Solution & Crux Move
Full Algebraic Derivation:
1. Quadratic factor for roots \(r, 1/r\):
   \((x - r)(x - 1/r) = x^2 - (r + 1/r)x + 1 = x^2 - 3x + 1\).
2. Quadratic factor for roots \(s, 1/s\):
   \((x - s)(x - 1/s) = x^2 - (s + 1/s)x + 1 = x^2 - 4x + 1\).
3. Multiply the two quadratic factors:
   \(P(x) = (x^2 - 3x + 1)(x^2 - 4x + 1)\)
   \(= x^2(x^2 - 4x + 1) - 3x(x^2 - 4x + 1) + 1(x^2 - 4x + 1)\)
   \(= x^4 - 4x^3 + x^2 - 3x^3 + 12x^2 - 3x + x^2 - 4x + 1\)
   \(= x^4 - 7x^3 + 14x^2 - 7x + 1 = 0\).
Final Answer: \(x^4 - 7x^3 + 14x^2 - 7x + 1 = 0\).
Key Move: Palindromic coefficient symmetry: Notice \(1, -7, 14, -7, 1\) is symmetric because all roots come in reciprocal pairs!

Q10 · Read Repeated Roots from Coefficients

\(1, 1, \alpha\) are roots of \(x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x - 4 = 0\). Find \(\alpha\).

Clue
By Vieta's formulas, the sum of all three roots equals \(-\frac{b}{a} = -(-6)/1 = 6\).
👁️ Reveal Worked Solution & Crux Move
1. Sum of roots: \(1 + 1 + \alpha = 6 \implies 2 + \alpha = 6 \implies \alpha = 4\).
2. Double check using product of roots: \(1 \cdot 1 \cdot \alpha = -(-4)/1 = 4 \implies \alpha = 4\).
3. Double check pairwise sum: \(1\cdot 1 + 1\cdot 4 + 1\cdot 4 = 1 + 4 + 4 = 9\). Consistent with coefficient of \(x\).
Final Answer: \(\alpha = 4\).

C12-A · Positive Integer Roots from Product Constraint

The roots of \(x^3 - kx^2 + 11x - 6 = 0\) are known to be positive integers. Find \(k\).

Clue
The product of the roots is \(-(-6)/1 = 6\). The only positive integer triples multiplying to 6 are \(\{1, 1, 6\}\) and \(\{1, 2, 3\}\). Test pairwise sums against \(11\).
👁️ Reveal Worked Solution & Crux Move
1. Roots \(\alpha, \beta, \gamma \in \mathbb{Z}^+\) have product \(\alpha\beta\gamma = 6\).
2. Case 1: \(\{1, 1, 6\}\). Pairwise sum: \(1(1) + 1(6) + 1(6) = 13 \ne 11\). Reject.
3. Case 2: \(\{1, 2, 3\}\). Pairwise sum: \(1(2) + 2(3) + 3(1) = 2 + 6 + 3 = 11\). Exact match!
4. Therefore, the roots are \(1, 2, 3\).
5. Sum of roots: \(k = \alpha + \beta + \gamma = 1 + 2 + 3 = 6\).
Final Answer: \(k = 6\).

C12-B · One Root Equals the Sum of the Other Two

The roots \(\alpha, \beta, \gamma\) of \(x^3 - 8x^2 + mx - 8 = 0\) satisfy \(\gamma = \alpha + \beta\). Find \(m\).

Crux Move
Substitute \(\alpha + \beta = \gamma\) directly into the Vieta sum \(\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 8\). This immediately determines \(\gamma\)!
👁️ Reveal Worked Solution & Crux Move
1. Sum of roots: \((\alpha + \beta) + \gamma = 8\).
2. Since \(\alpha + \beta = \gamma\), we have \(\gamma + \gamma = 8 \implies 2\gamma = 8 \implies \gamma = 4\).
3. Since \(\gamma = 4\) is an authentic root, it must satisfy the polynomial: \(P(4) = 0\)!
4. Substitute \(x = 4\):
   \(4^3 - 8(4^2) + m(4) - 8 = 0\)
   \(64 - 128 + 4m - 8 = 0\)
   \(-72 + 4m = 0 \implies 4m = 72 \implies m = 18\).
Final Answer: \(m = 18\).

Q14 · Symmetric Sum \(\sum \alpha^2\beta^2\) Without Solving

The roots of \(x^3 - 2x^2 + 3x - 4 = 0\) are \(\alpha, \beta, \gamma\). Find \(\alpha^2\beta^2 + \beta^2\gamma^2 + \gamma^2\alpha^2\).

Identity Clue
Square the pairwise sum: \((\alpha\beta + \beta\gamma + \gamma\alpha)^2 = \alpha^2\beta^2 + \beta^2\gamma^2 + \gamma^2\alpha^2 + 2\alpha\beta\gamma(\alpha + \beta + \gamma)\).
👁️ Reveal Worked Solution & Crux Move
1. Elementary symmetric sums:
   \(e_1 = \alpha + \beta + \gamma = 2\)
   \(e_2 = \alpha\beta + \beta\gamma + \gamma\alpha = 3\)
   \(e_3 = \alpha\beta\gamma = 4\)
2. Apply algebraic identity:
   \(\sum \alpha^2\beta^2 = e_2^2 - 2e_1 e_3 = (3)^2 - 2(2)(4) = 9 - 16 = -7\).
Final Answer: \(-7\).
Common Trap: Thinking a sum of squares cannot be negative. For polynomials with complex roots, \(\alpha^2\beta^2 + \beta^2\gamma^2 + \gamma^2\alpha^2\) can indeed be negative!

N1 · Fifth Power Sum \(S_5\) via Newton Recurrence

Find the sum of the fifth powers of the roots of \(x^4 - 7x^2 + 4x - 3 = 0\).

Newton Recurrence Relation
For \(x^4 + p_1 x^3 + p_2 x^2 + p_3 x + p_4 = 0\), with \(p_1 = 0, p_2 = -7, p_3 = 4, p_4 = -3\):
\(S_1 + p_1 = 0 \implies S_1 = 0\)
\(S_2 + p_1 S_1 + 2p_2 = 0 \implies S_2 - 14 = 0 \implies S_2 = 14\)
\(S_3 + p_1 S_2 + p_2 S_1 + 3p_3 = 0 \implies S_3 + 3(4) = 0 \implies S_3 = -12\)
For \(k \ge 4\): \(S_k - 7S_{k-2} + 4S_{k-3} - 3S_{k-4} = 0\).
👁️ Reveal Worked Solution & Crux Move
1. Base sums:
   \(S_1 = 0\)
   \(S_2 = 14\)
   \(S_3 = -12\)
2. Compute \(S_4\):
   \(S_4 - 7S_2 + 4S_1 - 4p_4 = 0 \implies S_4 - 7(14) + 4(0) - 4(-3) = 0\)
   \(S_4 - 98 + 12 = 0 \implies S_4 = 86\).
3. Compute \(S_5\) via recurrence \(S_5 - 7S_3 + 4S_2 - 3S_1 = 0\):
   \(S_5 - 7(-12) + 4(14) - 3(0) = 0\)
   \(S_5 + 84 + 56 = 0 \implies S_5 + 140 = 0 \implies S_5 = -140\).
Final Answer: \(S_5 = -140\).
