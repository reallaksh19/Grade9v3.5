Q1 · What Property Rises When Temperature Rises?

Compare diffusion, evaporation, gas compression, and gas expansion when temperature is raised. Which of these properties actually increase with temperature?

See the Idea
Temperature ↑ ⇒ Kinetic energy and particle speed ↑ ⇒ Diffusion rate ↑, Evaporation rate ↑, Gas volume/expansion tendency ↑. But compression requires external applied pressure; heating alone resists compression!
👁️ Reveal Worked Solution & Answer Check
Reasoning Route:
1. As temperature increases, average kinetic energy of particles increases proportionally: \(\text{KE}_{\text{avg}} = \frac{3}{2} k_B T\).
2. Faster moving particles intermix more rapidly, increasing the rate of diffusion.
3. More surface particles possess kinetic energy exceeding the intermolecular barrier, increasing evaporation rate.
4. At constant pressure, gases expand as \(V \propto T\) (Charles's Law).
5. Compression is forced reduction of volume by external work and pressure, which becomes harder at higher temperatures.
Final Answer: Diffusion, evaporation, and expansion increase. Compression does not.

Celsius to Kelvin Absolute Scale
Q4 · Convert Celsius to Kelvin Scale

Convert the following Celsius temperatures into the absolute Kelvin scale: (a) \(25^\circ\text{C}\), (b) \(373^\circ\text{C}\), (c) \(-100^\circ\text{C}\).

Conversion Rule
\(T(\text{K}) = \theta(^\circ\text{C}) + 273.15\) (use \(+273\) for NCERT standard). Notice the Kelvin scale never carries a degree symbol!
👁️ Reveal Worked Solution & Answer Check
1. (a) \(25^\circ\text{C} + 273 = 298\text{ K}\) (Standard room temperature).
2. (b) \(373^\circ\text{C} + 273 = 646\text{ K}\).
3. (c) \(-100^\circ\text{C} + 273 = 173\text{ K}\).
Final Answers: (a) 298 K, (b) 646 K, (c) 173 K.

Compression & Liquefaction
Q7 · Dual Conditions Required to Liquefy Atmospheric Gases

Explain why both high pressure AND low temperature are simultaneously required to liquefy atmospheric gases into liquids.

Two-Factor Mechanism
High pressure forces molecules into close proximity (decreasing mean intermolecular separation \(r\)). Low temperature drains kinetic energy so attraction can lock particles into liquid phase!
👁️ Reveal Worked Solution & Answer Check
Complete Molecular Derivation:
1. Gas particles possess high kinetic energy and are separated by enormous intermolecular spaces compared to their diameter.
2. Applying high pressure compresses the gas, reducing volume and forcing particles close together where short-range intermolecular forces (van der Waals attraction) become significant.
3. However, compression alone generates thermal heat and if kinetic energy remains high, thermal agitation causes particles to rebound upon collision.
4. Lowering temperature drains thermal kinetic energy, slowing particles down so attractive forces can overcome kinetic motion and bind particles into the condensed liquid state.
Key Conclusion: High pressure brings particles close together; low temperature slows them down so attraction can hold them.
D2 · Medium ⚡ Unit 1 Q15 · Part 3 · Page 18
Evaporative Cooling Mechanism
Q14 · Why Water in an Earthen Pot (Matka) Remains Cold in Summer

Explain the thermodynamic reason why water stored in an earthen pot (matka) cools down considerably during hot summer days.

👁️ Reveal Worked Solution & Answer Check
1. An earthen pot has millions of microscopic pores through which water continuously seeps to the outer surface.
2. The water on the exterior surface evaporates into the dry, hot summer air.
3. To vaporise, the escaping water molecules absorb their latent heat of vaporisation from the earthen pot and the remaining bulk water.
4. This continuous loss of thermal energy lowers the temperature of the pot and water within, producing substantial evaporative cooling.
Final Answer: Continuous seepage through porous walls followed by surface evaporation absorbs latent heat of vaporisation from the water.

Latent Heat of Vaporisation
Q20 · Why Steam at 373 K Produces More Severe Burns Than Boiling Water at 373 K

Both boiling water and steam exist at \(100^\circ\text{C}\) (\(373\text{ K}\)). Why does exposure to steam cause far more severe burns than boiling water? Support your answer with energy quantification.

Energy Ledger
Steam at \(373\text{ K}\) contains latent heat of vaporisation (\(2.26 \times 10^6\text{ J/kg}\) or \(540\text{ cal/g}\)) in addition to the sensible heat possessed by liquid water at \(373\text{ K}\)!
👁️ Reveal Worked Solution & Energy Ledger
Thermodynamic Energy Derivation:
1. Liquid water at \(373\text{ K}\) delivers only sensible heat as it cools down to skin temperature: \(\Delta Q = m c \Delta T\).
2. Steam at \(373\text{ K}\) must first undergo a phase change from vapor to liquid at constant \(373\text{ K}\), releasing its entire latent heat of vaporisation: \(\Delta Q_L = m L_v = m \times 2.26 \times 10^6\text{ J/kg}\).
3. Subsequently, the condensed boiling water still delivers sensible cooling heat \(\Delta Q_S = m c \Delta T\).
4. Therefore, each kilogram of steam delivers \(2.26\text{ MJ}\) of extra energy directly onto the skin tissue at the moment of contact, causing much deeper, catastrophic cellular burn damage.
Key Conclusion: Steam contains \(2.26 \times 10^6\text{ J/kg}\) of extra latent heat of vaporisation absorbed during phase transition.
