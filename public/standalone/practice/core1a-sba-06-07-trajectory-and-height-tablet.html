06A

State Description vs Path Description

Core (1A) Deep-Dive

Suppose we mark a projectile every second. Then x(t) and y(t) answer a state question: “where is it now?” If the time parameter is eliminated and height is connected directly to horizontal coordinate, y(x) answers a path question: “what height belongs to each horizontal coordinate, regardless of when it gets there?”

State Description: (x(t), y(t)) → Eliminate t → Path Description: y(x)
Physical Difference
• State View: Governed by clock t. Tells velocity, momentum, and position at an instant.
• Path View: Geometric trajectory curve in space. Tells if the projectile clears a wall or hits a target.
⚠️ Common Misconception
Thinking that eliminating time means time stopped existing. Time still flows; y(x) simply graphs the spatial curve traced by the particle.
06B · 06C

Derivation of the Trajectory Equation

Derivation Bridge

Start from the shared-clock equations of ideal projectile motion launched from origin (0,0) at angle θ with initial speed u:

x(t) = (u cos θ) t ⇒ t = x / (u cos θ)

Substitute this shared time t into the vertical equation y(t) = (u sin θ)t − ½gt²:

y = (u sin θ) [x / (u cos θ)] − ½g [x / (u cos θ)]²

Simplifying the first term (sin θ / cos θ = tan θ) and squaring the denominator of the second term gives the master trajectory equation:

y(x) = x tan θ − [g / (2u² cos²θ)] x²
Alternative Sec² Form
Since 1/cos²θ = 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ:
y = x tan θ − [g x² / (2u²)] (1 + tan²θ)
Enables solving for launch angle θ when (x, y) coordinates of a target are given (quadratic in tan θ).
06D · 06E

Slope + Curvature & Vector Notation

Algebraic Anatomy

The trajectory equation y = Ax − Bx² has two distinct physical terms:

Anatomy of y = Ax − Bx²
• Linear Term (Ax = x tan θ): The initial launch slope. Without gravity (g=0), the projectile would continue along this straight tangent line forever.
• Quadratic Term (−Bx² = −[g/(2u²cos²θ)]x²): The downward bending caused by gravity. It curves the path downward into a parabola.

Vector Notation Shortcut: If initial velocity is given in vector form u = aî + bĵ m/s, then u_x = a and u_y = b. Then tan θ = b/a and u cos θ = a. The equation simplifies instantly without computing θ:

y(x) = (b / a) x − [g / (2a²)] x²
⚡ Fast Shortcut
Never waste time calculating inverse tangents when given u = 6î + 8ĵ. Directly read u_x = 6 and u_y = 8!
06F · 06G

Wall Events & The Factored Range Form

Core Applications

Two Routes to a Wall Coordinate: If a vertical wall is situated at distance x = d:
1. Two-Step Clock Route: Find t = d / u_x, then substitute t into y(t) = u_y t − ½gt².
2. Direct Trajectory Route: Directly substitute x = d into y(x) = d tan θ − [gd²/(2u²cos²θ)]. Both routes yield identical results. Route 1 is usually simpler for arithmetic; Route 2 is required for symbolic proofs.

Factored Range Form (Same-Height Flights)
Recall that range R = (2u² sin θ cos θ) / g. Factoring out x tan θ from the trajectory equation gives:
y(x) = x tan θ (1 − x / R)
This elegant form exposes both x-intercepts: y=0 at x=0 (launch) and at x=R (landing).
⚠️ Range Form Constraint
The form y = x tan θ (1 − x/R) is valid ONLY when landing height matches launch height. Do NOT use it for cliff/tower launches!
WORKED EXAMPLE & FADED PRACTICE

Q38 & Q47 Question Families

Practice Workspaces
Worked Example · Q38 Family (Wall Height)
Problem: A projectile is launched at 20 m/s at 45° above the horizontal (g = 10 m/s²). A vertical wall is 10 m from launch. Find height y at the wall.
Solution:
• u_x = 20 cos 45° = 20/√2 = 10√2 m/s.
• Time to wall: t = x / u_x = 10 / (10√2) = 1/√2 s.
• Height: y = u_y t − ½gt² = (10√2)(1/√2) − 5(1/√2)² = 10 − 5(0.5) = 10 − 2.5 = 7.5 m.
Faded Practice · Q47 Family (Vector Trajectory)
Task: Initial velocity is u = 6î + 8ĵ m/s with g = 10 m/s². Derive y(x) and find height y at x = 6 m.
Reveal Model Solution
u_x = 6 m/s, u_y = 8 m/s. tan θ = 8/6 = 4/3. Trajectory equation: y(x) = (8/6)x − [10 / (2 × 6²)] x² = (4/3)x − (5/36)x². At x = 6 m: y(6) = (4/3)(6) − (5/36)(36) = 8 − 5 = 3 meters. Final answer: y(x) = (4/3)x − (5/36)x²; y(6) = 3 m.

07A · 07B

Horizontal Launch Model & The Vertical Clock

Foundation Model

At the instant of a pure horizontal launch, the velocity points entirely sideways. That means the initial vertical component is zero (u_y = 0), but only at launch! Because gravity accelerates downward at a_y = −g, vertical velocity builds up immediately: v_y(t) = −gt.

Vertical Fall Owns the Flight Time
For height h, the vertical drop equation is h = ½gt². The flight time is completely independent of horizontal speed u:
t_fall = √(2h / g)
Horizontal travel then follows simply from steady x-motion:
x_range = u · t_fall = u · √(2h / g)
⚠️ The Bullet-Drop Paradox
A bullet fired horizontally at 1000 m/s and a bullet dropped from rest from the same height strike flat ground at the EXACT same instant. Horizontal speed cannot delay vertical gravity fall!
07C · 07D

Impact State & Displacement Vector

Impact Kinematics

At ground impact, the projectile retains its full original horizontal velocity v_x = u, while having acquired downward speed v_y = gt = √(2gh).

v_impact = √(v_x² + v_y²) = √(u² + 2gh) | tan β = v_y / v_x = √(2gh) / u
Displacement Triangle vs Velocity Triangle
• Displacement Vector: r = xî − hĵ with magnitude r = √(x² + h²). Angle of displacement: tan α = h / x = (½gt²) / (ut) = gt / (2u).
• Notice: tan β = gt / u = 2 tan α. The impact velocity vector is twice as steep as the line-of-sight displacement vector!
Key Ratio Proof
tan β_velocity = 2 · tan α_displacement
A classic exam problem: at ground impact, the tangent of velocity angle is always double the tangent of displacement angle.
07E · 07F

General Unequal-Height & Signed Coordinates

Tower & Cliff Launch

When launched from a tower of height h with an angle (either upward or downward), the same-height flight time formula 2u sin θ/g does NOT apply. You must set up the signed vertical kinematic equation with +y upward:

y(t) = y_0 + u_y t − ½ g t² ⇒ −h = u_y t − ½ g t²
Sign Rules
• Upward launch from tower: u_y = +u sin θ; landing displacement Δy = −h.
• Horizontal launch from tower: u_y = 0; landing displacement Δy = −h.
• Downward launch from tower: u_y = −u sin θ; landing displacement Δy = −h.
⚠️ Sign Trap
Forgetting that landing below launch level means Δy = −h. Writing +h in the quadratic leads to an imaginary or physically false flight time.
WORKED EXAMPLES & FADED PRACTICE

Source Illustration 5 & Tower Practice

Practice Workspaces
Watch One · Source Illustration 5
Problem: Launch horizontally at u = 98 m/s from height h = 490 m (g = 9.8 m/s²).
Solution:
• Fall time: t = √(2h / g) = √(2 × 490 / 9.8) = √100 = 10 seconds.
• Range: x = u · t = 98 × 10 = 980 meters.
• Impact speed: v_y = gt = 9.8 × 10 = 98 m/s; v = √(98² + 98²) = 98√2 ≈ 138.6 m/s at 45° below horizontal.
Faded Practice · General Tower Launch
Task: A ball is thrown from a 20 m platform with u_x = 12 m/s and u_y = +5 m/s (g = 10 m/s²). Find the flight time and landing distance.
Reveal Model Solution
Signed vertical equation: −20 = 5t − ½(10)t² ⇒ −20 = 5t − 5t² Divide by 5: −4 = t − t² ⇒ t² − t − 4 = 0 Quadratic formula: t = [1 ± √(1 − 4(1)(−4))] / 2 = [1 + √17] / 2 ≈ [1 + 4.123] / 2 ≈ 2.56 seconds. Horizontal range: x = u_x · t = 12 × 2.56 = 30.74 meters.
