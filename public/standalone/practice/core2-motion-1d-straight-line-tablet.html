Concept Unit 01

MOTION 1D 01 - Distance and displacement

Path length and endpoint change answer different questions

M1D-01 · Core 1

What is happening?

Distance counts the total path travelled. Displacement compares only the final and initial positions, with sign set by the chosen axis.

Key Idea

Reversal is the decisive clue. Once the object turns around, distance and |displacement| are generally different.

How It Works

  1. Recognise: mark start, turn(s), and finish on one signed number line.
  2. Represent: keep each leg signed for displacement; use each leg length for distance.
  3. Build: Δx=xf-xi; distance=Σ|leg displacement|.
  4. Check: |Δx| can never exceed distance.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example. A robot moves +7 m, then -11 m, then +2 m. Δx=7-11+2=-2 m. Distance=7+11+2=20 m.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not turn every signed leg into a positive number before finding displacement.

✅ Quick Check

A runner returns to the start after travelling 18 m. What are distance and displacement?

Reveal Answer
Distance = 18 m; displacement = 0.

Concept Unit 02

MOTION 1D 02 - Average speed and average velocity

Same elapsed time, different numerator

M1D-02 · Core 1

What is happening?

Average speed uses total distance. Average velocity uses displacement. A round trip can have positive average speed and zero average velocity.

Key Idea

The denominator is the same: total elapsed time. The numerator decides which average you are calculating.

How It Works

  1. Trip table: list distance/displacement and time for each leg.
  2. Average speed: total distance / total time.
  3. Average velocity: total displacement / total time.
  4. Check: returning to the start forces average velocity to zero.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example. 60 km out at 40 km/h, 60 km back at 30 km/h. Total time=1.5+2=3.5 h. Average speed=120/3.5=34.3 km/h. Average velocity=0.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not average 40 and 30 directly unless the time spent at the two speeds is equal.

✅ Quick Check

A runner completes a 400 m lap in 80 s and returns to the start. Average speed and velocity?

Reveal Answer
5 m/s; 0 m/s.

Concept Unit 03

MOTION 1D 03 - Equal-time and equal-distance weighting

What is equal determines the weighting

M1D-03 · Core 1

What is happening?

If two speeds last for equal times, the arithmetic mean works. If they cover equal distances, the slower leg lasts longer and gets more time weight.

Key Idea

Average speed is always total distance / total time. The shortcut changes because the structure of the trip changes.

How It Works

  1. Equal time: let each interval last T; time cancels cleanly.
  2. Equal distance: let each leg be L; write times L/v.
  3. Compare: equal-distance average must lie closer to the slower speed.
  4. Reconstruct: derive the result from one common T or L instead of memorising formulas.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example. Equal distances at 20 and 30 m/s. vavg=2L/(L/20+L/30)=24 m/s.

⚠️ Watch Out

“Average of the speeds” is not a definition. It is only a special-case consequence.

✅ Quick Check

Equal distances are covered at 12 m/s and 18 m/s. Is the average above or below 15 m/s?

Reveal Answer
Below 15 m/s; it is 14.4 m/s.

Concept Unit 04

MOTION 1D 04 - Velocity sign, acceleration sign and reversal

Negative does not mean slowing down

M1D-04 · Core 1

What is happening?

Velocity sign tells direction. Acceleration sign tells how velocity changes. Speed increases when velocity and acceleration have the same sign and decreases when their signs oppose.

Key Idea

A turning point is a state, not a force switch: velocity may be zero while acceleration remains nonzero.

How It Works

  1. Choose + direction: signs have meaning only after this choice.
  2. Read v: sign gives motion direction.
  3. Read a: compare its sign with v to predict speed change.
  4. Turning: when v passes through zero, the direction changes; a need not vanish.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example. u=-4 m/s, a=-3 m/s² for 2 s. v=u+at=-4-6=-10 m/s. Speed grows from 4 to 10 m/s because v and a have the same sign.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not translate “negative acceleration” into “slowing down” without checking the sign of velocity.

✅ Quick Check

At one instant v=-6 m/s and a=+2 m/s². Is the object speeding up or slowing down?

Reveal Answer
Slowing down.

Concept Unit 05

MOTION 1D 05 - Build the state before choosing an equation

UVATS is a model check, not a formula list

M1D-05 · Core 1

What is happening?

For one constant-acceleration phase, organise the state as u, v, a, t, s. Then choose the relation that connects the knowns to the target while leaving an unnecessary variable absent.

Key Idea

Equation selection comes after model validity. These relations are legal only when acceleration is constant over the phase.

How It Works

  1. State: fill u, v, a, t, s with signs and units.
  2. Target: circle the requested quantity.
  3. Eliminate: identify a variable that is neither known nor needed.
  4. Select: choose the constant-a relation that omits it.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example. u=5 m/s, a=2 m/s², s=24 m, find v. Time is not needed. v²=u²+2as=25+96=121, so v=11 m/s.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not force every problem through t. Unnecessary unknowns create unnecessary algebra.

✅ Quick Check

Which relation avoids time when u, v, a and s are involved?

Reveal Answer
v² = u² + 2as.

Concept Unit 06

MOTION 1D 06 - Rebuild the constant-acceleration equations

Two physical ideas generate the equation family

M1D-06 · Core 1

What is happening?

The familiar equations are compressed consequences of constant acceleration and the area under a velocity-time graph. Rebuilding them makes equation choice easier to justify.

Key Idea

Mechanism first: acceleration changes velocity linearly; displacement accumulates the changing velocity.

How It Works

  1. From acceleration: a=(v-u)/t → v=u+at.
  2. From v-t area: rectangle ut + triangle ½(at)t → s=ut+½at².
  3. Average velocity: linear v(t) gives s=½(u+v)t.
  4. Eliminate t: combine the previous relations to obtain v²=u²+2as.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked reconstruction. Substitute t=(v-u)/a into s=½(u+v)t: 2as=(u+v)(v-u)=v²-u². Therefore v²=u²+2as.

⚠️ Watch Out

The squared velocities are not arbitrary symbols to memorise; they appear when time is eliminated.

✅ Quick Check

Why does s contain a t² term when acceleration is constant?

Reveal Answer
Because the extra velocity grows linearly with t, and displacement accumulates that growing velocity over time.

Concept Unit 07

MOTION 1D 07 - Multiphase motion and delayed starts

Carry physical state across boundaries; reset only the local clock

M1D-07 · Core 1

What is happening?

When acceleration changes, split the story into phases. Position and velocity at a boundary are physical state and must carry forward. A new local elapsed time may start there.

Key Idea

Never use one acceleration across a boundary where the motion law changes.

How It Works

  1. Mark events: acceleration change, stop, reversal, delayed start.
  2. Carry: vend,k=ustart,k+1 and position is continuous.
  3. Reset: local time may become 0 at a new phase without resetting position.
  4. Two bodies: define one common clock before comparing positions.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example. 0→8 m/s in 4 s, cruise 5 s, brake to rest in 2 s. s=½(0+8)(4)+8(5)+½(8+0)(2)=64 m.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not reset position when you reset a local phase clock.

✅ Quick Check

At the boundary between two phases, which quantities must remain physically continuous?

Reveal Answer
Position and velocity (unless the problem explicitly includes an impulsive event).

Concept Unit 08

MOTION 1D 08 - Vertical motion and free fall

The top is a velocity state, not a gravity-off state

M1D-08 · Core 1

What is happening?

With upward chosen positive, ideal vertical motion near Earth uses a=-g throughout. At the highest point the vertical velocity is zero for an instant, but acceleration is still -g.

Key Idea

Same model, different initial states: drop u=0; throw upward u>0; project downward u<0.

How It Works

  1. Choose axis: if up is +, gravity is negative.
  2. Top event: v=0, a=-g.
  3. Same-height symmetry: ideal return speed has the same magnitude as launch speed.
  4. Validity: constant g, negligible air resistance.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example. u=18 m/s upward, g=9 m/s². At top: 0=18-9t → t=2 s. Then H=18(2)-½(9)(2²)=18 m.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not set a=0 at the highest point. Zero velocity does not imply zero acceleration.

✅ Quick Check

At the highest point of an upward throw, state v and a (up positive).

Reveal Answer
v=0; a=-g.

Concept Unit 09

MOTION 1D 09 - Position-time graphs

Slope is velocity; height is position

M1D-09 · Core 1

What is happening?

An x-t graph is a record of position versus time. Its slope gives velocity. The vertical height of the graph is position, not speed.

Key Idea

Read the axes before reading the shape. A flat x-t line means the object is at rest even if the line sits high on the page.

How It Works

  1. Positive slope: positive velocity.
  2. Zero slope: v=0.
  3. Negative slope: negative velocity.
  4. Steepness: larger |slope| means larger speed.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example. x rises from 2 m to 14 m in 4 s, then stays flat for 3 s. First velocity=(14-2)/4=3 m/s. Flat interval velocity=0.

⚠️ Watch Out

A high point on x-t does not mean high speed. Speed comes from slope.

✅ Quick Check

What does a horizontal segment on an x-t graph mean?

Reveal Answer
The object is at rest: v=0.

Concept Unit 10

MOTION 1D 10 - Velocity-time and acceleration-time graphs

Slope, height and area have different jobs

M1D-10 · Core 1

What is happening?

On v-t, height is velocity, slope is acceleration, and signed area is displacement. On a-t, height is acceleration and area is change in velocity.

Key Idea

Signed area and absolute area answer different questions. Negative v-t area subtracts from displacement but still adds to distance.

How It Works

  1. v-t slope: acceleration.
  2. v-t signed area: displacement.
  3. v-t absolute area: distance.
  4. a-t area: Δv.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example. v=+4 m/s for 3 s, then v=-2 m/s for 4 s. Displacement=12-8=+4 m. Distance=12+8=20 m.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not use graph slope when the question asks for displacement from a v-t graph.

✅ Quick Check

What does signed area under a v-t graph give?

Reveal Answer
Displacement.

Concept Unit 11

MOTION 1D 11 - Interval motion and graph consistency

Cumulative laws must be converted carefully into interval questions

M1D-11 · Core 1

What is happening?

A displacement law S(t) gives position change from the chosen start to time t. Distance during a later interval comes from endpoint subtraction. Graph validity also depends on what each axis means.

Key Idea

Axis literacy prevents false pattern matching. For constant acceleration, v²-x is linear; v-x is generally not.

How It Works

  1. Interval: Δs from t1 to t2 = S(t2)-S(t1).
  2. Nth second: compare cumulative states at n and n-1.
  3. Graph test: time histories must assign one state to each time.
  4. Representation: inspect both axes before interpreting slope/linearity.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Worked example. From rest, S(4)=32 m and S∝t², so S(t)=2t². 5th-second distance=S(5)-S(4)=50-32=18 m.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not confuse “distance by 5 s” with “distance during the 5th second”.

✅ Quick Check

A closed loop on an x-t graph fails what exact consistency test?

Reveal Answer
The vertical-line test: one time cannot map to two positions for one particle in one frame.
