🔬 The Kinetic Particle Model of Matter

All matter consists of minute particles (atoms or molecules) in ceaseless, rapid random motion. The macroscopic properties of gases—pressure, temperature, diffusion, compressibility, and fluid flow—arise directly from the micro-dynamics of these particles.

Key Postulates of Kinetic Molecular Theory (KMT):
  • Gas molecules are point masses whose actual volume is negligible compared to the total volume occupied ($V_{ ext{particles}} \ll V_{ ext{gas}}$).
  • There are no intermolecular forces of attraction or repulsion between ideal gas molecules.
  • Molecules undergo continuous, random straight-line motion until colliding elastically with one another or the container walls.
  • Pressure exerted by a gas is the net momentum transferred per second per unit area of container wall: $P = \frac{1}{3}\frac{m N \overline{v^2}}{V}$.
  • Average kinetic energy of particles is directly proportional to absolute thermodynamic temperature $T$: $\overline{KE} = \frac{3}{2} k_B T = \frac{3}{2} \frac{R}{N_A} T$.

States of Matter Microscopic Comparison

Property Solid Liquid Gas
Particle Spacing Extremely tight, ordered lattice Close, loosely clustered Very large (~10× molecular diameter)
Intermolecular Forces Extremely strong (fixed positions) Intermediate (can slip past) Negligibly weak (free trajectories)
Kinetic Energy Vibrational only around fixed sites Vibrational + Rotational + Local Translational High Translational Kinetic Energy
Compressibility Negligible ($dV/dP \approx 0$) Very low Extremely high (large empty space)
Fluidity & Shape Rigid, definite shape & volume Fluid, adopts vessel shape, definite volume Fluid, expands to fill entire container volume

Molecular Speed Distributions ($v_{ ext{mp}}, v_{ ext{avg}}, v_{ ext{rms}}$)

At any given temperature $T$, molecules exhibit a Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution. Three characteristic speeds describe this population:

Most Probable Speed:   v_mp = √(2 R T / M) ≈ 1.414 √(RT/M)
Average Speed:        v_avg = √(8 R T / π M) ≈ 1.596 √(RT/M)
Root Mean Square:     v_rms = √(3 R T / M) ≈ 1.732 √(RT/M)
Ratio: v_mp : v_avg : v_rms = 1 : 1.128 : 1.225

⚖️ Fundamental Gas Laws & The Ideal Gas Equation

Four macroscopic variables completely specify the physical state of a fixed sample of gas: Pressure ($P$), Volume ($V$), Temperature ($T$), and Amount of substance ($n$).

1. Boyle's Law (Isothermal: $T = ext{const}, n = ext{const}$)

At constant temperature, the volume of a given mass of gas is inversely proportional to its pressure.

P ∝ 1/V  ⇒  P · V = ext{constant}  ⇒  P₁ V₁ = P₂ V₂

2. Charles's Law (Isobaric: $P = ext{const}, n = ext{const}$)

At constant pressure, the volume of a given mass of gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature in Kelvin.

V ∝ T  ⇒  V / T = ext{constant}  ⇒  V₁ / T₁ = V₂ / T₂

3. Gay-Lussac's Law (Isochoric: $V = ext{const}, n = ext{const}$)

At constant volume, the pressure of a given mass of gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature.

P ∝ T  ⇒  P / T = ext{constant}  ⇒  P₁ / T₁ = P₂ / T₂

4. Avogadro's Hypothesis ($P, T = ext{const}$)

Equal volumes of all gases under identical conditions of temperature and pressure contain an equal number of molecules ($V \propto n$).

5. The Ideal Gas Equation

Combining all four laws produces the universal equation of state for an ideal gas:

P · V = n · R · T = (m / M) · R · T
Density Form:   P · M = d · R · T  ⇒  d = (P · M) / (R · T)
Universal Gas Constant ($R$):
  • $R = 8.314 ext{ J}\cdot ext{K}^{-1}\cdot ext{mol}^{-1} = 8.314 ext{ Pa}\cdot ext{m}^3\cdot ext{K}^{-1}\cdot ext{mol}^{-1}$
  • $R = 0.0821 ext{ L}\cdot ext{atm}\cdot ext{K}^{-1}\cdot ext{mol}^{-1}$
  • $R = 0.08314 ext{ L}\cdot ext{bar}\cdot ext{K}^{-1}\cdot ext{mol}^{-1}$
  • $R \approx 2 ext{ cal}\cdot ext{K}^{-1}\cdot ext{mol}^{-1}$

6. Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures

In a non-reacting mixture of gases, the total pressure is the sum of partial pressures exerted by individual gases:

P_total = P₁ + P₂ + P₃ + ... + Pₙ = ∑ Pᵢ
Partial Pressure:   Pᵢ = xᵢ · P_total   (where xᵢ = nᵢ / n_total is the mole fraction)

7. Graham's Law of Diffusion / Effusion

Under identical temperature and pressure, the rate of effusion or diffusion ($r$) of a gas is inversely proportional to the square root of its molar mass or density:

r₁ / r₂ = √(M₂ / M₁) = √(d₂ / d₁) = (V₁ / t₁) / (V₂ / t₂)

🌊 Evaporation, Boiling, and Latent Heat

Phase transitions involve reorganizing molecular spacing and breaking intermolecular attractions without altering the chemical identity of the substance.

Evaporation vs. Boiling Critical Distinction:
Evaporation
  • Surface phenomenon occurring at ANY temperature below boiling.
  • Molecules with kinetic energy exceeding surface attraction escape.
  • Causes cooling of remaining liquid ($\overline{KE}$ drops).
  • Silent, gradual, non-turbulent.
Boiling
  • Bulk phenomenon occurring at a FIXED temperature (boiling point).
  • Vapour pressure of liquid equals external atmospheric pressure ($P_{ ext{vap}} = P_{ ext{ext}}$).
  • Bubbles form inside the entire liquid volume.
  • Temperature remains strictly constant during boiling.

Four Factors Controlling Rate of Evaporation

Factor Direction Microscopic Mechanism
1. Exposed Surface Area Area ↑  ⇒  Rate ↑ More surface molecules are simultaneously exposed to escape the liquid barrier into the air.
2. Temperature Temp ↑  ⇒  Rate ↑ A higher fraction of molecules possess kinetic energy exceeding the activation energy for escape.
3. Humidity (Atmospheric Moisture) Humidity ↑  ⇒  Rate ↓ Air is already saturated with water vapour; the rate of condensation back into liquid approaches the rate of evaporation.
4. Wind Speed Wind ↑  ⇒  Rate ↑ Moving air sweeps away water vapour molecules adjacent to the surface, preventing localized saturation.

Latent Heat & Heating Curve Analysis

During a phase change, thermal energy supplied goes entirely into breaking intermolecular binding forces rather than raising the average kinetic energy of the particles. Hence, temperature remains strictly constant.

Latent Heat of Fusion (L_f for Ice at 0°C):        3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg (≈ 80 cal/g)
Latent Heat of Vaporization (L_v for Water at 100°C): 2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg (≈ 540 cal/g)
Why Steam at 100°C Burns Far More Severely Than Boiling Water at 100°C:

Both steam and boiling water are at 100°C ($373 ext{ K}$). However, 1 kg of steam contains an extra $2.26 imes 10^6 ext{ J}$ of stored latent heat of vaporization. Upon contacting skin, steam first condenses into water at 100°C, releasing this colossal latent heat directly onto tissues, before cooling further as liquid water!

⚡ Real Gases, Deviations, & The Van der Waals Equation

Real gases deviate from ideal behavior because gas molecules possess finite molecular volume and exert intermolecular attractive forces (Van der Waals forces).

Van der Waals Equation (for n moles):
  (P + a n² / V²) · (V - n b) = n R T

For 1 mole (molar volume V_m):
  (P + a / V_m²) · (V_m - b) = R T

Significance of Van der Waals Constants '$a$' and '$b$'

Constant Physical Meaning Units Effect on Liquefaction
a (Pressure correction) Magnitude of intermolecular attractive forces between gas molecules. $ ext{atm}\cdot ext{L}^2\cdot ext{mol}^{-2}$  or  $ ext{Pa}\cdot ext{m}^6\cdot ext{mol}^{-2}$ Higher '$a$' indicates stronger intermolecular attraction $ ightarrow$ gas liquefies much more easily ($ ext{NH}_3 > ext{CO}_2 > ext{O}_2 > ext{H}_2$).
b (Volume correction / Co-volume) Effective incompressible volume excluded by molecules: $b = 4 imes N_A imes v_{ ext{molecule}}$. $ ext{L}\cdot ext{mol}^{-1}$  or  $ ext{m}^3\cdot ext{mol}^{-1}$ Higher '$b$' indicates larger molecular size.

Compressibility Factor ($Z$)

Z = (P · V_m) / (R · T) = V_real / V_ideal

• Ideal Gas: Z = 1 at all T and P.
• Low Pressure (P → 0): Attractive forces dominate ⇒ Z = 1 - a / (R T V_m) < 1.
• High Pressure: Repulsive / excluded volume forces dominate ⇒ Z = 1 + P b / (R T) > 1.
• Hydrogen & Helium (H₂, He): Negligible attraction ($a \approx 0$) ⇒ Z = 1 + P b / (RT) > 1 at all ordinary pressures!

Critical State & Liquefaction Constants

Critical Temperature:   T_c = 8 a / (27 R b)   (Highest temp at which gas can be liquefied by pressure alone)
Critical Pressure:     P_c = a / (27 b²)
Critical Volume:       V_c = 3 b
Critical Compressibility: Z_c = (P_c V_c) / (R T_c) = 3 / 8 = 0.375

Diffusion Rate and Temperature

Why does the aroma of hot sizzling food reach several metres away in seconds, while that of cold food requires getting close?

👁️ Reveal Conceptual Derivation
Step 1: Particles of food aroma disperse into atmospheric air via diffusion.
Step 2: Rate of diffusion depends on molecular speed: r ∝ v_rms = √(3RT/M).
Step 3: At high temperature (hot food), kinetic energy is elevated, increasing root-mean-square velocity and vapor pressure.
Step 4: Hot aromatic vapor molecules diffuse into air exponentially faster than cold food volatiles.
Ideal Gas Calculations Diagnostic 03
Molar Mass from Density (Dumas Method)

An unknown gas has a density of 1.96 g/L at 273.15 K and 1.00 atm pressure. Calculate its molar mass and identify the probable gas.

👁️ Reveal Mathematical Solution
Formula: P · M = d · R · T  ⇒  M = (d · R · T) / P
Substitution: M = (1.96 g/L × 0.0821 L·atm·K⁻¹·mol⁻¹ × 273.15 K) / 1.00 atm
Calculation: M = 1.96 × 22.414 = 43.93 ≈ 44.0 g/mol.
Identity: Carbon Dioxide (CO₂, M = 12 + 32 = 44 g/mol) or Propane (C₃H₈, M = 36 + 8 = 44 g/mol).
