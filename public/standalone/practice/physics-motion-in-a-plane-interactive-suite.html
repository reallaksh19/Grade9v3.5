CORE1 · ORIENTATION

Governing Idea & Orthogonal Independence

A projectile launched from origin $O(0,0)$ with velocity $u$ at angle $\theta$ moves simultaneously along two mutually perpendicular, independent axes. Because gravity acts purely downward, horizontal acceleration is zero while vertical acceleration is constant:

$$u_x = u \cos\theta, \quad a_x = 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad u_y = u \sin\theta, \quad a_y = -g$$

CORE1A · DETAILED TEACHING

Scanned Illustration 1 Walkthrough

Problem Stem (scanned p. 90): Assume that a ball is thrown from a field at a speed of 12.0 m/s and at an angle of 45° with the horizontal. At what distance will it hit the field again? Take g = 10.0 m/s².

Step 1: Identify Formula for Range: $R = \frac{u^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g}$
Step 2: Substitute Angle Factor: For $\theta = 45^\circ$, $2\theta = 90^\circ \implies \sin(90^\circ) = 1.0$.
Step 3: Execute Numerical Solution: $R = \frac{(12.0\text{ m/s})^2 \times 1.0}{10.0\text{ m/s}^2} = \frac{144}{10} = 14.4\text{ m}$.
Common Misconception Alert: At the apex of flight, the total velocity is not zero! Only the vertical component $v_y = 0$, while the horizontal velocity $v_x = u\cos\theta$ remains fully active and constant.
CORE1 · ORIENTATION

Curved Road Banking Dynamics (Figure 4.57)

On a curved road banked at angle $\theta$, the normal force $\vec{N}$ is tilted inward, contributing to the required centripetal acceleration. Depending on the car's speed $v$, friction $f$ adapts direction:

$$\text{Horizontal: } N\sin\theta \mp f\cos\theta = \frac{mv^2}{r}, \qquad \text{Vertical: } N\cos\theta \pm f\sin\theta = mg$$

CORE1B · RECONSTRUCTION

Active Recall Task: The Independent Velocity Principle

Explain why a bullet fired horizontally from a rifle and a bullet dropped vertically from the exact same height hit level ground at the identical instant (assuming negligible air drag).

$$y(t) = h - \frac{1}{2}gt^2 \implies t_{\text{ground}} = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}}$$

Model Answer: Vertical motion is dictated exclusively by downward gravitational acceleration $g$. Because both bullets begin with zero initial vertical velocity ($u_y = 0$), their vertical positions at every instant follow $y(t) = h - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$. The horizontal velocity $u_x$ changes only horizontal displacement ($x = u_x t$) and has no bearing on vertical fall time.

CORE2 · PRACTICE

Practice Problem with Progressive Hint Ladder

Question: A stunt motorcycle enters a flat curve of radius $50\text{ m}$. If the coefficient of static friction is $\mu_s = 0.50$, what is the maximum velocity the motorcycle can round the turn without skidding? (Take $g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$).

💡 Hint 1: Governing Force Balance

On an unbanked flat road ($\theta = 0$), the only horizontal force providing centripetal acceleration is static friction: $f_s = \frac{mv^2}{r}$.

💡 Hint 2: Maximum Static Friction Limit

The maximum static friction before slipping is $f_{\max} = \mu_s N = \mu_s mg$. Equate this to required centripetal force: $\frac{mv_{\max}^2}{r} = \mu_s mg$.

🎯 Reveal Final Worked Solution

Cancelling mass $m$ gives: $v_{\max} = \sqrt{\mu_s r g}$.

Substitute parameters: $v_{\max} = \sqrt{0.50 \times 50 \times 9.8} = \sqrt{245} \approx 15.65\text{ m/s}$ ($56.3\text{ km/h}$).
