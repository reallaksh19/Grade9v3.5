🧊 The Dimension Hierarchy (3D → 2D → 1D → 0D)

Euclid abstracted physical spatial objects by considering successive boundaries that peel away one dimension at each step:

Solid (3 Dimensions: length, breadth, depth)
  ↓ Boundaries of a solid are
Surfaces (2 Dimensions: length, breadth)
  ↓ Boundaries of a surface are
Lines / Curves (1 Dimension: length only)
  ↓ Ends/boundaries of a line are
Points (0 Dimensions: partless, no length, breadth, or depth)
Key Euclidean Definitions (Book I of 'Elements'):
  • 1. Point: A point is that which has no part (dimensionless).
  • 2. Line: A line is breadthless length.
  • 3. Straight Line: A line which lies evenly with the points on itself.
  • 4. Surface: A surface is that which has length and breadth only.
  • 5. Plane Surface: A surface which lies evenly with the straight lines on itself.

⚖️ Euclid's Seven Common Notions (Axioms)

Axioms are self-evident common notions applied throughout all mathematics (not limited solely to geometry).

1. Things which are equal to the same thing are equal to one another.
   (If A = B and C = B, then A = C)

2. If equals are added to equals, the wholes are equal.
   (If A = B, then A + C = B + C)

3. If equals are subtracted from equals, the remainders are equal.
   (If A = B, then A - C = B - C)

4. Things which coincide with one another are equal to one another.
   (Principle of superposition in geometric congruence)

5. The whole is greater than the part.
   (If A = B + C where C > 0, then A > B)

6. Things which are double of the same things are equal to one another.
   (If A = 2X and B = 2X, then A = B)

7. Things which are halves of the same things are equal to one another.
   (If A = X/2 and B = X/2, then A = B)

📐 Euclid's Five Geometric Postulates

Postulates are fundamental assumptions assumed true without proof specifically for geometric constructions.

Postulate 1: A straight line may be drawn from any one point to any other point.
• Modern Axiom: Given two distinct points, there is a unique line that passes through them.

Postulate 2: A terminated line (line segment) can be produced indefinitely.

Postulate 3: A circle can be described with any centre and any radius.

Postulate 4: All right angles are equal to one another (each equals 90°).

Postulate 5: If a straight line falling on two straight lines makes the interior angles on the same side taken together less than two right angles (< 180°), the two straight lines, if produced indefinitely, meet on that side on which the sum of angles is less than two right angles.

✨ The Fifth Postulate & Equivalent Playfair's Axiom

Euclid's Fifth Postulate is historical because attempts to prove it from the first four postulates led to the discovery of Non-Euclidean geometries (Spherical and Hyperbolic geometry).

Playfair's Axiom (Equivalent Formulation):

For every line l and for every point P not lying on l, there exists a unique line m passing through P and parallel to l.

Consequence:
Two distinct intersecting lines cannot be parallel to the same line.
If line l meets line m at angle sum = 180°, lines l and m NEVER intersect (Parallel lines).
