Thrust belongs to a chosen interface, not to an isolated force arrow

Subject: PhysicsTopic: Gravitation and contact pressureConcept: Thrust belongs to a chosen interface, not to an isolated force arrowConcept difficulty: Hard concept
1

Decide which part of a contact interaction the word thrust names and when a full resultant is required.

Hard concept
Section 1 of 6Next →

The key step

Thrust at a solid interface is the normal component of the contact interaction on the chosen body. A rough surface may simultaneously exert tangential friction; the vector sum of those components is the total contact reaction, not the thrust alone.

You need

  • Can choose one body for a free-body diagram and name the external agent producing each force.
  • A free-body diagram belongs to one chosen body (Physics)

This unit builds toward

  • INJSO|2016|Question Paper|Physics|Q32A · D4 · the hardest question in this set
    The move learners miss: For the two-box system, balance F against both friction forces; for Q alone, keep the table's normal and friction components as one contact resultant only after their components are known. Try it in Core2
  1. Circle the body and mark the one interface whose thrust or reaction is being asked for before drawing arrows.
    Why valid: Force ownership is body-specific; without the chosen body and interface, equal-and-opposite partner forces and other contacts can be mixed into one diagram.
    Result: chosen body + named interface
  2. Resolve that interface interaction into a component normal to the surface and, when the surface is rough, a tangential friction component.
    Why valid: Normal and tangential directions are perpendicular and describe distinct ways a contact can constrain or oppose relative motion.
    Result: N normal to surface; f tangent to surface
  3. The step the hard question turns onName the normal component N as the thrust magnitude for the interface, but retain both N and f when the problem asks for the total force/reaction exerted by the surface.
    Why valid: Thrust is the perpendicular component; the total contact force is a vector and can contain both perpendicular and tangential components.
    Result: thrust=N; total contact reaction R=N_vector+f_vector
  4. Place the equal-and-opposite partner contact components on the other body's diagram, not on the chosen body's diagram.
    Why valid: Newton's third-law partner forces act on different bodies, which checks both direction and force ownership.
    Result: body-specific interaction pair checked

INJSO · 2016 · Question Paper · Q32A · Official past paper, faithful restatement of the original

Two cubical boxes P (4 kg) and Q (8 kg), made of the same-density material, are in contact on a rough horizontal table. Their kinetic-friction coefficients with the table are 0.4 and 0.6 respectively. A horizontal force F pushes P so that both boxes move with constant speed. Take g=10 m/s^2. Find (i) F, (ii) the magnitude and direction of the resultant force exerted by the table on Q, and (iii) draw free-body diagrams for P and Q.

  1. Draw separate free-body diagrams for P and Q, assigning weight, normal, friction, applied/contact forces to the correct body.

    Why valid: Newton-law equations must be written for a chosen body; partner forces belong on different diagrams.

    Result: two body-specific FBDs

  2. Use vertical equilibrium to obtain N_P=40 N and N_Q=80 N, then kinetic friction f_P=16 N and f_Q=48 N.

    Why valid: There is no vertical acceleration and the stated kinetic-friction model is f_k=mu_k N.

    Result: N_P=40 N, N_Q=80 N, f_P=16 N, f_Q=48 N

  3. For the two-box system, balance F against both friction forces; for Q alone, keep the table's normal and friction components as one contact resultant only after their components are known.

    Why valid: Constant speed gives zero net horizontal force, while the table transmits two perpendicular contact-force components to Q.

    Result: F=64 N and table-force components on Q=(48 N backward, 80 N upward)

  4. Combine the perpendicular table-force components on Q and check that their horizontal component equals the friction required by the Q FBD.

    Why valid: The resultant of perpendicular normal and friction components has magnitude sqrt(N^2+f^2) and direction fixed by their ratio.

    Result: R_Q=93.3 N; angle=arctan(5/3) above horizontal toward P

Result

F=64 N. On Q the table exerts normal 80 N upward and kinetic friction 48 N opposite the motion, giving a resultant 93.3 N at tan^-1(5/3) above the horizontal toward P.

Check

Resolve the 93.3 N resultant back into components: it must recover exactly 80 N normal and 48 N friction, and the full two-box horizontal balance must return F=64 N.

Decide which part of a contact interaction the word thrust names and when a full resultant is required.

A tempting wrong path

  • The reaction of a rough table is always just the vertical normal force.
  • Any applied force component perpendicular to the surface is automatically the thrust on that surface.

Diagnose

  • If a sliding box experiences 80 N upward normal force and a nonzero horizontal friction force from the table, is the table's total force on the box exactly 80 N?
  • A block is pushed downward while its weight also acts downward. Can the downward push alone equal the table's normal thrust?

Repair

  • No. 80 N is the normal/thrust component. The total table reaction is the vector resultant of normal and friction.
  • No. Solve the whole perpendicular force balance; the surface response depends on every perpendicular force and any perpendicular acceleration.

1-2-3 quick check

  1. 1 · CheckOn a frictionless table, say which contact component remains and what the total reaction becomes.
  2. 2 · ApplyIf N=60 N and f=0, identify thrust and total reaction without calculation beyond the definitions.
  3. 3 · ConnectCarry the body/interface-first rule into the next unit, where N itself must be solved rather than assumed.

N = mg is a result with conditions, not the definition of thrust

Subject: PhysicsTopic: Gravitation and contact pressureConcept: N = mg is a result with conditions, not the definition of thrustConcept difficulty: Hard concept
1

Derive the contact thrust from the whole perpendicular dynamics instead of substituting N=mg by habit.

Hard concept
Section 2 of 6← PreviousNext →

The key step

A normal force is a constraint response found from the complete perpendicular equation Sigma F_perp = m a_perp. N=mg appears only for a horizontal contact with no other vertical force and zero vertical acceleration.

You need

  • Can write one-dimensional Newton's second law for a chosen body with a declared positive direction.
  • Net force, not any one force, sets acceleration (Physics)
  1. Choose the axis perpendicular to the contact and assign one positive direction before resolving any force.
    Why valid: Only signed components along one declared axis can be added in a Newton-law equation.
    Result: declared normal axis and sign
  2. List every external force component along that axis: normal response, weight component, and any applied push or pull component.
    Why valid: The surface response depends on the net requirement, not on one convenient force in isolation.
    Result: complete perpendicular force inventory
  3. Apply Sigma F_perp = m a_perp and solve algebraically for N before inserting the pressure relation.
    Why valid: Newton's second law determines the contact force needed to produce the actual perpendicular acceleration while contact is maintained.
    Result: N expressed from full perpendicular balance
  4. Check the contact constraint N >= 0; if the algebra demands N<0, replace the assumed-contact model by loss of contact with N=0.
    Why valid: An ordinary surface can push a body away from itself but cannot pull it through a lost contact unless another attachment is specified.
    Result: contact-valid N or contact-loss boundary

Average pressure depends on total normal thrust and actual loaded area

Subject: PhysicsTopic: Gravitation and contact pressureConcept: Average pressure depends on total normal thrust and actual loaded areaConcept difficulty: Medium concept
1

Choose the force and actual area independently before forming the pressure quotient.

Medium concept
Section 3 of 6← PreviousNext →

The key step

Average pressure is not a new force. It describes how a known total normal thrust is distributed over the actual loaded/contact area: p_avg=F_thrust/A_contact.

You need

  • Can solve the normal thrust for the contact and convert squared length units.
  • Solve the contact normal force from the complete perpendicular Newton-law equation and enforce the non-negative contact constraint.
  1. Identify the exact surface patch carrying the normal interaction and label its actual area A_contact.
    Why valid: Only the area over which the chosen force is distributed belongs in this average-pressure quotient.
    Result: actual loaded/contact area selected
  2. Use the already-solved normal thrust F_thrust as the numerator rather than replacing it automatically by weight.
    Why valid: Pressure inherits the real contact force determined by the force balance; weight equals that thrust only in the special balance already established.
    Result: pressure inputs identified independently
  3. Form p_avg=F_thrust/A_contact in SI units, converting a squared length unit by squaring the length conversion.
    Why valid: A pascal is N/m^2; area contains two length factors, so for example 1 cm^2=10^-4 m^2.
    Result: average pressure in Pa
  4. Before accepting the result, vary only area: the same thrust on a smaller area must give a larger pressure and the units must reduce to N/m^2.
    Why valid: The inverse-area trend and dimensional unit are independent checks on the quotient.
    Result: trend and unit checked

Watch one

INJSO · 2013 · Question Paper · Q58 · Official past paper, faithful restatement of the original

Books A and B are made from the same paper stock. Every linear dimension of book B is twice the corresponding dimension of book A. Each book rests with its largest face flat on a horizontal table. Select the correct statement.

  1. Represent B as a geometrically similar enlargement of A with one linear scale factor λ=2.

    Why valid: The source states that every corresponding dimension doubles.

    Result: λ=2

  2. Convert linear scaling into weight scaling: same density gives W proportional to volume, so W_B/W_A=λ^3=8.

    Why valid: For the same material, mass is density times volume and weight is proportional to mass at the same place.

    Result: W_B/W_A=8

  3. Scale the actual contact face independently: A_B/A_A=λ^2=4.

    Why valid: Area contains two independent length factors, not three.

    Result: A_B/A_A=4

  4. Form the pressure ratio only after both scalings are known: p_B/p_A=(8)/(4)=2, then compare with the choices.

    Why valid: Average pressure is normal thrust divided by actual contact area.

    Result: p_B/p_A=2; option D

Result

Option D. Book B has 8 times the weight and 4 times the contact area, so it exerts 2 times the pressure.

Check

For a general similar-object scale factor λ, weight scales as λ^3 and face area as λ^2, so pressure scales as λ; λ=2 must therefore give a factor 2.

Choose the force and actual area independently before forming the pressure quotient.

A tempting wrong path

  • Pressure is larger whenever the force is larger, regardless of area.
  • 1 cm^2 equals 10^-2 m^2 because 1 cm equals 10^-2 m.

Diagnose

  • Two orientations of the same block have the same normal thrust but different contact areas. Must their pressures be equal?
  • How many powers of the centimetre-to-metre conversion belong in an area conversion?

Repair

  • No. Hold the same thrust fixed and compare the actual contact areas; pressure is larger on the smaller area.
  • Two. Area has two length factors, so 1 cm^2=(10^-2 m)^2=10^-4 m^2.

1-2-3 quick check

  1. 1 · CheckWith the same thrust, doubling the actual contact area must halve average pressure.
  2. 2 · ApplyCompare a block on a 200 cm^2 face and a 50 cm^2 face without changing its normal thrust; identify which orientation has four times the pressure.
  3. 3 · ConnectCarry the fact that area has two length factors into the similar-object scaling unit.

Try it with less support

Explain why turning the same rectangular block from a broad face onto a narrower face can increase average pressure even when its normal thrust is unchanged.

Similar objects make weight and contact area scale with different powers

Subject: PhysicsTopic: Gravitation and contact pressureConcept: Similar objects make weight and contact area scale with different powersConcept difficulty: Hard concept
1

Assign the correct dimensional exponent before combining force and area into pressure.

Hard concept
Section 4 of 6← PreviousNext →

The key step

For similar same-density bodies scaled by a linear factor lambda, weight scales as lambda^3 while a corresponding contact face scales as lambda^2; therefore pressure on that face scales as lambda, not lambda^2 or lambda^3.

You need

  • Can use p=F/A after identifying the correct normal thrust and actual contact area.
  • Can recognise area as a product of two lengths and volume as a product of three lengths.
  • Compute or compare average pressure using the total normal thrust and the actual area over which that thrust is distributed.

This unit builds toward

  • INJSO|2013|Question Paper|Physics|Q58 · D3
    The move learners miss: Scale the actual contact face independently: A_B/A_A=λ^2=4. Try it in Core2
  1. Represent every corresponding length of the enlarged body as lambda times the original length.
    Why valid: Geometric similarity means one common linear scale factor relates all corresponding lengths.
    Result: single linear scale factor lambda
  2. Scale volume and same-density weight with three length factors: V' / V=lambda^3 and W'/W=lambda^3.
    Why valid: Volume contains three independent length dimensions, and equal density at the same g makes mass and weight proportional to volume.
    Result: weight ratio lambda^3
  3. The step the hard question turns onScale the corresponding contact face with two length factors: A'/A=lambda^2.
    Why valid: A planar face is a two-dimensional measure; the third body dimension changes volume but not that face area.
    Result: contact-area ratio lambda^2
  4. Form the ratio p'/p=(lambda^3)/(lambda^2)=lambda and test lambda=1 as a no-change limit.
    Why valid: Only after force and area scalings are independently correct may their quotient be simplified; the lambda=1 limit must recover equal pressure.
    Result: pressure ratio lambda

Pressure is scalar; the surface normal gives the force direction

Subject: PhysicsTopic: Gravitation and contact pressureConcept: Pressure is scalar; the surface normal gives the force directionConcept difficulty: Hard concept
1

Keep scalar pressure, actual area and vector force direction as separate objects when the surface is tilted.

Hard concept
Section 5 of 6← PreviousNext →

The key step

Pressure at a point is a scalar. On each small surface element it produces a force normal to that element; for uniform pressure on one flat face, those parallel elemental forces sum to a resultant of magnitude pA normal to the face, independent of the face's tilt relative to the horizontal.

You need

  • Can identify a surface normal and distinguish a scalar magnitude from a vector direction.
  • Compute or compare average pressure using the total normal thrust and the actual area over which that thrust is distributed.

This unit builds toward

  • INJSO|2013|Question Paper|Physics|Q62 · D3
    The move learners miss: Use the actual face area in F=pA; do not replace it by A cos 45 degrees. Try it in Core2
  1. Treat p as the same scalar pressure value at every point of the stated uniformly loaded face, without assigning p a vector arrow.
    Why valid: Pressure is a scalar field; direction belongs to the force a surface experiences, not to the pressure value itself.
    Result: uniform scalar p over actual face
  2. At several small patches draw each pressure-force element perpendicular to the local face.
    Why valid: Normal traction is the defining mechanical action of static pressure on a surface element.
    Result: parallel normal elemental forces
  3. The step the hard question turns onSum the parallel elemental forces over the actual area to obtain resultant magnitude F=pA in the surface-normal direction.
    Why valid: Every small contribution has magnitude p dA and the same normal direction on one plane, so their magnitudes integrate to p times actual area.
    Result: resultant F=pA normal to face
  4. Rotate the same rigid face without changing p; verify that its actual area and force magnitude stay fixed while only the normal direction rotates.
    Why valid: Rigid rotation changes orientation, not physical area, and uniform pressure magnitude was held fixed.
    Result: magnitude invariant under tilt; direction rotates

Atmospheric pressure at the Earth's surface is P_a N/m^2. A flat table has top-surface area 2 m^2 and is tilted 45 degrees to the horizontal. What is the magnitude of the force exerted by the atmosphere on the top surface?

  1. Identify P_a as uniform pressure on the actual top face, not as a downward force density.

    Why valid: Static atmospheric pressure has no preferred downward vector direction; surface-force direction comes from the local normal.

    Result: uniform scalar pressure on the face

  2. Draw the resultant force perpendicular to the tilted face.

    Why valid: Pressure force on a surface element acts normal to that element.

    Result: resultant direction = surface normal

  3. Use the actual face area in F=pA; do not replace it by A cos 45 degrees.

    Why valid: Integrating uniform normal traction p over the actual planar area gives magnitude pA.

    Result: F=P_a A

  4. Substitute A=2 m^2 and check the limiting case that rotating the same table cannot change its physical area.

    Why valid: A rigid tilt preserves actual area; only the normal direction rotates.

    Result: F=2P_a N; option A

Result

Option A: the magnitude is F=P_a A=2P_a N.

Check

Imagine slowly rotating the same rigid table while pressure remains uniform: its actual top area is unchanged, so force magnitude remains P_a A while the force direction rotates with the normal.

Keep scalar pressure, actual area and vector force direction as separate objects when the surface is tilted.

A tempting wrong path

  • A tilted surface has total pressure force p A cos(theta) because only its horizontal projection counts.

  • Does tilting a rigid tabletop change its actual physical area?

  • Pressure is scalar; the force direction is set by the surface normal.
  • No. Use p times the actual loaded area for total resultant magnitude. A cosine appears only when resolving that resultant into a chosen external direction or when a problem explicitly supplies a projected area.

Depth adds gauge pressure; gas laws need absolute pressure

Subject: PhysicsTopic: Gravitation and contact pressureConcept: Depth adds gauge pressure; gas laws need absolute pressureConcept difficulty: Hard concept
1

Choose the pressure reference correctly before coupling hydrostatics to another model.

Hard concept
Section 6 of 6← Previous

The key step

In a static liquid of uniform density, vertical depth h below a free surface adds gauge pressure rho g h to the surface pressure: P_abs=P_surface+rho g h. A gas law compares absolute pressures, so atmospheric/surface pressure cannot be silently discarded.

You need

  • Can compare pressures and use proportional reasoning.
  • For the selected competition item, accepts the supplied isothermal PV=constant relation as a stated model rather than as new Grade-9 curriculum.
  • Compute or compare average pressure using the total normal thrust and the actual area over which that thrust is distributed.

This unit builds toward

  • INJSO|2012|Question Paper|Physics|Q39 · D4
    The move learners miss: Separate bottom absolute pressure into atmospheric pressure plus the pressure contribution of the water column. Try it in Core2
  1. Mark the free-surface pressure P_surface and the deeper point's vertical depth h in one connected static liquid.
    Why valid: Hydrostatic pressure change depends on vertical depth below the reference surface, not path length or container shape.
    Result: surface reference + vertical depth h
  2. Write the liquid-column contribution as Delta P=rho g h and label it gauge pressure relative to the chosen surface.
    Why valid: For uniform density and constant g, each additional depth adds the weight per area of an additional liquid column.
    Result: P_gauge=rho g h
  3. The step the hard question turns onRecover absolute pressure by adding the surface pressure: P_abs=P_surface+rho g h before using a gas-law pressure ratio.
    Why valid: A gas's mechanical pressure is measured from zero pressure, whereas gauge pressure is measured relative to the surface reference.
    Result: P_abs at depth
  4. Take h to zero and check that P_abs returns to P_surface; for the bubble anchor, back-substitute the final depth to recover the required absolute-pressure ratio.
    Why valid: Both are independent boundary/reversal checks on the pressure reference bookkeeping.
    Result: reference and ratio checks satisfied

Watch one

INJSO · 2012 · Question Paper · Q39 · Official past paper, faithful restatement of the original

A diver releases gas bubbles from the bottom of a lake. At the surface each bubble has ten times its original volume. Assume the gas change is isothermal. A 5 m column of lake water produces a pressure equal to twice atmospheric pressure. Determine the lake depth.

  1. Use Boyle's law to translate V_surface=10V_bottom into P_bottom=10P_surface.

    Why valid: The question's isothermal gas model gives P_bottom V_bottom=P_surface V_surface.

    Result: P_bottom=10P_atm

  2. Separate bottom absolute pressure into atmospheric pressure plus the pressure contribution of the water column.

    Why valid: Hydrostatic gauge pressure adds to the pressure applied at the free surface.

    Result: P_water=9P_atm

  3. Use the supplied calibration 5 m -> 2P_atm to write P_water/P_atm=2h/5.

    Why valid: For the same liquid and g, hydrostatic pressure contribution is proportional to vertical depth.

    Result: P_water=(2h/5)P_atm

  4. Equate the two water-column pressure expressions and solve 2h/5=9.

    Why valid: Both expressions describe the same hydrostatic contribution at the lake bottom.

    Result: h=22.5 m; option D

Result

Option D: 22.5 m.

Check

At h=22.5 m the water column contributes 9P_atm, so absolute bottom pressure is 10P_atm and Boyle's law predicts exactly a tenfold volume increase at the surface.

Choose the pressure reference correctly before coupling hydrostatics to another model.

A tempting wrong path

  • At depth h the absolute pressure is always just rho g h.
  • Container shape or slanted path length to the surface sets hydrostatic pressure change.

Diagnose

  • At the liquid surface where h=0, would this idea predict zero absolute pressure even though the atmosphere is present?
  • Two points in the same connected liquid have equal vertical depth but different horizontal positions and path shapes. Should their static pressures differ?

Repair

  • rho g h is the pressure increase relative to the surface. Add the surface pressure to obtain absolute pressure.
  • No. Under this model the pressure difference depends on vertical depth rho g Delta h, not path length.
