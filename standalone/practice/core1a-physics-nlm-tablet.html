⚖️ Newton's Laws of Motion Foundations (Core 1)

Build the force-and-momentum reasoning system before transfer: Choose body → Identify interactions → Construct FBD → Select Law → Verify dimensional sanity.

Newton I:          \(\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = 0 \iff \vec{a} = 0 \implies \vec{v} = \text{constant}\)
Newton II:         \(\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} = m\vec{a}\)
Impulse:            \(\vec{J} = \Delta\vec{p} = \int \vec{F}\,dt = \vec{F}_{\text{avg}}\,\Delta t\)
Newton III:        \(\vec{F}_{AB} = -\vec{F}_{BA}\) (equal and opposite on DIFFERENT bodies)
Concept Unit 01

NLM 01 - Motion is not force

Constant velocity can coexist with zero net force

NLM-01 · Core 1

What is happening?

Newton I links change of velocity to net external force. Motion itself does not require a sustaining net force.

Key Idea

If velocity is constant in an inertial frame, acceleration is zero and therefore the net external force is zero.

How It Works

  1. Choose the body and ask whether its velocity is changing.
  2. Draw all external forces on that body.
  3. Add them vectorially before discussing motion.
  4. Check: zero net force means constant velocity, including rest. NLM - Revised Core 1
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
A crate moves right at 3 m/s while 60 N right and 60 N left act on it. Fnet=0, so a=0; it continues at constant velocity.

⚠️ Watch Out

A moving object does not automatically need a forward net force.

✅ Quick Check

A puck glides at constant velocity on an ideal frictionless surface. What

Reveal Answer
horizontal net force is required? Answer: 0 N. NLM - Revised Core 1 4
Concept Unit 02

NLM 02 - Choose the body before drawing forces

Interactions become forces only after the body is chosen

NLM-02 · Core 1

What is happening?

A free-body diagram is a diagram of one chosen body. First identify what interacts with it; then draw only the forces acting on that body.

Key Idea

The phrase “force on X by Y” prevents body confusion and Newton-III mistakes.

How It Works

  1. Circle the body/system being analysed.
  2. List interacting bodies: Earth, surface, rope, hand, etc.
  3. Convert each interaction into one force arrow on the chosen body.
  4. Do not include forces the chosen body exerts on something else. NLM - Revised Core 1
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
For a book on a table, the book FBD contains Earth-on-book weight and table-on-book normal. The book-on-table force belongs to the table FBD.

⚠️ Watch Out

An interaction pair is not automatically two arrows on one FBD.

✅ Quick Check

A crate is pushed by a worker while sliding on a floor. Name three possible

Reveal Answer
external interactions on the crate. Answer: Worker-crate, Earth-crate, floor-crate. NLM - Revised Core 1 6
Concept Unit 03

NLM 03 - Net force is a vector sum

Balanced forces and no forces are different statements

NLM-03 · Core 1

What is happening?

Newton II uses the net external force, not whichever force is easiest to notice. Balanced forces can be nonzero individually.

Key Idea

Choose a positive axis, assign signs to force components, and sum them.

How It Works

  1. Choose +x and +y.
  2. Resolve forces into components when needed.
  3. Compute ΣFx and ΣFy.
  4. Only then infer acceleration components. NLM - Revised Core 1
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
A cart has 25 N right and 10 N left. With right positive, Fnet,x=25-10=15 N right.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not confuse “Fnet=0” with “there are no forces.”

✅ Quick Check

A box has 30 N right and 30 N left. What is its horizontal acceleration?

Reveal Answer
Answer: 0 m/s², regardless of its current velocity. NLM - Revised Core 1 8
Concept Unit 04

NLM 04 - Newton II and scaling

Acceleration responds to net force and mass together

NLM-04 · Core 1

What is happening?

For a chosen body of constant mass, Fnet=ma. At fixed mass, a scales with net force; at fixed force, a scales inversely with mass.

Key Idea

For changes in both force and mass, compare ratios before substituting numbers.

How It Works

  1. Write a1=F1/m1 and a2=F2/m2.
  2. Form a2/a1=(F2/F1)(m1/m2).
  3. Keep direction from the signed net force.
  4. Check units: 1 N = 1 kg·m/s². NLM - Revised Core 1
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
If force halves while mass doubles, a becomes (1/2)(1/2)=1/4 of its original value.

⚠️ Watch Out

“Half the force means half the acceleration” is true only if mass is unchanged.

✅ Quick Check

A 40 N net force accelerates 5 kg. What is a?

Reveal Answer
Answer: 8 m/s². NLM - Revised Core 1 10
Concept Unit 05

NLM 05 - Stopping force needs two models

Kinematics supplies acceleration; Newton II supplies force

NLM-05 · Core 1

What is happening?

Stopping-over-distance problems often require a kinematics step before a force step. Do not jump directly from distance to force.

Key Idea

For constant stopping acceleration, find a from v²=u²+2as, then use Fnet=ma; find time separately if requested.

How It Works

  1. Write the motion state u, v=0, s.
  2. Use v²=u²+2as to find a.
  3. Use F=ma with sign/direction.
  4. If time is needed, use v=u+at after a is known. NLM - Revised Core 1
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
A 0.020 kg pellet at 100 m/s stops uniformly in 0.10 m. a=(0-10000)/(0.20)=-50000 m/s², so F=-1000 N.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not mix the kinematic distance relation with Newton II as though they were one formula.

✅ Quick Check

If the same pellet stops over twice the distance, what happens to |a| and

Reveal Answer
|F|? Answer: Both halve. NLM - Revised Core 1 12
Concept Unit 06

NLM 06 - Momentum is a signed state

Momentum carries the direction of velocity

NLM-06 · Core 1

What is happening?

Momentum is p=mv. In one dimension its sign follows velocity. A change in direction can make Δp much larger than a simple speed difference.

Key Idea

Always write pi, pf, then Δp=pf-pi.

How It Works

  1. Choose a positive direction.
  2. Write signed velocities.
  3. Convert to signed momenta.
  4. Subtract final minus initial. NLM - Revised Core 1
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
A 0.20 kg ball changes from +8 m/s to -6 m/s. Δp=0.20(-6)-0.20(8)=-2.8 kg·m/s.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not discard signs when a body rebounds.

✅ Quick Check

At fixed mass, what is the shape of a p-v graph?

Reveal Answer
Answer: A straight line through the origin; slope = mass. NLM - Revised Core 1 14
Concept Unit 07

NLM 07 - Impulse and average force

The same momentum change can be spread over more time

NLM-07 · Core 1

What is happening?

Average force measures how quickly momentum changes: Favg=Δp/Δt. Increasing stopping time reduces force magnitude for the same Δp.

Key Idea

This is the physics behind moving the hands backward while catching.

How It Works

  1. Find the signed Δp.
  2. Identify the interaction duration Δt.
  3. Divide Δp by Δt.
  4. Check force direction against Δp, not against the initial velocity alone. NLM - Revised Core 1
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
A 0.50 kg ball at +12 m/s stops in 0.30 s: Δp=-6 kg·m/s, Favg=-20 N.

⚠️ Watch Out

Momentum and force are not the same quantity.

✅ Quick Check

If the same ball is stopped in twice the time, what happens to average

Reveal Answer
force magnitude? Answer: It halves. NLM - Revised Core 1 16
Concept Unit 08

NLM 08 - Newton III pairs

Equal and opposite does not mean same body

NLM-08 · Core 1

What is happening?

A third-law pair belongs to the same interaction, has equal magnitude and opposite direction, and acts on different bodies.

Key Idea

Use complete names: force on B by A; force on A by B.

How It Works

  1. Name both interacting bodies.
  2. Write the two force names.
  3. Place each force on the correct body’s FBD.
  4. Use Newton II separately to compare accelerations. NLM - Revised Core 1
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Earth pulls an apple downward with the same force magnitude that the apple pulls Earth upward. Their accelerations differ because their masses differ.

⚠️ Watch Out

Weight and normal on a resting book are balanced forces on one body, not a Newton-III pair.

✅ Quick Check

What is the third-law partner of the table-on-book normal force?

Reveal Answer
Answer: The book-on-table contact force. NLM - Revised Core 1 18
Concept Unit 09

NLM 09 - Momentum conservation is a system statement

Choose the system and check external impulse first

NLM-09 · Core 1

What is happening?

Total momentum is conserved only for a chosen system when the relevant external impulse over the interaction is negligible.

Key Idea

The equation pbefore,total=pafter,total comes after the system/interval check.

How It Works

  1. Draw a system boundary.
  2. Choose a positive direction.
  3. List all system momenta before and after.
  4. Check external impulse; then equate totals if valid. NLM - Revised Core 1
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Two skaters push apart from rest. For the two-skater system, initial total p=0, so their final momenta are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not claim conservation from one object’s momentum alone when an external force acts on it.

✅ Quick Check

A falling ball speeds up under gravity. Is the ball-only momentum

Reveal Answer
conserved? Answer: No; gravity gives the ball external impulse. NLM - Revised Core 1 20
Concept Unit 10

NLM 10 - Recoil, rockets and exchanged momentum

Equal momentum can mean very different speeds

NLM-10 · Core 1

What is happening?

In recoil/explosion problems with negligible external impulse, momentum exchanged internally appears with opposite signs.

Key Idea

For equal momentum magnitude, speed is inversely proportional to mass.

How It Works

  1. Choose launcher+projectile (or rocket+exhaust) as the system.
  2. Set initial total momentum.
  3. Assign opposite signs after separation.
  4. Solve one object’s speed from total momentum conservation. NLM - Revised Core 1
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
A 0.020 kg bullet leaves a 2.0 kg pistol at +150 m/s from rest. 0=(0.020)(150)+2v, so v=-1.5 m/s.

⚠️ Watch Out

A lighter rifle recoils faster for the same bullet momentum, not with a larger recoil momentum.

✅ Quick Check

Two rifles fire identical bullets with the same bullet momentum. Which

Reveal Answer
rifle has larger recoil speed? Answer: The lighter rifle. NLM - Revised Core 1 22
Concept Unit 11

NLM 11 - Read force from a motion graph

v-t slope → acceleration → force

NLM-11 · Core 1

What is happening?

A velocity-time graph gives acceleration from its slope. If friction is the only horizontal force, then Ffriction=ma.

Key Idea

The graph height is velocity; the slope is acceleration.

How It Works

  1. Read two points on the straight segment.
  2. Compute a=Δv/Δt.
  3. Convert mass to kilograms.
  4. Apply F=ma and interpret the sign. NLM - Revised Core 1
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
A 50 g object slows from 80 m/s to 0 in 8 s. a=-10 m/s²; F=0.050(-10)=-0.50 N.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not use the area under a v-t graph when the question asks for acceleration or force.

✅ Quick Check

If the same velocity change takes twice as long, what happens to |a| and

Reveal Answer
|F|? Answer: Both halve. NLM - Revised Core 1 24
