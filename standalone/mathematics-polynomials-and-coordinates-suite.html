📐 CORE1: Governing Principles & Domain Validity Orientation Map
1. Cartesian Coordinate Axiom:

Every point $P$ in a two-dimensional Euclidean plane is in one-to-one correspondence with an ordered real pair $(x, y)$.

  • Sign Conventions: QI $(+,+)$, QII $(-,+)$, QIII $(-,-)$, QIV $(+,-)$.
  • Boundary Axioms: Points on $X$-axis have ordinate $y = 0$; points on $Y$-axis have abscissa $x = 0$. Origin is $(0, 0)$.
2. Remainder Theorem & Factor Theorem:

Let $p(x)$ be any polynomial of degree $\ge 1$ and $a$ be any real number. If $p(x)$ is divided by the linear polynomial $(x - a)$, then the remainder is $p(a)$.

  • If divisor is $(b - ax)$, root is $x = b/a \implies \text{Remainder} = p(b/a)$.
  • Factor Theorem: $(x - a)$ is a factor of $p(x)$ if and only if $p(a) = 0$.
✍️ CORE1B: Active Recall Reconstruction (Mandatory Attempt) Commitment Gated

Recall Prompt: Without looking at the formulas above, state the Remainder Theorem for a polynomial $P(x)$ divided by $(ax - b)$, and explain why the perpendicular distance from the Y-axis to point $P(x_0, y_0)$ equals $|x_0|$.

Canonical Derivation:

1. Division Algorithm: $p(x) = (ax - b) \cdot q(x) + r(x)$.
Since divisor degree is $1$, remainder $r(x)$ must have degree $0$ (a constant $R$).
Setting $x = b/a$: $p(b/a) = (a(b/a) - b) \cdot q(b/a) + R = 0 \cdot q(b/a) + R = R$. Hence $\text{Remainder} = p(b/a)$.
2. Geometric Distance: By definition, the $Y$-axis is the vertical line $x = 0$. The perpendicular line from $P(x_0, y_0)$ to the $Y$-axis is horizontal ($y = y_0$), meeting the $Y$-axis at $M(0, y_0)$. The distance between $(x_0, y_0)$ and $(0, y_0)$ is $\sqrt{(x_0 - 0)^2 + (y_0 - y_0)^2} = \sqrt{x_0^2} = |x_0|$.

Problem 2: Find the value of $k$ if $(x - 1)$ is a factor of $p(x) = 4x^3 + 3x^2 - 4x + k$.

Apply the Factor Theorem.

💡 Rung 1: Factor Theorem Condition
If $(x - 1)$ is a factor of $p(x)$, then $p(1)$ must equal $0$.
💡 Rung 2: Substitution Setup
Substitute $x = 1$ into $p(x)$: $p(1) = 4(1)^3 + 3(1)^2 - 4(1) + k = 4 + 3 - 4 + k = 3 + k$.
💡 Rung 3: Solution
Set $3 + k = 0 \implies k = -3$.
