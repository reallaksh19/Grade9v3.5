## 1. The actual academic proposition

The learner is asked to **justify**, for every positive integer `m`, why
`(m+1)(m+2)(m+3)` is divisible by 6. This is a wholly **authored** familiar-family Core2A question, **not** a purported SOF or NCERT original Core2. It is not the same written item as the Core1A worked proof `n(n+1)(n+2)`; it is mathematically equivalent under `n=m+1`, so **no changed-demand/transfer claim** is made.

The targeted inferential bottleneck is not performing multiplication. The learner must replace a finite search for successful examples with two *exhaustive* number-theoretic guarantees: an even factor and a multiple-of-three factor; coprimality then establishes divisibility by 6. The same argument applies whether these divisors arise from distinct factors or happen to arise from a common factor.

**Proof check:** Let `n=m+1`, so the product is `n(n+1)(n+2)`. Exactly one of `n,n+1` is even. Among three consecutive integers, one is divisible by 3: the residues `n mod 3=0,1,2` respectively choose `n,n+2,n+1`. Therefore 2 and 3 each divide the whole integer product; since gcd(2,3)=1, 6 divides it. This proves the universal claim and does not rely on special sample m values.

The Core1A lesson teaches that warrant openly and contains an **independent authored exit**: prove the product of four consecutive positive integers divisible by 24. Its extra power-of-two argument (one factor of 4 and another even factor) must be checked separately. It is not the Core2A question's answer and its success would require an actual learner attempt before any mastery conclusion.

## 2. Review of the QRT decision, not a new scoring layer

The question's **one primary demand** is **JUSTIFY**, because establishing the general warrant decides the task. `REPRESENT` is secondary: `n=m+1` makes the relationship legible but is not the ultimate decision. This is **not** an APPLY answer merely because a taught construction is reusable.

Its authored five-component D evidence is (1,1,1,0,1), sum 4, hence **D2** under the existing shared difficulty derivation—not a second difficulty system. An independent academic reviewer may dispute these *authored estimates* without editing the 28-cell QRT grid. The existing resolver must produce the single template **`QRT-JUSTIFY-D2`** with all 12 H/S/P/M review asks.

The resolved slots are learner-relative, not new canonical mathematical truths:

- **X:** how to guarantee divisibility for arbitrary `m`, with the missing/uncertain capability named if known.
- **Y:** relevant **demonstrated** bridge only. If the synthetic profile marks the capability UNCERTAIN and provides no demonstrated prerequisite, the resolver explicitly says UNRESOLVED. Neither an estimated 95% nor 10% knowledge changes this.
- **Z:** exhaustive modulo-three and independent parity warrant, tied to the question's canonical `answer.crux_move_ref`.
- **W:** the learner must supply that actual general warrant. Pre-attempt figure and support must not write the residue table or complete the proof.

The 12 review questions are *not* a quota for 12 visible hint elements; H, S, P, M denote semantic jobs, and an honestly non-applicable figure can be justified. There is no 29th template and no QRT award/acceptance record created by this pilot.

The protected work W remains to show *universally* which factors satisfy 2 and 3 and why the result follows. Inspect actual hint timing and do not claim exam-level protection.
3. **SVG meaning:** The initial graphic must say only `m+1,m+2,m+3` and be fully named, while the complete parity/residue diagram belongs in the Core1A teaching and post-attempt repair. A decorative three-box strip alone is *not* evidence of student learning; it is a low-cognitive-load representation and should not be marketed as a sophisticated interaction.
4. **Teacher honesty:** The original Core1A package's title and lesson are authored. None of 68 SOF positions has been source-cleared, and the seven earlier original IMO practice drafts are separate. No real learner has been profiled or assessed in this TEST run.
5. **Academic independence:** This is the designer's self-critique. An independent academic reviewer must examine whether the crux, D-band, hints, figure, alternative proof and exit match the intended learner. Existing CI cannot claim this review.
6. **Page layout / accessibility:** Chrome desktop, tablet, phone, 200%-text and keyboard Enter checks are not a substitute for human screen-reader testing, motor accessibility review or actual comprehension observations. All unperformed checks remain NOT_RUN.
7. **Full six-Core architecture:** This evidence qualifies **two connected roles, CORE2A and CORE1A**, not the Core1/Core1B/Core2/Core2B roles, authentic source pipeline, all 28 QRT cells in rendered products or accepted curriculum.
