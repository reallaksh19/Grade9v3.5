Worked explanation

Unit 1 Ex 1.1 Q1 · Verified curricular source, faithful NCERT wording

Every rational number is

Follow the operation, its justification and its result.

  1. Interpret 'Every rational number is' as asking for a set containing all rational numbers.

    Why valid: A universal claim must hold for negative and fractional rationals, not just positive whole examples.

    Result: Find a valid universal superset of Q.

  2. Use Q ⊆ R: every rational a/b with integer a, b ≠ 0 represents a real number.

    Why valid: Every quotient of integers with nonzero denominator is a real number.

    Result: Option (C) is valid.

  3. Test 1/2 against natural, integer and whole-number categories.

    Why valid: One nonintegral rational refutes each narrower universal option.

    Result: Reject the narrower categories; retain (C).

Result

(C) a real number. Every rational number is a real number.

Check

Independent definition and counterexample check: 1/2 is rational and real, but not a natural number, integer or whole number; √2 is real but irrational, so reverse inclusion does not follow.

Mistake clinic · diagnose and repair

A tempting wrong path

  • Every rational number is an integer because it is a quotient of integers.

Diagnose

  • Is 1/2 an integer? Can 1/2 be written as a/b with integers a and b ≠ 0?

Repair

  • A quotient of integers need not be an integer; rationals include non-integer fractions.
