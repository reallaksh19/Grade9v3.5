📐 Mathematical Invariants: The Grade 9 Coordinate Proof Toolkit

At Grade 9 level, coordinate geometry ceases to be passive point-marking. It becomes a tool for rigorous geometric deduction:

1. The Rhombus vs. Square Diagnostic

Equal side lengths alone do not prove a square. A figure with vertices on axes bisecting at right angles is a Rhombus unless both diagonal lengths are strictly verified to be equal.

2. The Trapezium Parallelism Proof

Prove one pair of opposite sides has identical horizontal or vertical ordinates/abscissas (e.g. \( y_1 = y_2 \) and \( y_3 = y_4 \)), while the other pair has non-parallel slopes and unequal segment lengths.

3. Constrained Vertex Derivation

When coordinates must satisfy multi-step constraints (e.g. origin anchor, axis alignment, specified quadrant), solve by enforcing orthogonal displacements along the grid.

4. Collinearity without Distance Formula

Verify whether points share an identical coordinate step ratio (slope): \( \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{y_3 - y_2}{x_3 - x_2} \).

Problem 1 · Short Answer Q5 · Page 3.9 SRC-COORD-M2-SA-05 · D3
If we join the points \( A(-2, 0), B(0, 1), C(2, 0) \) and \( D(0, -1) \) in order, then the name of the quadrilateral formed is:
💡 Need a Hint? (Progressive 3-Rung Ladder)
Rigorous Deduction Steps:
  1. Side Lengths: \( AB = \sqrt{(0 - (-2))^2 + (1 - 0)^2} = \sqrt{4 + 1} = \sqrt{5} \). All four side lengths equal \( \sqrt{5} \).
  2. Diagonal Analysis: Diagonal \( AC \) lies along the x-axis with length \( 2 - (-2) = 4 \). Diagonal \( BD \) lies along the y-axis with length \( 1 - (-1) = 2 \).
  3. Perpendicular Bisector Test: The axes intersect orthogonally at \( (0, 0) \), which is the midpoint of both diagonals.
  4. Conclusion: Diagonals bisect each other perpendicularly at \( 90^\circ \) but have unequal lengths (\( 4 \neq 2 \)). Therefore, the quadrilateral is a Rhombus (Option C), not a square!
🧭 Formal Mathematical Reasoning Route:
DIAGONAL_AXIS_PROJECTION Identify diagonals AC on x-axis and BD on y-axis
Why Valid: Points with zero ordinate lie on x-axis; points with zero abscissa lie on y-axis.
ORTHOGONALITY Determine that diagonals intersect at origin (0, 0) at 90°
Why Valid: Cartesian coordinate axes are mutually perpendicular.
DIAGONAL_LENGTH_COMPARISON Compute AC = 4 and BD = 2, noting AC ≠ BD
Why Valid: Distance on axes is absolute difference of coordinates.
CLASSIFICATION Conclude the quadrilateral is a Rhombus, not a square
Why Valid: A quadrilateral with mutually perpendicular bisecting diagonals of unequal length is a rhombus.
⚠️ Diagnostic Exam Tip: Many students calculate equal sides (\( \sqrt{5} \)) and prematurely pick 'Square', neglecting the essential diagonal equality condition!
Figure 1: Cartesian Quadrilateral Diagnostic
Key Invariant: All 4 sides equal $\sqrt{5}$. Diagonals $AC=4$ and $BD=2$ bisect perpendicularly at $(0,0)$. Unequal diagonals ($4 \neq 2$) prove Rhombus.
Problem 2 · Short Answer Q2 · Page 3.9 SRC-COORD-M2-SA-02 · D3
Plot the points \( P(-3, 2), Q(-7, -3), R(6, -3), S(2, 2) \) and deduce the precise geometric name of the quadrilateral obtained by joining them in order.
💡 Need a Hint? (Progressive 3-Rung Ladder)
Rigorous Deduction Steps:
  • Side \( PS \) has constant ordinate \( y = 2 \); length \( 2 - (-3) = 5 \).
  • Side \( QR \) has constant ordinate \( y = -3 \); length \( 6 - (-7) = 13 \).
  • Both segments are parallel to the x-axis, so \( PS \parallel QR \).
  • Their lengths are unequal (\( 5 \neq 13 \)), and opposite sides \( PQ \) and \( SR \) are non-parallel.
  • A quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel opposite sides is a Trapezium.
🧭 Formal Mathematical Reasoning Route:
PARALLELISM_DETECTION Identify constant ordinate lines y = 2 and y = -3
Why Valid: Lines with identical constant ordinates are parallel to the x-axis and to each other.
SEGMENT_LENGTH_EVALUATION Compute horizontal segment lengths PS = 5 and QR = 13
Why Valid: Length of horizontal segment is x_right - x_left.
NON_PARALLEL_VERIFICATION Verify slopes of PQ and SR are unequal
Why Valid: PQ slope is 5/4; SR slope is -5/4.
CLASSIFICATION Conclude PQRS is a Trapezium
Why Valid: A quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel opposite sides is a trapezium.
⚠️ Diagnostic Exam Tip: Do not assume equal side lengths without checking the horizontal base coordinates.
Figure 2: Parallelism Verification (y = 2 & y = -3)
Key Invariant: $PS \parallel QR$ along horizontal lines $y=2$ and $y=-3$. Non-parallel sides $PQ$ and $SR$ are unequal $\implies$ Trapezium.
Problem 3 · Short Answer Q10 · Page 3.9 SRC-COORD-M2-SA-10 · D3
Write the coordinates of the vertices of a rectangle whose length and breadth are 5 and 3 units respectively, one vertex is at the origin, the longer side lies on the x-axis, and one of the vertices lies in the third quadrant.
💡 Need a Hint? (Progressive 3-Rung Ladder)
Rigorous Deduction Steps:
  1. Vertex 1: Given at origin \( D(0, 0) \).
  2. Longer side (length 5) on x-axis: Since a vertex lies in Quadrant III (\( x < 0, y < 0 \)), the longer side must extend along the negative x-axis: \( A(-5, 0) \).
  3. Breadth (length 3): Extends downward into the negative half-plane: \( C(0, -3) \).
  4. Fourth Vertex in Quadrant III: Combining step \( -5 \) and step \( -3 \) yields \( B(-5, -3) \).
  5. Final Vertices: \( (0, 0), (-5, 0), (-5, -3), (0, -3) \) (Option B).
🧭 Formal Mathematical Reasoning Route:
QUADRANT_SIGN_CONSTRAINT Enforce x < 0 and y < 0 for Quadrant III target
Why Valid: By Cartesian sign convention, Quadrant III contains points where both coordinates are negative.
PRIMARY_AXIS_EXTENSION Place longer side of 5 units on negative x-axis: A(-5, 0)
Why Valid: Side lies on x-axis starting from origin (0, 0) towards negative half-plane.
ORTHOGONAL_DISPLACEMENT Place breadth of 3 units on negative y-axis: C(0, -3)
Why Valid: Adjacent side of rectangle must be perpendicular to x-axis, extending along y-axis.
FOURTH_VERTEX_CLOSURE Deduce fourth vertex B(-5, -3)
Why Valid: Fourth vertex combines horizontal shift of -5 and vertical shift of -3.
⚠️ Diagnostic Exam Tip: Option A places the vertex in Quadrant IV instead of Quadrant III; Option C confuses length and breadth.
Figure 3: Quadrant III Spatial Constraint Deduction
Key Invariant: Origin anchor $(0,0)$ + length 5 on x-axis + Q3 constraint ($x<0, y<0$) uniquely forces $A(-5, 0)$, $C(0, -3)$, and $B(-5, -3)$.
Problem 4 · Short Answer Q4 · Page 3.9 SRC-COORD-M2-SA-04 · D3
Check which of the following sets of points are collinear (lie on a single straight line):
(i) \( (1, 3), (-1, -1), (-2, -3) \)
(ii) \( (1, 1), (2, -3), (-1, -2) \)
(iii) \( (0, 0), (2, 2), (5, 5) \)
💡 Need a Hint? (Progressive 3-Rung Ladder)
Rigorous Deduction Steps:
  • Set (i): Slope between first two: \( \frac{-1 - 3}{-1 - 1} = \frac{-4}{-2} = 2 \). Slope between last two: \( \frac{-3 - (-1)}{-2 - (-1)} = \frac{-2}{-1} = 2 \). Slopes are equal (\( 2 = 2 \)) \( \implies \) collinear.
  • Set (ii): Slope 1: \( \frac{-3 - 1}{2 - 1} = -4 \). Slope 2: \( \frac{-2 - (-3)}{-1 - 2} = -\frac{1}{3} \). Slopes differ (\( -4 \neq -\frac{1}{3} \)) \( \implies \) not collinear.
  • Set (iii): All points satisfy \( y = x \) with slope 1 \( \implies \) collinear.
  • Conclusion: (i) and (iii) only (Option B).
🧭 Formal Mathematical Reasoning Route:
STEP_RATIO_DEFINITION Formulate slope ratio condition m = (y2 - y1)/(x2 - x1)
Why Valid: Points on a single line maintain invariant rise-over-run step ratios.
EVALUATION_SET_1 Evaluate Set (i): (-4)/(-2) = 2 and (-2)/(-1) = 2 -> Collinear
Why Valid: Identical step ratio 2 confirms collinearity.
EVALUATION_SET_2 Evaluate Set (ii): (-4)/(1) = -4 vs (1)/(-3) = -1/3 -> Non-collinear
Why Valid: Different step ratios mean direction bends.
EVALUATION_SET_3 Evaluate Set (iii): (2)/(2) = 1 and (3)/(3) = 1 -> Collinear
Why Valid: All points satisfy y = x with slope 1.
⚠️ Diagnostic Exam Tip: Do not evaluate collinearity by eye on sketches; always verify equal step ratios (slopes).
Figure 4: Comparative Collinearity by Slope Ratios
Key Invariant: Collinearity is tested via constant coordinate step ratios: $\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1} = \frac{y_3-y_2}{x_3-x_2}$. Holds for (i) & (iii) only.
Problem 5 · Short Answer Q11 · Page 3.9 SRC-COORD-M2-SA-11 · D3
Three vertices of a square are given as \( P(1, 0), Q(4, 0) \) and \( S(1, 3) \). Determine the exact coordinates of the fourth vertex \( R \) such that \( PQRS \) forms a square.
💡 Need a Hint? (Progressive 3-Rung Ladder)
Rigorous Deduction Steps:
  • Base side \( PQ \): Lies along x-axis from \( (1, 0) \) to \( (4, 0) \); side length = \( 4 - 1 = 3 \).
  • Vertical side \( PS \): Extends along \( x = 1 \) from \( y = 0 \) to \( y = 3 \); side length = \( 3 - 0 = 3 \).
  • To close square \( PQRS \), vertex \( R \) must be 3 units directly above \( Q(4, 0) \) and 3 units directly right of \( S(1, 3) \).
  • Hence, \( x_R = 4 \) and \( y_R = 3 \), giving \( R(4, 3) \) (Option B).
🧭 Formal Mathematical Reasoning Route:
BASE_VECTOR_ANALYSIS Calculate base segment vector PQ = (4 - 1, 0) = (3, 0)
Why Valid: Horizontal segment with length 3.
SIDE_VECTOR_ANALYSIS Calculate side segment vector PS = (0, 3 - 0) = (0, 3)
Why Valid: Vertical segment of equal length 3 orthogonal to PQ.
VECTOR_ADDITION Compute R = Q + PS = (4, 0) + (0, 3) = (4, 3)
Why Valid: Opposite side QR of a square is parallel and equal to PS.
⚠️ Diagnostic Exam Tip: Confusing abscissa and ordinate produces (3, 4), which does not match vertical alignment above Q.
Figure 5: Square Vertex Projection Construction
Key Invariant: Base $\vec{PQ} = (3, 0)$ and vertical edge $\vec{PS} = (0, 3)$. The 4th vertex must satisfy $\vec{R} = \vec{Q} + (0, 3) = (4, 3)$.
Problem 6 · Short Answer Q12 · Page 3.9 SRC-COORD-M2-SA-12 · D3
Three vertices of a rectangle are given as \( A(-4, 2), B(3, 2) \) and \( C(-4, 5) \). Plot these points and determine the coordinates of the fourth vertex \( D \).
💡 Need a Hint? (Progressive 3-Rung Ladder)
Rigorous Deduction Steps:
  • Base \( AB \) lies on horizontal line \( y = 2 \) from \( x = -4 \) to \( x = 3 \); length = \( 3 - (-4) = 7 \).
  • Left side \( AC \) lies on vertical line \( x = -4 \) from \( y = 2 \) to \( y = 5 \); breadth = \( 5 - 2 = 3 \).
  • Opposite side \( BD \) must be vertical through \( B(3, 2) \) with length 3: \( x_D = 3 \), \( y_D = 2 + 3 = 5 \).
  • Opposite side \( CD \) must be horizontal through \( C(-4, 5) \) with length 7: \( y_D = 5 \), \( x_D = -4 + 7 = 3 \).
  • Therefore, \( D = (3, 5) \) (Option A).
🧭 Formal Mathematical Reasoning Route:
AXIS_ALIGNMENT Identify AB as horizontal line y = 2 and AC as vertical line x = -4
Why Valid: Identical ordinates define horizontal lines; identical abscissas define vertical lines.
RECTANGLE_INVARIANT Enforce opposite sides parallel and equal in length
Why Valid: Opposite vertices in axis-aligned rectangle share abscissas and ordinates.
COORDINATE_SYNTHESIS Set x_D = x_B = 3 and y_D = y_C = 5 to get D(3, 5)
Why Valid: Horizontal from C gives (-4 + 7, 5) = (3, 5); vertical from B gives (3, 2 + 3) = (3, 5).
⚠️ Diagnostic Exam Tip: Verify that opposite sides match both horizontal length 7 and vertical breadth 3.
Figure 6: Rectangle Vertex Coordinate Derivation
Key Invariant: Opposite sides are equal and parallel: $x_D = x_B = 3$, and $y_D = y_C = 5 \implies D(3, 5)$.
Problem 7 · Comprehension I Q1 · Page 3.8 SRC-COORD-M2-COMP-01 · D3
Plot the points \( A(-2, 0), B(2, 0), C(2, 2), D(0, 4), E(-2, 2) \) on graph paper and join in order. What is the figure obtained by joining \( ABCDE \)?
💡 Need a Hint? (Progressive 3-Rung Ladder)
Rigorous Deduction Steps:
  • Connecting five distinct non-collinear vertices \( A \to B \to C \to D \to E \to A \) in cyclic order forms a 5-sided closed polygon.
  • A polygon with 5 sides and 5 angles is by definition a Pentagon (Option D).
🧭 Formal Mathematical Reasoning Route:
VERTEX_ENUMERATION Count 5 non-collinear points in the Cartesian plane
Why Valid: Coordinates represent 5 unique positions.
POLYGON_CLOSURE Connect segments AB, BC, CD, DE, EA in cyclical order
Why Valid: Sequential connection of n non-collinear vertices forms an n-gon.
CLASSIFICATION Classify the 5-gon as a Pentagon
Why Valid: By definition, a polygon with 5 edges and 5 vertices is a pentagon.
⚠️ Diagnostic Exam Tip: Do not confuse the count of vertices (5) with a 6-sided hexagon.
Figure 7: 5-Vertex Symmetric Polygon Formation
Key Invariant: 5 distinct non-collinear vertices joined in order produce a 5-sided closed polygon, which is a Pentagon.
Problem 8 · Comprehension I Q2 · Page 3.8 SRC-COORD-M2-COMP-02 · D3
Points \( A(-2, 0), B(2, 0), C(2, 2), D(0, 4), E(-2, 2) \) are joined in order to form pentagon \( ABCDE \). What is the exact area of this figure?
💡 Need a Hint? (Progressive 3-Rung Ladder)
Rigorous Deduction Steps:
  1. Draw horizontal chord \( EC \) at \( y = 2 \). This partitions the pentagon into two canonical regions.
  2. Rectangle \( ABCE \): Base \( AB = 2 - (-2) = 4 \); Height \( BC = 2 - 0 = 2 \). Area = \( 4 \times 2 = 8 \) sq. units.
  3. Triangle \( ECD \): Base \( EC = 4 \); Height from \( y = 2 \) to apex \( D(0, 4) \) is \( 4 - 2 = 2 \). Area = \( \frac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 2 = 4 \) sq. units.
  4. Total Area: \( 8 + 4 = 12 \) sq. units (Option A).
🧭 Formal Mathematical Reasoning Route:
PARTITION_SELECTION Construct horizontal chord EC at line y = 2
Why Valid: Both E(-2, 2) and C(2, 2) share ordinate y = 2, forming a valid interior chord.
RECTANGLE_AREA Calculate Area(ABCE) = base × height = 4 × 2 = 8 sq units
Why Valid: Base AB = 2 - (-2) = 4; vertical height = 2 - 0 = 2.
TRIANGLE_AREA Calculate Area(ECD) = (1/2) × base × height = (1/2) × 4 × 2 = 4 sq units
Why Valid: Base EC = 4; apex D(0, 4) height above chord is 4 - 2 = 2.
ADDITIVE_COMPOSITION Compute Total Area = 8 + 4 = 12 sq units
Why Valid: Area of a non-overlapping planar union is the sum of regional areas.
⚠️ Diagnostic Exam Tip: Forgetting the triangular roof area (4 sq units) leads to Option B (8 sq units).
Figure 8: Decomposition Method (Area = 8 + 4 = 12)
Key Invariant: Splitting along chord $EC$ yields Base Rectangle ($4 \times 2 = 8$) and Apex Triangle ($\frac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 2 = 4$). Total Area = 12 sq. units.
Problem 9 · Multiple Correct 4 · Page 3.6 SRC-COORD-M2-MC-04 · D3
The distance between the points \( A(C, 0) \) on the positive x-axis and \( B(0, -C) \) on the negative y-axis (where \( C > 0 \)) is:
💡 Need a Hint? (Progressive 3-Rung Ladder)
Rigorous Deduction Steps:
  • Apply distance formula: \( d = \sqrt{(C - 0)^2 + (0 - (-C))^2} = \sqrt{C^2 + C^2} = \sqrt{2C^2} \).
  • Since \( C > 0 \), \( \sqrt{2C^2} = \sqrt{2} \cdot C \) (Option B).
  • Geometrically, this represents the hypotenuse of an isosceles right triangle with perpendicular legs of length \( C \).
🧭 Formal Mathematical Reasoning Route:
AXIS_PROJECTION Establish that A and B lie on orthogonal coordinate axes
Why Valid: A has y=0 (x-axis); B has x=0 (y-axis).
PYTHAGOREAN_FORMULATION Form right triangle with legs OA = C and OB = C
Why Valid: Axes are perpendicular at the origin.
ALGEBRAIC_SIMPLIFICATION Evaluate hypotenuse d = sqrt(C^2 + C^2) = sqrt(2C^2) = sqrt(2)*C
Why Valid: Since C > 0, sqrt(C^2) = C.
⚠️ Diagnostic Exam Tip: Do not square the result (producing Option D) or confuse \( \sqrt{2}C \) with \( \sqrt{2}C^2 \).
Figure 9: Parametric Right Triangle Distance
Key Invariant: By the Pythagorean theorem on isosceles right $\triangle AOB$: $AB = \sqrt{C^2 + (-C)^2} = \sqrt{2C^2} = \sqrt{2}C$.
Problem 10 · Multiple Correct 6 · Page 3.6 SRC-COORD-M2-MC-06 · D3
If the coordinates of point \( A \) are \( (a, b) \) with \( ab > 0 \), then \( A \) can lie in which quadrant(s)?
💡 Need a Hint? (Progressive 3-Rung Ladder)
Rigorous Deduction Steps:
  • The product \( ab > 0 \) is positive if and only if \( a \) and \( b \) have the same sign.
  • Case 1: \( a > 0 \) and \( b > 0 \implies (a, b) \) lies in Quadrant I.
  • Case 2: \( a < 0 \) and \( b < 0 \implies (a, b) \) lies in Quadrant III.
  • In Quadrant II (\( -, + \)) and Quadrant IV (\( +, - \)), the product is negative (\( ab < 0 \)).
  • Therefore, \( A \) lies in either Quadrant I or Quadrant III (Option C).
🧭 Formal Mathematical Reasoning Route:
SIGN_DECOMPOSITION Split condition ab > 0 into two sign equivalence classes
Why Valid: By algebraic sign laws, xy > 0 iff sgn(x) = sgn(y) ≠ 0.
QUADRANT_I_MAPPING Map case a > 0, b > 0 to Quadrant I
Why Valid: Quadrant I is defined by positive abscissa and positive ordinate.
QUADRANT_III_MAPPING Map case a < 0, b < 0 to Quadrant III
Why Valid: Quadrant III is defined by negative abscissa and negative ordinate.
CONCLUSION State that point A can lie in Quadrant I or Quadrant III
Why Valid: Both cases satisfy the strict inequality ab > 0.
⚠️ Diagnostic Exam Tip: Remembering only positive coordinates forgets that the product of two negatives is also positive!
Figure 10: Sign Invariant Region (ab > 0)
Key Invariant: Product $ab > 0$ requires identical signs. Both positive in Quadrant I; both negative in Quadrant III.
Problem 11 · Long Answer Q4 · Page 3.10 SRC-COORD-M2-LA-04 · D3
In the figure given below, perpendiculars of lengths \( a \) and \( b \) are dropped from point \( Q \) (lying in Quadrant III) to the y-axis and x-axis respectively. Determine: (i) Abscissa of \( Q \), (ii) Ordinate of \( Q \), (iii) Coordinates of \( Q \).
💡 Need a Hint? (Progressive 3-Rung Ladder)
Rigorous Deduction Steps:
  1. Abscissa (x-coordinate): The perpendicular distance from \( Q \) to the y-axis is \( a \). Because \( Q \) lies to the left of the y-axis (Quadrant III), its abscissa is \( -a \).
  2. Ordinate (y-coordinate): The perpendicular distance from \( Q \) to the x-axis is \( b \). Because \( Q \) lies below the x-axis (Quadrant III), its ordinate is \( -b \).
  3. Point Coordinates: Combining signed displacements gives \( Q(-a, -b) \) (Option B).
🧭 Formal Mathematical Reasoning Route:
ABSCISSA_DEFINITION Determine x-coordinate from y-axis distance a: x = -a
Why Valid: Distance to y-axis is |x| = a; Quadrant III requires x < 0, so x = -a.
ORDINATE_DEFINITION Determine y-coordinate from x-axis distance b: y = -b
Why Valid: Distance to x-axis is |y| = b; Quadrant III requires y < 0, so y = -b.
COORDINATE_PAIRING Assemble full coordinate tuple Q(-a, -b)
Why Valid: Cartesian point representation is ordered pair (abscissa, ordinate).
⚠️ Diagnostic Exam Tip: Do not swap the perpendicular distances: distance to y-axis is the x-coordinate, not the y-coordinate!
Figure 11: Orthogonal Axis Projections (Textbook Model)
Key Invariant: Displacement $a$ to y-axis in Q3 gives abscissa $-a$; displacement $b$ to x-axis gives ordinate $-b$. Coordinates are $(-a, -b)$.
Problem 12 · Archive Corner 3 · Page 3.10 SRC-COORD-M2-ARC-03 · D3
The distance of the point \( P(-6, 8) \) from the origin \( (0, 0) \) is:
💡 Need a Hint? (Progressive 3-Rung Ladder)
Rigorous Deduction Steps:
  • Distance from origin is given by \( d = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \).
  • Substitute coordinates: \( d = \sqrt{(-6)^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10 \).
  • Forms the canonical \( (6, 8, 10) \) Pythagorean triple (scaled \( 3, 4, 5 \)).
  • Correct answer is 10 units (Option B).
🧭 Formal Mathematical Reasoning Route:
METRIC_FORMULATION Set up origin distance formula d = sqrt(x^2 + y^2)
Why Valid: Euclidean metric from (0, 0) to (x, y) via Pythagorean theorem.
COORDINATE_SUBSTITUTION Substitute (-6, 8): (-6)^2 = 36, 8^2 = 64
Why Valid: Squaring any real non-zero number yields a strictly positive result.
RADICAL_EVALUATION Compute sqrt(36 + 64) = sqrt(100) = 10 units
Why Valid: 100 is a perfect square; 10 is the principal root.
⚠️ Diagnostic Exam Tip: Never subtract squared negative coordinates: \( (-6)^2 = +36 \), not \( -36 \).
Figure 12: Pythagorean Metric to Origin
Key Invariant: Origin distance = $\sqrt{(-6)^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10$. Forms the fundamental $(6, 8, 10)$ Pythagorean triple.
