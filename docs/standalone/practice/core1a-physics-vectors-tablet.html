Concept Unit 01

VECTORS 01 - Scalars, vectors and sign

Direction is information; sign belongs to a declared axis

VEC-01 · Core 1

What is happening?

A scalar is complete with magnitude alone. A vector needs magnitude and direction. In one dimension, direction can be encoded by sign only after you declare the positive axis.

Key Idea

Do not classify from the unit. Ask whether reversing direction changes the physical state. Then declare an axis before attaching + or - signs.

How It Works

  1. Recognise: displacement, velocity, acceleration, force and momentum are vectors; distance, speed, time and mass are scalars.
  2. Represent: in 1D, choose +x first; then words such as east/west become signed components.
  3. Interpret: a negative answer is not a failed calculation. It points opposite the chosen positive axis.
  4. Validity: the sign changes if the axis convention changes; the physical motion does not.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Example. Take east as +x. A trolley moves 5 m east then 9 m west. Displacement = +5 + (-9) = -4 m = 4 m west. Distance = 5 + 9 = 14 m.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not use “negative = smaller” or “negative = wrong”. For a vector component, negative normally means opposite the declared positive axis.

✅ Quick Check

Take north as +y. A displacement is 7 m south.

Reveal Answer
-7 m.
Concept Unit 02

VECTORS 02 - Draw, compare and reverse vectors

Length represents magnitude; the arrowhead represents direction

VEC-02 · Core 1

What is happening?

A vector diagram is a physical representation, not decoration. Translating a free vector without rotating or resizing it leaves the vector unchanged. Reversing it creates the opposite vector.

Key Idea

Equal vectors need both the same magnitude and the same direction. The opposite vector -A has the same magnitude as A but points exactly the other way.

How It Works

  1. Read: direction runs from tail to head.
  2. Scale: relative arrow length should track relative magnitude.
  3. Translate: a free vector may slide without changing.
  4. Reverse: -A reverses direction, not magnitude.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Example. A 6 N force east and a 6 N force west have equal magnitudes, but they are not equal vectors. They are opposites.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not compare arrows only by length. Two arrows with the same length but different directions are different vectors.

Concept Unit 03

VECTORS 03 - Components, quadrants and angle reference

Choose the frame first; then project the arrow

VEC-03 · Core 1

What is happening?

Components are the signed projections of one vector onto chosen axes. They are not extra physical vectors; they are another description of the same arrow.

Key Idea

Geometry decides sine/cosine. The component adjacent to the stated angle uses cosine; the opposite component uses sine. Signs come from the arrow direction.

How It Works

  1. Frame: mark +x and +y before calculating.
  2. Project: identify adjacent and opposite sides relative to the stated angle.
  3. Sign: decide the quadrant before writing the final component values.
  4. Reconstruct: perpendicular components must satisfy A²=Aₓ²+Aᵧ².
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Example. A 10 N vector is 30° above the negative x-axis. Aₓ = -10 cos30° = -5√3 N Aᵧ = +10 sin30° = +5 N.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not memorize “cosine means x”. If the angle is measured from the y-axis, the y-component may be the adjacent side instead.

✅ Quick Check

A vector has Aₓ=-6 and Aᵧ=+8. Which quadrant?

Reveal Answer
Quadrant II; magnitude 10.
Concept Unit 04

VECTORS 04 - Add vectors and find a resultant

Head-to-tail geometry and component addition must agree

VEC-04 · Core 1

What is happening?

Vector addition combines directional changes. Place vectors head-to-tail; the resultant runs from the first tail to the final head. In components, add x with x and y with y.

Key Idea

The resultant is not the path length. Geometry and components are two views of the same addition.

How It Works

  1. Diagram route: place vectors head-to-tail and draw the closing diagonal.
  2. Component route: Rₓ=ΣAₓ, Rᵧ=ΣAᵧ.
  3. Magnitude: for perpendicular components, |R|=√(Rₓ²+Rᵧ²).
  4. Direction: use signs to fix the quadrant before using an inverse tangent.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Example. 9 m east then 12 m north gives R=(9,12) m. |R|=√(9²+12²)=15 m. θ=tan⁻¹(12/9)=53.1° north of east.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not add magnitudes blindly when directions differ. 9+12=21 m is the path distance, not the resultant displacement.

✅ Quick Check

A walker moves 3 m east then 4 m north.

Reveal Answer
5 m.
Concept Unit 05

VECTORS 05 - Subtract vectors and build change vectors

Final minus initial is a geometric operation

VEC-05 · Core 1

What is happening?

Vector subtraction means add the opposite: A-B=A+(-B). Changes use the same rule: Δv=v_f-v_i and Δp=p_f-p_i.

Key Idea

A change vector compares two states. Equal magnitudes do not imply zero change when directions differ.

How It Works

  1. State: draw or write the initial and final vectors from a common origin.
  2. Reverse: subtracting a vector means reversing it before addition.
  3. Compute: subtract corresponding components.
  4. Interpret: the change vector has its own magnitude and direction.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Example. vᵢ=(6,0) m/s and v_f=(0,6) m/s. Δv=(-6,6) m/s |Δv|=√72=6√2 m/s.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not say “speed did not change, so velocity did not change”. Turning changes velocity even at constant speed.

Reveal Answer
-7 m/s.
Concept Unit 06

VECTORS 06 - Recover magnitude and direction

Components are coordinates of the vector state

VEC-06 · Core 1

What is happening?

When Rₓ and Rᵧ are known, the magnitude follows from the component triangle. The direction needs both a reference axis and the correct quadrant.

Key Idea

Magnitude alone is not a complete vector answer. Report direction whenever the question asks for a vector.

How It Works

  1. Magnitude: |R|=√(Rₓ²+Rᵧ²) for perpendicular Cartesian components.
  2. Reference angle: tan α=|Rᵧ/Rₓ|.
  3. Quadrant: use the signs of Rₓ and Rᵧ to convert α into a physical direction.
  4. Check: reconstruct the components from the final magnitude and direction.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Example. F=(9,-12) N. |F|=15 N. Reference angle = tan⁻¹(12/9)=53.1°. Signs (+,-) place it in quadrant IV: 53.1° south of east.

⚠️ Watch Out

A calculator inverse tangent can return only a reference angle. It cannot choose the physical quadrant for you.

✅ Quick Check

Reveal Answer
5 units; 53.1° south of west.
Concept Unit 07

VECTORS 07 - Relative velocity

The observer is the vector you subtract

VEC-07 · Core 1

What is happening?

Relative velocity asks what one observer sees another object doing. “Velocity of A relative to B” means subtract the observer B from A.

Key Idea

Order follows the words: v_A/B = v_A - v_B. Express both velocities in the same axes before subtracting.

How It Works

  1. Name roles: object A, observer B.
  2. Same frame: write both ground-frame velocity vectors using one coordinate system.
  3. Subtract: component-by-component.
  4. Special case: if A and B move together with identical velocity, v_A/B must be zero.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Example. Cyclist A moves east at 8 m/s and B east at 5 m/s. v_A/B = +8-(+5)=+3 m/s: A moves 3 m/s east relative to B.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not reverse the order. v_A/B and v_B/A are opposites.

✅ Quick Check

Reveal Answer
0.
Concept Unit 08

VECTORS 08 - Tangent and radial directions

Circular motion separates velocity direction from acceleration direction

VEC-08 · Core 1

What is happening?

At any point on a circular path, instantaneous velocity is tangent to the path. Centripetal acceleration points radially inward toward the centre.

Key Idea

Constant speed does not mean constant velocity. Direction changes continuously, so the velocity vector changes and acceleration is nonzero.

How It Works

  1. Draw: mark the radius from the particle to the centre.
  2. Velocity: draw a tangent perpendicular to the radius.
  3. Acceleration: point inward along the radius.
  4. Release: if the inward constraint disappears, the object initially continues along the tangent.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Example. At the rightmost point of a circle, acceleration points left toward the centre. Velocity is vertical along the tangent; up/down depends on the direction of travel.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not draw the release direction radially outward. “Centrifugal” is not the instantaneous velocity direction in an inertial frame.

Reveal Answer
Rightward along the tangent.
Concept Unit 09

VECTORS 09 - Interaction pairs and body ownership

Equal-and-opposite forces can act on different bodies

VEC-09 · Core 1

What is happening?

Newton-III pairs are vector pairs with equal magnitude and opposite direction, but they act on different bodies. Net-force equations contain only forces acting on the chosen body.

Key Idea

Body ownership prevents false cancellation. Always label force as “A on B”.

How It Works

  1. Choose body: decide whose force diagram you are drawing.
  2. Name interaction: table on book, book on table, Earth on book, etc.
  3. Pair: F_A on B = -F_B on A.
  4. Net force: only sum forces acting on the same chosen body.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Example. The normal force of the table on the book and the normal force of the book on the table form a third-law pair. They do not cancel on the book because only one of them acts on the book.

⚠️ Watch Out

Weight and normal may balance on a resting book, but they are not a Newton-III pair because both act on the same body.

✅ Quick Check

Reveal Answer
No.
Concept Unit 10

VECTORS 10 - Angle between vectors and work

The angle between force and displacement controls the sign

VEC-10 · Core 1

What is happening?

Work is a scalar built from two vectors. Its sign depends on the angle between force and displacement: parallel gives positive work; opposite gives negative work.

Key Idea

Read the angle from the physical arrows before using the equation.

How It Works

  1. Represent: draw F and s from a common reference.
  2. Angle: identify θ between the vectors.
  3. Relation: W=Fs cosθ.
  4. Checks: θ=0° gives +Fs, θ=90° gives 0, θ=180° gives -Fs.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Example. A 200 N braking force acts opposite a 15 m displacement. θ=180° W=(200)(15)cos180°=-3000 J.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not infer the work sign from the word “force” alone. Direction relative to displacement decides the sign.

Reveal Answer

Concept Unit 11

VECTORS 11 - Bridge to Motion in 2D

One vector state becomes two component equations

VEC-11 · Core 1

What is happening?

Motion in a plane becomes manageable when position, velocity and acceleration are split into x and y components. The components share the same time but may evolve differently.

Key Idea

The component method is the bridge: represent the vector state first, then solve each axis, then reconstruct the final vector if needed.

How It Works

  1. State: v=vₓ i+vᵧ j.
  2. Separate: write one equation for x and one for y.
  3. Shared clock: the same time variable belongs to both component motions.
  4. Reconstruct: speed = √(vₓ²+vᵧ²); direction comes from the component ratio and quadrant.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Example. vₓ=6 m/s, vᵧ=8 m/s. speed = √(6²+8²)=10 m/s. Direction = tan⁻¹(8/6)=53.1° above +x.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not solve x and y with different times for the same physical instant.

✅ Quick Check

Reveal Answer
Straight downward.
