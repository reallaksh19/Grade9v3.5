Declared Conventions & Reference Rules

Established invariants required before any quantity or identity is evaluated:

  • Cartesian Reference Frame: Displacements along the surface are chosen positive in the direction of motion; upward perpendicular is positive normal.
  • Sign and Invariant Energy Conventions: Potential energy is referenced to zero at the lowest point of interest ($U(0) = 0$). Work done by dissipative forces is zero in frictionless models.
1

Mechanical Energy Conservation on a Frictionless Incline

D4 · High-Transfer Principle
Subject: Physics Topic: Cognitive Demand Calibration Specimen Concept difficulty: Hard concept Cognitive demand: ⛓️ Model & multi-step chain Review rubric: CRT-PHY-CONSTRUCTION

Inferential Leap: Proving that normal contact force does strictly zero work, so that mechanical energy depends solely on vertical altitude change $h = s \sin\theta$ independent of path length or mass.

Demand Scaffolding ($X, Y, Z, W$) · Cognitive Architecture ▾
Target Concept ($X$)Conservation of mechanical energy on an inclined plane
Entry Foundation ($Y$)1D work-energy theorem, vector resolution of gravity, and normal force orthogonality
Inferential Leap ($Z$)Decoupling ramp distance $s$ into vertical drop $h = s \sin\theta$ where non-conservative work $W_{\text{nc}} = 0$
Exit Verification ($W$)Observable exit task: derive the limiting case as $\theta \to 90^\circ$ and verify $v = \sqrt{2gh}$

Entry Assumptions & Prerequisites

  • Can apply the one-dimensional work-energy theorem $\Delta K = W_{\text{net}}$.
  • Can resolve force vectors into orthogonal Cartesian components.
  • Understands that work is the scalar product of force and displacement ($W = \vec{F} \cdot \Delta \vec{s}$).

Logical Construction Steps

  1. Action Analyze the work done by the normal contact force $\vec{N}$.
    Why Valid The normal force is perpendicular to the surface of the incline at all points, so $\vec{N} \cdot \Delta \vec{s} = N \Delta s \cos(90^\circ) = 0$.
    State Output $W_N = 0\text{ J}$ (Normal force performs zero work)
  2. Action Resolve the gravitational force along the incline and compute work over distance $s$.
    Why Valid Component of gravity along the incline is $F_{\parallel} = mg \sin\theta$. Work done is $W_g = (mg \sin\theta)s = mg(s \sin\theta) = mgh$.
    State Output $W_{\text{net}} = W_g = mgh$
  3. Action Equate network done to change in kinetic energy ($u = 0$).
    Why Valid By the Work-Energy Theorem, $\Delta K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 - 0 = W_{\text{net}} = mgh$. Mass cancels from both sides.
    State Output $\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = mgh \implies v = \sqrt{2gh} = \sqrt{2gs \sin\theta}$

Governing Relations & Domain of Validity

$$\mathbf{ \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = mgh = mg s \sin\theta \iff v = \sqrt{2gs \sin\theta} }$$

Valid when: Valid for smooth surfaces where frictional dissipation is negligible ($\mu = 0$) under uniform gravity $g$.

Authentic Worked Anchor

Problem: A 2.0 kg block is released from rest at the top of a smooth 30° incline of length 10 m. Calculate its speed at the base ($g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$).

  1. Identify the given parameters: $m = 2.0\text{ kg}, s = 10\text{ m}, \theta = 30^\circ, g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$.
  2. Compute vertical displacement: $h = s \sin(30^\circ) = 10 \times 0.5 = 5.0\text{ m}$.
  3. Apply energy conservation: $v = \sqrt{2gh} = \sqrt{2 \times 9.8 \times 5.0} = \sqrt{98}$.
  4. Evaluate: $v \approx 9.90\text{ m/s}$.

Verified Conclusion

The block reaches the bottom with speed $v = \sqrt{98} \approx 9.90\text{ m/s}$, entirely independent of its 2.0 kg mass.

Progressive Representation Interactive Staging

⚠️ Plausible Wrong Path (Misconception)

Assuming that the longer the incline, the more energy is lost or the slower the block travels, or multiplying gravity by $s$ directly ($mgs$) without the $\sin\theta$ factor.

🔧 Diagnostic & Repair

Diagnostic prompt: If an incline has length 20 m but the same vertical height $h = 5\text{ m}$, does the block reach the bottom with a different speed?

Corrective move: No. In the absence of friction, $v = \sqrt{2gh}$ depends purely on the vertical altitude drop $h$, not on the slope length $s$. The longer incline simply reduces acceleration while increasing travel time, yielding the exact same final speed.

  • 1. Check (Recall)

    Why does the normal contact force do zero mechanical work on a sliding object?

  • 2. Apply (Minimal Case)

    A frictionless ramp has height $h = 20\text{ m}$. What is the speed of an object sliding from rest at the bottom ($g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$)?

  • 3. Connect (Transfer)

    How does this energy conservation principle extend to curved ramps and roller-coasters where the angle $\theta$ changes continuously?

Try it with less support · Observable Exit Criterion

Dimensional Synthesis of the Newton from Fundamental SI Base Units

D1 · Foundational Definition
Subject: Physics Topic: Cognitive Demand Calibration Specimen Concept difficulty: Easy concept Cognitive demand: 💡 Conceptual explanation Review rubric: CRT-PHY-CONSTRUCTION

Inferential Leap: Understanding that derived SI units are not arbitrary names, but are structurally bound definitions dictated by governing physical laws.

Demand Scaffolding ($X, Y, Z, W$) · Cognitive Architecture ▾
Target Concept ($X$)Derived unit Newton (N) in terms of fundamental dimensions
Entry Foundation ($Y$)Base SI units of mass (kg), length (m), and time (s)
Inferential Leap ($Z$)Dynamic definition from Newton's second law: $1\text{ N} \equiv 1\text{ kg} \cdot 1\text{ m/s}^2$
Exit Verification ($W$)Observable exit task: derive the base unit expression of momentum $p = mv$ and show $[F \times t] = [p]$

Entry Assumptions & Prerequisites

  • Familiarity with the 7 fundamental SI base quantities (specifically mass, length, time).
  • Understanding Newton's second law in algebraic form: $F = ma$.

Logical Construction Steps

  1. Action Identify the base SI quantities that form Newton's second law.
    Why Valid Mass is measured in kilograms ($\text{kg}$), and acceleration is change in velocity per unit time ($\text{m/s}^2$).
    State Output $[m] = \text{kg},\quad [a] = \text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-2}$
  2. Action Multiply base dimensions according to the product relation $F = ma$.
    Why Valid Dimensional consistency requires $[F] = [m][a] = \text{kg} \times (\text{m/s}^2)$.
    State Output $[F] = \text{kg}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-2}$
  3. Action Bind the derived unit name (Newton) to this composite base product.
    Why Valid By international convention, 1 Newton is defined as the force giving 1 kg mass an acceleration of $1\text{ m/s}^2$.
    State Output $1\text{ N} \equiv 1\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}/\text{s}^2$

Governing Relations & Domain of Validity

$$\mathbf{ 1\text{ N} = 1\text{ kg} \cdot 1\text{ m/s}^2 = 1\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-2} }$$

Valid when: Universal definition in the International System of Units (SI).

Authentic Worked Anchor

Problem: Express a force of $25\text{ N}$ acting on a $5.0\text{ kg}$ body in fundamental base units and compute the resulting acceleration.

  1. Express force in base units: $25\text{ N} = 25\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}/\text{s}^2$.
  2. Apply $a = F/m$: $a = \frac{25\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}/\text{s}^2}{5.0\text{ kg}}$.
  3. Cancel kilograms: $a = 5.0\text{ m/s}^2$.

Verified Conclusion

The resulting acceleration is $5.0\text{ m/s}^2$, demonstrating dimensional cancellation.

Progressive Representation Interactive Staging

⚠️ Plausible Wrong Path (Misconception)

Confusing force with energy (writing $\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2$) or dividing by time instead of time squared.

🔧 Diagnostic & Repair

Diagnostic prompt: What is the difference between the base unit of force ($ ext{N}$) and the base unit of energy ($ ext{J}$)?

Corrective move: Energy is work ($F \times d$), which multiplies force by an extra meter: $1\text{ J} = 1\text{ N}\cdot\text{m} = 1\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2$. Force has length to power 1, whereas energy has length to power 2.

1-2-3 Triad Quick Check

  • 1. Check (Recall)

    What are the three fundamental SI base units that compose one Newton?

  • 2. Apply (Minimal Case)

    If an object has mass $0.5\text{ kg}$ and acceleration $4\text{ m/s}^2$, what net force in Newtons acts on it?

  • 3. Connect (Transfer)

    How is impulse defined as $J = F \Delta t$, and what are its base SI units?

Try it with less support · Observable Exit Criterion

Determine the SI base unit composition of linear momentum $p = mv$. Then, prove that impulse $F \Delta t$ has identical SI base units.

The Factor Theorem and Remainder Vanishing from First Principles

D2 · Algebraic Foundation
Subject: Physics Topic: Cognitive Demand Calibration Specimen Concept difficulty: Medium concept Cognitive demand: 📐 Proof & formal derivation Review rubric: CRT-MAT-CONSTRUCTION

Inferential Leap: Connecting polynomial remainder evaluation $p(a) = 0$ directly to exact algebraic divisibility $(x - a) \mid p(x)$.

Demand Scaffolding ($X, Y, Z, W$) · Cognitive Architecture ▾
Target Concept ($X$)Factor Theorem: $(x - a) \mid p(x) \iff p(a) = 0$
Entry Foundation ($Y$)Division Algorithm for polynomials: $p(x) = (x - a)q(x) + r$
Inferential Leap ($Z$)Annihilation of quotient via $x = a$, giving remainder $r = p(a)$
Exit Verification ($W$)Verifying factor status of $(x - a)$ by evaluating $p(a)$

Entry Assumptions & Prerequisites

  • Polynomial Division Algorithm: Dividend = Divisor $\times$ Quotient + Remainder.
  • Remainder degree property: $\deg(r) < \deg(\text{divisor})$. For a linear divisor, $\deg(r) < 1 \implies r$ is a constant.

Logical Construction Steps

  1. Action Set up the polynomial division equation for $p(x)$ divided by linear factor $(x - a)$.
    Why Valid Polynomial division algorithm guarantees unique quotient $q(x)$ and constant remainder $r$.
    State Output $p(x) = (x - a)q(x) + r$
  2. Action Substitute $x = a$ into the identity.
    Why Valid Setting $x = a$ annihilates the divisor: $(a - a)q(a) = 0 \cdot q(a) = 0$, leaving $p(a) = r$.
    State Output $p(a) = 0 + r \implies r = p(a)$ (Remainder Theorem)
  3. Action Establish the exact divisibility criterion ($r = 0$).
    Why Valid $(x - a)$ is a factor of $p(x)$ if and only if the remainder $r = 0$, which occurs if and only if $p(a) = 0$.
    State Output $(x - a) \mid p(x) \iff p(a) = 0$ (Factor Theorem)

Governing Relations & Domain of Validity

$$\mathbf{ p(x) = (x - a)q(x) \iff p(a) = 0 }$$

Valid when: Valid for any polynomial $p(x)$ with coefficients over a field and linear divisor $(x - a)$.

Authentic Worked Anchor

Problem: Determine whether $(x + 2)$ is a factor of $p(x) = x^3 + 3x^2 + 5x + 6$.

  1. Set the divisor to zero: $x + 2 = 0 \implies x = -2$.
  2. Compute $p(-2) = (-2)^3 + 3(-2)^2 + 5(-2) + 6$.
  3. Evaluate powers and products: $-8 + 12 - 10 + 6 = 0$.
  4. Since $p(-2) = 0$, by the Factor Theorem $(x + 2)$ is an exact factor of $p(x)$.

Verified Conclusion

$(x + 2)$ divides $x^3 + 3x^2 + 5x + 6$ with zero remainder.

Progressive Representation Interactive Staging

⚠️ Plausible Wrong Path (Misconception)

Evaluating $p(a)$ when the divisor is $(x + a)$ instead of using the root $x = -a$.

🔧 Diagnostic & Repair

Diagnostic prompt: If $(x + 4)$ divides $p(x)$, what value must evaluate to zero?

Corrective move: Find the zero of the factor: $x + 4 = 0 \implies x = -4$. Hence $p(-4) = 0$, not $p(4) = 0$.

  • 1. Check (Recall)

  • 2. Apply (Minimal Case)

  • 3. Connect (Transfer)

    How does the Factor Theorem allow decomposing degree $n$ polynomials into products of linear and irreducible quadratic factors?

Try it with less support · Observable Exit Criterion
