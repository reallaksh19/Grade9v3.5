E5.1-Q1 · Dimensional Hierarchy & Boundary Sequence

Which chain correctly moves from a three-dimensional object to a zero-dimensional boundary endpoint?

  • A. Solid → Surface → Line → Point
  • B. Solid → Line → Surface → Point
  • C. Surface → Solid → Line → Point
  • D. Line → Surface → Solid → Point
Core 1 Recovery Anchor (EU-C1-ANCHOR)
Recall the boundaries: A solid (3D) has surfaces (2D) as its boundaries; a surface has lines (1D) as its boundaries; a line has points (0D) as its boundaries.
👁️ Reveal Worked Solution & Axiomatic Justification
Deductive Justification:
1. Euclid Definition 1-3 establishes that the boundary of a solid is a surface.
2. The boundary of a surface is a line or curve.
3. The ends of a line are points.
4. Dimensions decrease strictly by one at each stage: 3D → 2D → 1D → 0D.
Correct Option: A (Solid → Surface → Line → Point).

E5.1-Q14 · Euclid's Axiom 1 (Transitivity of Equality)

If \(a + b = 15\) and \(a + c = 15\), which Euclid's axiom directly implies that \(a + b = a + c\)?

  • A. Things which are equal to the same thing are equal to one another.
  • B. If equals are added to equals, the wholes are equal.
  • C. If equals are subtracted from equals, the remainders are equal.
  • D. The whole is greater than the part.
👁️ Reveal Worked Solution & Axiomatic Justification
1. Both expressions \((a + b)\) and \((a + c)\) equal the same quantity, \(15\).
2. By Euclid's Axiom 1: "Things which are equal to the same thing are equal to one another", \((a + b) = (a + c)\).
3. Further, by Euclid's Axiom 3 ("If equals are subtracted from equals, the remainders are equal"), subtracting \(a\) from both sides yields \(b = c\).
Correct Option: A.

E5.3-Q1 · Equilateral Triangle Construction from Circle Intersections

On a given line segment \(AB\), two circles \(C_1\) with centre \(A\) and radius \(AB\), and \(C_2\) with centre \(B\) and radius \(BA\) are drawn. They intersect at point \(C\). Prove that \(\triangle ABC\) is an equilateral triangle using Euclid's Postulates and Common Notions.

Proof Strategy
Use Postulate 3 to draw circles. Radii of the same circle are equal. Then apply Common Notion 1 (transitivity) to establish all three side lengths equal.
👁️ Reveal Complete Geometric Proof
Formal Deductive Proof:
1. By Euclid's Postulate 3, a circle can be described with any centre and any radius. Circle \(C_1\) has centre \(A\) and radius \(AB\); circle \(C_2\) has centre \(B\) and radius \(BA\).
2. Since \(C\) lies on circle \(C_1\), \(AC\) and \(AB\) are both radii of circle \(C_1\). Therefore, \(AC = AB\).
3. Since \(C\) lies on circle \(C_2\), \(BC\) and \(BA\) are both radii of circle \(C_2\). Therefore, \(BC = AB\).
4. Now \(AC = AB\) and \(BC = AB\). Both \(AC\) and \(BC\) are equal to the same thing (\(AB\)).
5. By Euclid's Common Notion 1 ("Things which are equal to the same thing are equal to one another"):
   \(AC = BC\).
6. Thus, \(AB = BC = AC\).
7. By definition of an equilateral triangle, \(\triangle ABC\) is equilateral. \(\blacksquare\)

Playfair & Fifth Postulate Equivalence
E5.4-Q3 · Proof of Playfair's Parallel Axiom Equivalence

Prove that Euclid's Fifth Postulate and Playfair's Axiom ("For every line \(l\) and point \(P\) not on \(l\), there exists a unique line through \(P\) parallel to \(l\)") are logically equivalent under the first four postulates.

Equivalence Route
Prove both directions: (1) Fifth Postulate ⇒ Uniqueness in Playfair's Axiom. (2) Non-parallelism ⇒ Interior angles sum strictly less than \(180^\circ\) using exterior angle theorem.
👁️ Reveal Worked Equivalence Proof
Direction 1: Fifth Postulate &implies; Playfair's Axiom:
1. Let line \(l\) be given and \(P \notin l\). Drop perpendicular \(PQ \perp l\) and draw line \(m \perp PQ\) at \(P\). Alternate interior angles are equal (\(90^\circ\)), so interior angles on either side sum to \(90^\circ + 90^\circ = 180^\circ\).
2. By the contrapositive of Postulate 5, \(m\) never meets \(l\), so \(m \parallel l\) exists.
3. Suppose there exists a distinct second parallel line \(n \ne m\) through \(P\). Since \(n \ne m\), the angles it forms with \(PQ\) cannot both be \(90^\circ\). On one side, the interior angle must be \(< 90^\circ\).
4. The sum of interior angles on that side is \(< 90^\circ + 90^\circ = 180^\circ\).
5. By Euclid's Fifth Postulate, line \(n\) MUST intersect \(l\). This contradicts the assumption that \(n \parallel l\). Hence, the parallel line through \(P\) is unique.

Direction 2: Playfair's Axiom &implies; Fifth Postulate:
6. Suppose transversal \(t\) falls on lines \(l\) and \(m\) such that \(\angle 1 + \angle 2 < 180^\circ\).
7. Assume for contradiction that \(l\) and \(m\) never intersect (i.e. \(l \parallel m\)).
8. But we can construct through the point of transversal intersection a unique line \(l'\) making alternate interior angles equal, so \(l' \parallel m\).
9. This yields two distinct lines through \(P\) parallel to \(m\), violating Playfair's Uniqueness Axiom. Therefore, \(l\) and \(m\) must intersect on the side where the angle sum is \(< 180^\circ\). \(\blacksquare\)
