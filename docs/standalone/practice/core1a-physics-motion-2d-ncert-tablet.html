🧭 Motion in 2D Foundations (Core 1)

Understanding displacement, velocity vectors, tangential acceleration, and centripetal force in planar motion.

Position Vector:       \(\vec{r}(t) = x(t)\,\hat{i} + y(t)\,\hat{j}\)
Displacement:           \(\Delta\vec{r} = (x_2 - x_1)\,\hat{i} + (y_2 - y_1)\,\hat{j}\)
Average Velocity:       \(\vec{v}_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\Delta\vec{r}}{\Delta t}\)
Instantaneous Velocity: \(\vec{v} = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} = \frac{dx}{dt}\,\hat{i} + \frac{dy}{dt}\,\hat{j}\)
Concept Unit 01

01 Curved path: distance is not displacement

The path can bend; displacement still joins the two positions directly

U8-Q1 + Sample-I-Q10

What is happening?

A particle can travel along a curved path while its displacement is a single straight vector from its starting position to its ending position.

Key Idea

For half a circle of radius r, the start and end points are opposite ends of a diameter. The path length is πr, but the displacement magnitude is 2r.

How It Works

  1. Mark only the initial and final positions first.
  2. Draw the direct arrow from initial to final position; that arrow is displacement.
  3. Use the actual travelled path only when finding distance.
  4. A closed trip can have non-zero distance and zero displacement.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
A runner moves from the rightmost point of a circular track to the leftmost point along the upper semicircle. Distance = πr; displacement magnitude = 2r, directed across the diameter.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not use arc length as displacement. Curved distance and straight displacement answer different questions.

✅ Quick Check

A particle completes one full circle. What is its displacement magnitude?

Reveal Answer
0.
Concept Unit 02

02 Constant speed can still mean acceleration

Velocity changes whenever direction changes

U8-Q6

What is happening?

On a merry-go-round, the rider can keep the same speed while the direction of motion changes continuously.

Key Idea

Velocity is a vector. Constant magnitude with changing direction means changing velocity, so acceleration is non-zero.

How It Works

  1. At two nearby points, draw the velocity tangent to the path.
  2. Compare the two velocity arrows: equal length, different direction.
  3. A change in velocity means acceleration.
  4. For circular motion, the acceleration points inward toward the centre.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
A rider moving at 10 m/s around a circle has constant speed, but not uniform velocity. The velocity turns continuously, so the rider is accelerated.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not equate “constant speed” with “zero acceleration”. That is true only when the velocity vector is constant.

✅ Quick Check

Can an object accelerate while its speed stays constant?

Reveal Answer
Yes - if the direction of velocity changes.
Concept Unit 03

03 When are distance and displacement equal?

Equality requires the travelled path to match the straight separation

U8-Q11

What is happening?

Distance measures the full path travelled. Displacement magnitude measures only the straight separation between start and finish.

Key Idea

Distance equals |displacement| only when the object moves along the straight line from start to finish without reversing direction.

How It Works

  1. Sketch the actual path.
  2. Join start to finish with a straight displacement arrow.
  3. Compare path length with the direct separation.
  4. Any detour, curve or reversal makes distance greater than |displacement|.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
A car travelling 80 m east along a straight road with no reversal has distance 80 m and displacement magnitude 80 m. If it detours around a bend, the distance becomes larger.

⚠️ Watch Out

“Straight road” alone is not enough if the object reverses direction. The no-reversal condition matters.

✅ Quick Check

A runner goes 20 m east then 5 m west. Are distance and displacement magnitude equal?

Reveal Answer
No. Distance = 25 m; |displacement| = 15 m.
Concept Unit 04

04 Release from circular motion: follow the tangent

The inward force turns the velocity; it is not the velocity direction

U10-Q4

What is happening?

Before the string breaks, tension supplies the inward acceleration needed to bend the stone’s path. At the instant of breaking, the stone already has a tangential velocity.

Key Idea

When the inward constraint disappears, the stone does not move toward or away from the centre first. Its immediate motion follows the tangent.

How It Works

  1. Mark the release point on the circle.
  2. Draw the radius to that point.
  3. Draw the instantaneous velocity perpendicular to the radius.
  4. Remove the inward force; keep the existing velocity state.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
At the rightmost point of a counter-clockwise circular path, the instantaneous velocity points upward. If the string breaks there, the stone initially moves upward along the tangent.

⚠️ Watch Out

Radially outward is not the release direction in an inertial frame. The object keeps its instantaneous tangential velocity.

✅ Quick Check

At the top of a clockwise circle, which way is the tangent velocity?

Reveal Answer
To the right.
Concept Unit 05

05 Gravity can be the centripetal force

Orbiting means continuous inward turning under gravity

U10-Q16

What is happening?

A planet has tangential motion, while the Sun’s gravitational pull continuously bends that motion inward.

Key Idea

The force that provides the centripetal acceleration is gravity. Its magnitude depends on the two masses and their separation.

How It Works

  1. Choose the planet as the body.
  2. Draw its velocity tangent to the orbit.
  3. Draw gravitational force toward the Sun.
  4. For a circular-orbit model, the inward gravitational force supplies the centripetal requirement.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
For masses M and m separated by r, the gravitational force has magnitude F = GMm/r2. That inward force changes the direction of the planet’s velocity.

⚠️ Watch Out

“Centripetal force” is not an extra force. It names the net inward force role; here gravity fills that role.

✅ Quick Check

What happens to gravitational force if the separation doubles while masses stay fixed?

Reveal Answer
It becomes one quarter as large.
Concept Unit 06

06 Horizontal launch and drop share the same vertical motion

Horizontal motion does not delay gravitational fall in the ideal model

U10-Q17

What is happening?

Two stones start at the same height. One is dropped; the other is given only horizontal velocity. Their horizontal states differ, but their initial vertical states are the same.

Key Idea

Ignoring air resistance, both have initial vertical velocity zero and the same downward acceleration g. Therefore their vertical positions match at every equal time.

How It Works

  1. Separate the state into horizontal and vertical components.
  2. Vertical: both start with vy0=0 and acceleration g downward.
  3. Horizontal: the thrown stone moves sideways; the dropped stone does not.
  4. The ground is reached when the common vertical position reaches zero.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
From height h, both obey the same vertical relation y(t) = h - ½gt2. Their horizontal coordinates differ, but their landing time is the same.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not add horizontal speed to the vertical falling speed. Perpendicular components evolve independently in this model.

✅ Quick Check

If the horizontal launch speed doubles, does the ideal landing time change?

Reveal Answer
No. The vertical initial state and g are unchanged.
Concept Unit 07

07 If gravity vanished: Moon follows the tangent

Without inward acceleration, instantaneous velocity persists

U10-Q18

What is happening?

The Moon’s velocity is tangential to its orbit. Earth’s gravity continuously changes the direction of that velocity.

Key Idea

If Earth’s gravity suddenly became zero and no other force acted, there would be no inward acceleration. The Moon would continue in the direction of its instantaneous tangential velocity.

How It Works

  1. Freeze the orbital state at one instant.
  2. Keep the current velocity vector.
  3. Remove the inward gravitational force and acceleration.
  4. With no net force, the velocity remains constant: straight-line tangent motion.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
At a point where the Moon’s velocity points northeast, removing Earth’s gravity makes the Moon initially continue northeast along the tangent line.

⚠️ Watch Out

The Moon would not shoot radially outward. Removing the inward force does not rotate the velocity vector.

✅ Quick Check

What changes immediately when gravity is removed: the Moon’s velocity or its acceleration?

Reveal Answer
Its acceleration changes immediately; its velocity is initially unchanged.
Concept Unit 08

08 Why Earth does not simply fall into the Sun

Tangential motion plus inward gravity produces an orbit

U10-Q22

What is happening?

The Sun pulls Earth inward, but Earth also has substantial tangential velocity. Gravity continually bends that motion rather than stopping it.

Key Idea

An orbit can be viewed as continuous free fall in which the body keeps missing the central object because its tangential motion carries it sideways while gravity turns the velocity inward.

How It Works

  1. Draw Earth’s instantaneous velocity tangent to the orbit.
  2. Draw gravitational acceleration toward the Sun.
  3. Over a short time, gravity changes the direction of velocity.
  4. Repeating this inward turning generates the curved orbital path.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
In an ideal circular model, inward acceleration has magnitude v2/r. Gravity supplies that inward acceleration, so the trajectory curves around the Sun.

⚠️ Watch Out

Do not describe the orbit as “gravity balanced by a centrifugal force” in the inertial-frame picture used here.

✅ Quick Check

If the tangential velocity vanished while gravity remained, would the orbital picture persist?

Reveal Answer
No. The body would begin falling more directly inward.
Concept Unit 09

09 A tossed coin can reveal train acceleration

Compare the coin’s inertial horizontal motion with the train’s changing motion

U9-Q5 (transfer)

What is happening?

At release, the coin shares the train’s horizontal velocity. While airborne, it approximately keeps that horizontal velocity, but an accelerating train can move ahead beneath it.

Key Idea

If the coin lands behind the passenger, the train has gained forward speed after release. That indicates forward acceleration of the train.

How It Works

  1. Use the ground as an inertial reference for the comparison.
  2. At release: coin and train share horizontal velocity v0.
  3. Airborne coin: horizontal acceleration is approximately zero.
  4. Train: positive horizontal acceleration makes its position advance faster, so the coin appears to fall
  5. behind inside the train.
⚡ WORKED EXAMPLE
Over time t, a simple model gives xcoin=v0t while xtrain=v0t+½at2. For a>0, the train moves farther forward.

⚠️ Watch Out

This is a reference-frame question. “Behind the passenger” does not mean the coin accelerated backward in the ground frame.

✅ Quick Check

If the train moved at constant velocity, where would an ideal vertically tossed coin return relative to the passenger?

Reveal Answer
Back to the passenger (ignoring air effects).
