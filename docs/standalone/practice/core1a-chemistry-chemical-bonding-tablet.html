BOND-MOD-01

Lewis Octet Rule & Formal Charge Calculation

Unit 1 · Concept Core
🎯 Learning Target: Draw valid electron-dot representations and calculate atom-specific formal charges.
🧠 Memory Anchor
Formal Charge FC = V - L - ½B (Valence e⁻ - Lone pair e⁻ - ½ Bonding e⁻). Net sum equals molecular charge.
⚙️ Why It Works
Atoms share valence electrons to attain stable inert-gas noble octets. Formal charge assesses stability: lowest formal charges and negative charges on most electronegative atoms represent the major resonance contributor.
🔍 Worked Contrast
Flawed: Distributing electrons randomly to make all atoms neutral. Correct: In O₃, central O has FC = +1, single-bonded O has FC = -1, double-bonded O has FC = 0.
✍️ Active Practice & Step-by-Step Working

Problem: Calculate the formal charge on each oxygen atom in the carbonate ion (CO₃²⁻).

Reveal Checked Model Answer
Carbon has 4 valence electrons and forms 1 double bond and 2 single bonds (no lone pairs, FC = 4 - 0 - 4 = 0). The double-bonded oxygen has 2 lone pairs and 2 bonds: FC = 6 - 4 - 2 = 0. Each single-bonded oxygen has 3 lone pairs and 1 bond: FC = 6 - 6 - 1 = -1. Total charge = 0 + 0 + (-1) + (-1) = -2.
⚠️ Common Trap to Avoid
Hypervalent exceptions: Elements of period 3 and beyond (P, S, Cl) possess vacant d-orbitals and can expand octets (e.g. SF₆ has 12 electrons around S).

BOND-MOD-02

VSEPR Theory & Molecular Geometry

Unit 2 · Concept Core
🎯 Learning Target: Predict 3D shapes from steric numbers (bond pairs + lone pairs) accounting for repulsive orders.
🧠 Memory Anchor
Repulsion hierarchy: Lone Pair–Lone Pair > Lone Pair–Bond Pair > Bond Pair–Bond Pair. Lone pairs compress ideal bond angles.
⚙️ Why It Works
Valence shell electron pairs repel each other electrostatically and orient in 3D space to maximize inter-pair distances and minimize repulsive potential energy.
🔍 Worked Contrast
CH₄ (4 bp, 0 lp): 109.5° tetrahedral. NH₃ (3 bp, 1 lp): 107° trigonal pyramidal. H₂O (2 bp, 2 lp): 104.5° bent. Each lone pair compresses bond angles by ~2–2.5°.

✍️ Active Practice & Step-by-Step Working

Problem: Determine the steric number, electron geometry, and molecular shape of SF₄ and ClF₃.

Reveal Checked Model Answer
SF₄: S has 6 valence e⁻, 4 bonding pairs + 1 lone pair = steric 5 (Trigonal Bipyramidal). Lone pair occupies equatorial position to minimize 90° repulsions => See-Saw shape. ClF₃: Cl has 7 valence e⁻, 3 bp + 2 lp = steric 5. Two lone pairs in equatorial positions => T-shaped.
⚠️ Common Trap to Avoid
Confusing Electron Geometry with Molecular Shape. In XeF₄, electron geometry is octahedral (steric 6: 4 bp + 2 lp), but molecular shape is Square Planar!

BOND-MOD-03

Hybridization & Bent's Rule (PCl₅ Axial Elongation)

Unit 3 · Concept Core
🎯 Learning Target: Explain directional bonding, inter-orbital mixing, and unequal axial vs equatorial bonds.
🧠 Memory Anchor
Steric 2: sp (180°). Steric 3: sp² (120°). Steric 4: sp³ (109.5°). Steric 5: sp³d (90° & 120°). Steric 6: sp³d² (90°).
⚙️ Why It Works
In sp³d (trigonal bipyramidal, e.g. PCl₅), hybrid orbitals are split into equatorial (sp², 120°) and axial (p-d hybrid, 90°). Axial bonds experience three 90° repulsions while equatorial experience only two 90° repulsions; thus axial bonds are longer and weaker.
🔍 Worked Contrast
In solid state: 2PCl₅(s) dissociates into [PCl₄]⁺ (tetrahedral, sp³) and [PCl₆]⁻ (octahedral, sp³d²).
✍️ Active Practice & Step-by-Step Working

Problem: Explain why PCl₅ decomposes easily upon heating to PCl₃ and Cl₂, whereas SF₆ is exceptionally inert.

Reveal Checked Model Answer
In PCl₅, the two axial bonds are significantly longer, weaker, and more strained due to three 90° repulsions each. Gentle heating breaks these two axial bonds yielding PCl₃ and Cl₂. In SF₆, all 6 S-F bonds are identical, octahedral, with no lone-pair strain and steric crowding preventing nucleophilic attack.
⚠️ Common Trap to Avoid
Assuming all P-Cl bonds in PCl₅ are equivalent. Equatorial = 202 pm, Axial = 240 pm (axial bonds break first upon heating).

BOND-MOD-04

Dipole Moments & Vectorial Symmetry

Unit 4 · Concept Core
🎯 Learning Target: Sum bond dipoles vectorially to predict net molecular dipole moment (μ = q × d).
🧠 Memory Anchor
Symmetric molecules have μ = 0 (non-polar) even with polar bonds. Trans-isomers and regular geometries cancel vectors.
⚙️ Why It Works
Dipole moment is a vector quantity pointing from electropositive to electronegative atom. In regular planar/linear geometries, bond dipoles cancel by symmetry (e.g. CO₂, BF₃, CCl₄).
🔍 Worked Contrast
NH₃ vs NF₃: In NH₃, bond dipoles (H→N) reinforce lone pair dipole => μ = 1.47 D (large). In NF₃, bond dipoles (N→F) oppose lone pair dipole => μ = 0.24 D (very small).
✍️ Active Practice & Step-by-Step Working

Problem: Which has a higher dipole moment: cis-1,2-dichloroethene or trans-1,2-dichloroethene? Justify with vector diagrams.

Reveal Checked Model Answer
Cis-1,2-dichloroethene has both C-Cl dipoles pointing in the same direction, reinforcing each other (μ > 0). Trans-1,2-dichloroethene has equal C-Cl dipoles pointing in exactly opposite directions (180°), canceling out completely (μ = 0).
⚠️ Common Trap to Avoid
Assuming BF₃ and NH₃ have similar polarity because both have 3 terminal atoms. BF₃ is trigonal planar (μ = 0), NH₃ is pyramidal (μ ≠ 0).

BOND-MOD-05

Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT) & Magnetism

Unit 5 · Concept Core
🎯 Learning Target: Construct MO diagrams, calculate bond order (BO = ½(Nb - Na)), and deduce magnetic behaviour.
🧠 Memory Anchor
≤ 14 e⁻ (B₂, C₂, N₂): π2px = π2py < σ2pz. > 14 e⁻ (O₂, F₂): σ2pz < π2px = π2py. Unpaired e⁻ = Paramagnetic; all paired = Diamagnetic.
⚙️ Why It Works
Atomic orbitals overlap constructively to form bonding MOs (lower energy, electron density between nuclei) and destructively to form antibonding MOs (higher energy, nodal plane).
🔍 Worked Contrast
O₂ has 16 electrons. Highest occupied MOs are π*2px¹ and π*2py¹ (two unpaired electrons). Hence O₂ is PARAMAGNETIC (VBT incorrectly predicted diamagnetic!).

✍️ Active Practice & Step-by-Step Working

Problem: Compare bond order, bond length, and magnetism for O₂²⁺, O₂, O₂⁻, and O₂²⁻.

Reveal Checked Model Answer
O₂²⁺ (14 e⁻): BO = 3.0, Diamagnetic, Shortest bond length. O₂ (16 e⁻): BO = 2.0, Paramagnetic (2 unpaired e⁻). O₂⁻ (17 e⁻): BO = 1.5, Paramagnetic (1 unpaired e⁻). O₂²⁻ (18 e⁻): BO = 1.0, Diamagnetic, Longest bond length. Bond length order: O₂²⁺ < O₂ < O₂⁻ < O₂²⁻.
⚠️ Common Trap to Avoid
Bond order formula shortcut: 14 e⁻ (N₂) = 3.0. Each electron added or subtracted changes BO by 0.5. (e.g. 15 e⁻ = 2.5, 16 e⁻ = 2.0, 17 e⁻ = 1.5, 18 e⁻ = 1.0).

BOND-MOD-06

Hydrogen Bonding & Anomalous Properties

Unit 6 · Concept Core
🎯 Learning Target: Differentiate intermolecular vs intramolecular H-bonding and explain boiling point anomalies.
🧠 Memory Anchor
H-bond forms only when H is covalently bound to small, highly electronegative atoms: F, O, N.
⚙️ Why It Works
Extreme electronegativity difference strips electron density from tiny H nucleus, creating concentrated positive charge attracting lone pairs on adjacent F/O/N atoms.
🔍 Worked Contrast
Intermolecular (e.g. p-nitrophenol, H₂O): Associates molecules into chains/clusters => elevated boiling point. Intramolecular (e.g. o-nitrophenol, salicylic acid): Chelates within single molecule => lower boiling point, higher steam volatility.
✍️ Active Practice & Step-by-Step Working

Problem: Why is o-nitrophenol steam volatile while p-nitrophenol is not?

Reveal Checked Model Answer
o-Nitrophenol forms intramolecular hydrogen bonding (chelation between -OH and -NO₂ on adjacent carbons), preventing association with neighbouring molecules. p-Nitrophenol forms intermolecular hydrogen bonding, linking molecules into extensive associated clusters with much higher boiling points and low volatility.
⚠️ Common Trap to Avoid
Water vs HF: HF has stronger individual H-bonds (F > O electronegativity), but H₂O forms 4 H-bonds per molecule forming a 3D network, giving H₂O higher boiling point (100°C vs 19.5°C).

BOND-Q01 · Molecular Orbital Theory

JEE Main 2026 (Online) 8th April Morning Shift

ExamSIDE PYQ

According to Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT), which of the following oxygen species is diamagnetic and has the shortest bond length?

  • (A) O₂²⁺
  • (B) O₂
  • (C) O₂⁻
  • (D) O₂²⁻
✍️ Your Working & Selected Option
Reveal Correct Option & Step-by-Step Derivation
Correct Answer: O₂²⁺

Step-by-Step Derivation:
1. Count total electrons: - O₂²⁺: 16 - 2 = 14 electrons (isoelectronic with N₂). BO = (10 - 4)/2 = 3.0. All electrons paired -> DIAMAGNETIC. - O₂: 16 electrons. BO = (10 - 6)/2 = 2.0. Two unpaired in π*2p -> Paramagnetic. - O₂⁻: 17 electrons. BO = 1.5. Paramagnetic. - O₂²⁻: 18 electrons. BO = 1.0. Diamagnetic. 2. Higher Bond Order corresponds to higher bond strength and SHORTEST bond length. 3. Therefore O₂²⁺ has both highest bond order (3.0, shortest bond) and zero unpaired electrons (diamagnetic).

BOND-Q02 · VSEPR & Molecular Geometry

JEE Main 2025 (Online) 1st Feb Evening Shift

ExamSIDE PYQ

The total number of lone pairs on the central atom in XeF₄, XeO₃, and XeF₂ respectively are:

  • (A) 2, 1, 3
  • (B) 2, 2, 2
  • (C) 1, 1, 3
  • (D) 2, 1, 2
✍️ Your Working & Selected Option
Reveal Correct Option & Step-by-Step Derivation
Correct Answer: 2, 1, 3

Step-by-Step Derivation:
1. Xe has 8 valence electrons. - XeF₄: 4 bond pairs with F. Remaining electrons = 8 - 4 = 4 (2 lone pairs). Steric number = 6 (Square planar). - XeO₃: 3 double bonds with O (uses 6 electrons). Remaining = 8 - 6 = 2 (1 lone pair). Steric number = 4 (Pyramidal). - XeF₂: 2 bond pairs with F. Remaining = 8 - 2 = 6 (3 lone pairs). Steric number = 5 (Linear). 2. Lone pairs are: 2, 1, 3.

BOND-Q03 · Dipole Moment

JEE Main 2024 (Online) 27th Jan Shift 1

ExamSIDE PYQ

Which of the following molecules has a non-zero dipole moment (μ ≠ 0)?

  • (A) NF₃
  • (B) BF₃
  • (C) BeF₂
  • (D) CCl₄
✍️ Your Working & Selected Option
Reveal Correct Option & Step-by-Step Derivation
Correct Answer: NF₃

Step-by-Step Derivation:
1. BF₃ is trigonal planar (120°): 3 equal B-F bond dipoles cancel vectorially (μ = 0). 2. BeF₂ is linear (180°): 2 opposite Be-F dipoles cancel (μ = 0). 3. CCl₄ is regular tetrahedral: 4 equal C-Cl dipoles cancel vectorially (μ = 0). 4. NF₃ is trigonal pyramidal with a lone pair: bond dipoles do not cancel, yielding a net non-zero dipole moment (μ = 0.24 D).

BOND-Q04 · Hybridization & Axial Bonds

JEE Main 2023 (Online) 11th April Shift 1

ExamSIDE PYQ

In PCl₅ molecule, which statement regarding the P-Cl bonds is correct?

  • (A) All five P-Cl bonds are of equal length and strength.
  • (B) Axial bonds are longer and weaker than equatorial bonds.
  • (C) Equatorial bonds are longer than axial bonds.
  • (D) Axial bonds experience fewer 90° repulsions than equatorial bonds.
✍️ Your Working & Selected Option
Reveal Correct Option & Step-by-Step Derivation
Correct Answer: Axial bonds are longer and weaker than equatorial bonds.

Step-by-Step Derivation:
1. PCl₅ has trigonal bipyramidal geometry (sp³d hybridization). 2. Three equatorial bonds lie in one plane with 120° angles. 3. Two axial bonds lie above and below at 90° to the equatorial plane. 4. Each axial bond experiences three 90° lone/bond pair repulsions, while each equatorial bond experiences only two 90° repulsions. 5. To minimize repulsive strain, axial bonds elongate to 240 pm (vs 202 pm equatorial), making them weaker.
