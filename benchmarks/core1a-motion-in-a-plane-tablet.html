From One Direction to Two

D1 FOUNDATION CU-PHY-KIN-2D-01
⚡ Inferential Leap / Pivot
Two-dimensional motion in a plane can be solved as two simultaneous, independent one-dimensional motions that share the same clock.
🎯 Model Contract & Assumptions
Understanding of 1D displacement, velocity, and basic Cartesian (x, y) coordinates.
🛠️ Stepwise Construction (Action → Physical License → Output)
1
Declare one fixed x-y coordinate frame and common time origin t=0.
Why physically valid: Component magnitudes and signs are physically meaningful only with respect to a declared coordinate system.
State output: Declared frame with perpendicular x and y axes.
2
Resolve position and velocity vectors into Cartesian components.
Why physically valid: Any plane vector is uniquely and completely specified by perpendicular components: r = (x, y) and v = (vx, vy).
State output: Vector state: r = x î + y ĵ and v = vx î + vy ĵ.
3
Apply the respective 1D kinematic laws to x and y independently.
Why physically valid: Perpendicular axes have zero projection on each other; forces in y do not produce acceleration in x.
State output: Two separate equations coupled solely by time t.
4
Recombine x and y coordinates at the identical instant t.
Why physically valid: Mixing x at one time with y at another time describes no physical state of the moving object.
State output: One physically valid plane state r(t) = (x(t), y(t)).
📐 Mathematical Definition & Validity
r(t) = x(t) î + y(t) ĵ | v(t) = vx(t) î + vy(t) ĵ | |v| = √(vx² + vy²)
Validity Scope: Valid whenever coordinate axes are chosen strictly perpendicular (orthogonal Cartesian frame).
🔍 Worked Conceptual Anchor
Problem: A particle moves with vx = 6 m/s and vy = 8 m/s at t = 3 s. What makes these components one physical state?
  • Check that both components are measured with respect to the same declared coordinate axes.
  • Verify that both components belong to the exact same instant of time t = 3 s.
  • Combine them by vector addition: |v| = √(6² + 8²) = 10 m/s at angle θ = arctan(8/6) ≈ 53.1°.
Result: The velocity vector is v = (6, 8) m/s with speed 10 m/s at one shared physical instant.
👁️ Staged Visual Anchor
Declare perpendicular x and y axes with origin O.
⚠️ Easy Mistake to Make
Mixing horizontal speed into vertical equations or treating the components as having separate clocks.
Diagnostic: If a ball moves sideways at 20 m/s for 2 seconds, can its vertical fall calculation use t = 1.5 seconds?
Repair: No. Independence separates the axis equations, but both axes belong to one object and share one common clock.

What Makes a Projectile Special?

D1 FOUNDATION CU-PHY-KIN-2D-02
⚡ Inferential Leap / Pivot
After release, an ideal projectile has NO forward or pushing force; gravity is the ONLY continuing force acting on it.
🎯 Model Contract & Assumptions
Newton's first law (inertia) and free fall acceleration under gravity.
🛠️ Stepwise Construction (Action → Physical License → Output)
1
Identify the release boundary: launching agent ceases contact.
Why physically valid: Once released, no mechanical contact force acts on the object.
State output: Free flight condition licensed.
2
Set horizontal acceleration ax = 0 in the absence of air drag.
Why physically valid: Gravity acts strictly vertically downward; no horizontal force exists.
State output: ax = 0 → vx = constant.
3
Set vertical acceleration ay = -g (taking +y upward).
Why physically valid: Earth's gravitational force near the surface produces constant downward acceleration.
State output: ay = -g → vy(t) = uy - gt.
📐 Mathematical Definition & Validity
ax = 0 | ay = -g | F_net = m·g (downward only)
Validity Scope: Near-Earth surface; neglect aerodynamic drag and Earth's curvature.
🔍 Worked Conceptual Anchor
Problem: A cannonball travels through the air after being fired. What forces continue to act on it during flight?
  • Check contact forces: none, since it has left the cannon barrel.
  • Check field forces: Earth exerts a downward gravitational force W = mg.
  • Conclude: There is no 'force of the throw' keeping it moving forward; inertia maintains its horizontal speed.
Result: Only gravity acts downward throughout the flight.
👁️ Staged Visual Anchor
Launched at velocity u; initial contact ends.
⚠️ Easy Mistake to Make
Believing a forward force travels with the projectile to keep it moving.
Diagnostic: At the peak of its flight, is there a forward force pushing the projectile?
Repair: No. By Newton's First Law, an object in motion continues in motion with constant velocity unless acted upon by a net force. ax = 0 means horizontal velocity persists naturally.

Show Verified Model Answer

Meet the Parts of a Projectile Flight

D1 FOUNDATION CU-PHY-KIN-2D-03
⚡ Inferential Leap / Pivot
A diagonal launch velocity u at angle θ is completely characterized by horizontal speed ux = u cos θ and initial vertical speed uy = u sin θ.
🎯 Model Contract & Assumptions
Trigonometric resolution of a vector into sine and cosine components.
🛠️ Stepwise Construction (Action → Physical License → Output)
1
Resolve initial velocity vector u into Cartesian components.
Why physically valid: Right-triangle trigonometry gives the projections on the x and y axes.
State output: ux = u cos θ, uy = u sin θ.
2
Define Point of Projection O, Launch Angle θ, and Apex H.
Why physically valid: Apex is the highest point where vertical velocity momentarily stops.
State output: Apex condition: vy = 0.
3
Define Horizontal Range R and Total Time of Flight T.
Why physically valid: Landing event B marks the return to ground level or target height.
State output: R = x(T) = ux · T.
📐 Mathematical Definition & Validity
ux = u cos θ | uy = u sin θ | tan θ = uy / ux
Validity Scope: θ is measured relative to the horizontal axis.
🔍 Worked Conceptual Anchor
Problem: A football is kicked at u = 20 m/s at an angle θ = 30° above horizontal. Find its initial component velocities.
  • Horizontal component: ux = 20 · cos(30°) = 20 · (√3/2) ≈ 17.32 m/s.
  • Vertical component: uy = 20 · sin(30°) = 20 · (0.5) = 10.0 m/s.
  • Verify: √(17.32² + 10²) = √(300 + 100) = √400 = 20 m/s.
Result: ux ≈ 17.32 m/s and uy = 10.0 m/s.
👁️ Staged Visual Anchor
Velocity vector u at angle θ from origin O.
⚠️ Easy Mistake to Make
Using sin for the horizontal component or cos for the vertical component.
Diagnostic: If θ = 0° (horizontal launch), what should uy be?
Repair: For horizontal launch, uy = u sin(0°) = 0. Therefore sin θ belongs with the vertical component when θ is measured from horizontal.

One Clock, Two Directions

D2 THINK CAREFULLY CU-PHY-KIN-2D-04
⚡ Inferential Leap / Pivot
Horizontal motion has zero acceleration; vertical motion has constant downward acceleration -g. The shared clock t connects the two independent equations.
🎯 Model Contract & Assumptions
1D kinematic relations v = u + at and s = ut + ½at².
🛠️ Stepwise Construction (Action → Physical License → Output)
1
Write the horizontal position and velocity equations.
Why physically valid: ax = 0 means velocity is constant over all intervals.
State output: vx(t) = ux and x(t) = ux · t.
2
Write the vertical position and velocity equations.
Why physically valid: ay = -g means vertical motion is constant acceleration free fall.
State output: vy(t) = uy - gt and y(t) = uy·t - ½gt².
3
Solve for the event time from one axis and substitute into the other.
Why physically valid: An event (e.g. landing, reaching a wall) happens at one specific clock reading t for both coordinates.
State output: Linked state (x(t), y(t)) at time t.
📐 Mathematical Definition & Validity
x(t) = (u cos θ) t | y(t) = (u sin θ) t - ½ g t² | vy(t) = u sin θ - g t
Validity Scope: Constant acceleration kinematics; valid for all time t during flight.
🔍 Worked Conceptual Anchor
Problem: A projectile is launched with ux = 15 m/s and uy = 20 m/s. Find its position and velocity after t = 1.0 s (take g = 10 m/s²).
  • Horizontal position: x = 15 × 1.0 = 15 m; Horizontal velocity: vx = 15 m/s.
  • Vertical position: y = 20(1.0) - 0.5(10)(1.0)² = 20 - 5 = 15 m.
  • Vertical velocity: vy = 20 - 10(1.0) = 10 m/s.
  • Resultant speed: v = √(15² + 10²) = √(225 + 100) = √325 ≈ 18.03 m/s.
Result: Position is (15 m, 15 m) and velocity is (15 m/s, 10 m/s) with speed 18.03 m/s.
👁️ Staged Visual Anchor
One launch vector u resolved into ux and uy.
⚠️ Easy Mistake to Make
Using g in the horizontal equation or mixing vx into the vertical equation.
Diagnostic: If g = 9.8 m/s², does horizontal velocity decrease over time in vacuum?
Repair: No. Gravity has no horizontal component. Keep x quantities strictly in x equations and y quantities strictly in y equations.

At the Top, What Actually Becomes Zero?

D2 THINK CAREFULLY CU-PHY-KIN-2D-05
⚡ Inferential Leap / Pivot
At the apex, only the VERTICAL velocity component vy is momentarily zero. The horizontal velocity vx remains equal to u cos θ, and acceleration is STILL g downward.
🎯 Model Contract & Assumptions
Understanding turning points in 1D motion where velocity crosses zero.
🛠️ Stepwise Construction (Action → Physical License → Output)
1
Identify the apex condition: vertical motion reaches its upper turning point.
Why physically valid: Before the apex vy > 0 (rising); after the apex vy < 0 (falling); at the transition vy = 0.
State output: vy = 0 at t = t_apex.
2
Retain the constant horizontal velocity component vx = u cos θ.
Why physically valid: Zero vertical velocity does not affect horizontal motion.
State output: v_apex = (ux, 0) = (u cos θ, 0).
3
Check acceleration at the apex: a = (0, -g).
Why physically valid: Gravity does not turn off at the top; if acceleration were zero, the ball would float forever!
State output: a_apex = -g ĵ (downward).
📐 Mathematical Definition & Validity
vy(t_apex) = 0 | t_apex = (u sin θ) / g | v_apex = u cos θ î | a_apex = -g ĵ
Validity Scope: Holds at the exact peak of any ideal projectile trajectory.
🔍 Worked Conceptual Anchor
Problem: A cannonball is launched with speed 50 m/s at 37° (ux = 40 m/s, uy = 30 m/s). What is its velocity and acceleration at the apex? (g = 10 m/s²)
  • At the apex, set vy = 0.
  • Horizontal velocity is unchanged: vx = ux = 40 m/s.
  • Resultant velocity vector: v = 40 î + 0 ĵ m/s (speed is 40 m/s, pointing purely horizontally).
  • Acceleration vector: a = -10 ĵ m/s² (pointing purely downward).
Result: Speed at top is 40 m/s (horizontal) and acceleration is 10 m/s² downward.
👁️ Staged Visual Anchor
vy > 0, vx = ux, speed decreasing.
⚠️ Easy Mistake to Make
Claiming that velocity is zero or acceleration is zero at the highest point.
Diagnostic: If both velocity and acceleration were zero at the top, what would the projectile do?
Repair: It would remain stationary in mid-air! Only the vertical component of velocity is zero; speed equals u cos θ, and gravity continues pulling downward at g.

Same-Height Flight: Time, Range and Matching Angles

D2 THINK CAREFULLY CU-PHY-KIN-2D-06
⚡ Inferential Leap / Pivot
Because ascent and descent under constant g are symmetric when landing height equals launch height, total time T = 2 t_apex. Furthermore, complementary angles (θ and 90° - θ) produce identical range.
🎯 Model Contract & Assumptions
Level ground launch and landing condition (Δy = 0).
🛠️ Stepwise Construction (Action → Physical License → Output)
1
Impose the same-height landing condition: y(T) = 0.
Why physically valid: Vertical displacement over the complete flight is zero.
State output: uy T - ½ g T² = 0 → T = 2 uy / g = 2u sin θ / g.
2
Calculate Horizontal Range R = ux · T.
Why physically valid: Horizontal velocity ux is constant during the entire flight time T.
State output: R = (u cos θ)(2u sin θ / g) = u² (2 sin θ cos θ) / g = u² sin(2θ) / g.
3
Recognize the complementary angle property: sin(2(90° - θ)) = sin(180° - 2θ) = sin(2θ).
Why physically valid: The trigonometric identity shows that complementary angles yield identical range for the same launch speed.
State output: R(θ) = R(90° - θ). Maximum range occurs at θ = 45°.
📐 Mathematical Definition & Validity
T = 2u sin θ / g | H = u² sin²θ / (2g) | R = u² sin(2θ) / g | R_max = u² / g (at 45°)
Validity Scope: STRICTLY CONDITIONAL on launch and landing at the same vertical height (Δy = 0). Do NOT use for cliffs or elevated targets!
🔍 Worked Conceptual Anchor
Problem: Source Worked Examples (P8): Ball launched at u = 12.0 m/s at 45° (g = 10 m/s²). Also find the angle where R = H.
  • Direct Range: R = (12.0)² sin(90°) / 10 = 144 / 10 = 14.4 m.
  • Condition R = H: u² sin(2θ)/g = u² sin²θ/(2g) → 2 sin θ cos θ = sin²θ / 2.
  • Cancel sin θ (for θ ≠ 0): 2 cos θ = sin θ / 2 → tan θ = 4.
  • Angle: θ = arctan(4) ≈ 75.96° (about 76°).
Result: Direct range is 14.4 m. Range equals maximum height when tan θ = 4 (θ ≈ 76°).
👁️ Staged Visual Anchor
Low trajectory: shorter flight time, flat arc, reaches range R.
⚠️ Easy Mistake to Make
Applying R = u² sin(2θ)/g to projectiles launched from cliffs or landing at different elevations.
Diagnostic: A ball thrown horizontally from a 20 m cliff lands on the ground. Can you find its range with u² sin(2θ)/g?
Repair: No! For a cliff, θ = 0° which would give R = 0, which is absurd. Use the vertical position equation y = -h to find time first.

Describe the Path Without Using Time

D3 CHALLENGE CU-PHY-KIN-2D-07
⚡ Inferential Leap / Pivot
Solving x(t) for t and substituting into y(t) eliminates the clock variable, proving that the geometric trajectory y(x) is an inverted parabola.
🎯 Model Contract & Assumptions
Algebraic substitution to eliminate a common parameter between two equations.
🛠️ Stepwise Construction (Action → Physical License → Output)
1
Express time from the horizontal coordinate: t = x / (u cos θ).
Why physically valid: Since ax = 0, the relationship between x and t is strictly linear.
State output: t = x / (u cos θ).
2
Substitute t into the vertical position equation: y = (u sin θ)t - ½gt².
Why physically valid: Both equations refer to the same projectile at the same physical instant.
State output: y = (u sin θ)(x / u cos θ) - ½g(x / u cos θ)².
3
Simplify trigonometric ratios to obtain the canonical trajectory equation.
Why physically valid: sin θ / cos θ = tan θ and 1 / cos²θ = sec²θ = 1 + tan²θ.
State output: y = x tan θ - [g / (2u² cos²θ)] x² = x tan θ [1 - x / R].
📐 Mathematical Definition & Validity
y(x) = x tan θ - gx² / (2u² cos²θ) | y(x) = x tan θ (1 - x / R)
Validity Scope: Ideal near-Earth projectile flight launched from the origin (0, 0).
🔍 Worked Conceptual Anchor
Problem: A projectile trajectory has the equation y = 2x - 5x² in SI units. Find its launch angle θ and initial speed u (g = 10 m/s²).
  • Compare with y = x tan θ - [g / (2u² cos²θ)] x².
  • Match coefficient of x: tan θ = 2 → θ = arctan(2) ≈ 63.4°.
  • Match coefficient of x²: g / (2u² cos²θ) = 5 → 10 / (2u² cos²θ) = 5 → u² cos²θ = 1.
  • Since tan θ = 2, cos²θ = 1 / (1 + tan²θ) = 1 / (1 + 4) = 1/5.
  • Therefore u² (1/5) = 1 → u² = 5 → u = √5 ≈ 2.24 m/s.
Result: Launch angle is arctan(2) ≈ 63.4° and launch speed is √5 m/s.
👁️ Staged Visual Anchor
In zero gravity, path would be straight line y = x tan θ.
⚠️ Easy Mistake to Make
Confusing the position-time graphs x(t) and y(t) with the spatial trajectory y(x).
Diagnostic: Does the curve y(x) show what happens as a movie in time, or the physical shape of the path in space?
Repair: y(x) is a snapshot of the spatial path in space. It tells you the vertical height for any horizontal distance, with time eliminated.

Horizontal Launch: One Sideways Speed, One Falling Motion

D2 THINK CAREFULLY CU-PHY-KIN-2D-08
⚡ Inferential Leap / Pivot
A horizontal launch has zero initial vertical velocity (uy = 0). The vertical drop determines the time in the air; horizontal speed determines how far it travels in that time.
🎯 Model Contract & Assumptions
Launch angle θ = 0° from an elevated height h.
🛠️ Stepwise Construction (Action → Physical License → Output)
1
Set initial vertical velocity uy = 0 and horizontal velocity ux = u.
Why physically valid: The projectile is projected strictly horizontally.
State output: Initial state: (u, 0).
2
Determine time of flight from vertical drop: h = ½ g t².
Why physically valid: Vertical motion is pure free fall from rest.
State output: t_fall = √(2h / g).
3
Calculate horizontal range: x = u · t_fall.
Why physically valid: Horizontal velocity remains constant throughout the fall.
State output: x = u √(2h / g).
4
Find impact velocity components: vx = u and vy = -gt.
Why physically valid: Components combine perpendicularly at ground impact.
State output: v_impact = √(u² + 2gh) at angle arctan(gt / u).
📐 Mathematical Definition & Validity
t = √(2h / g) | R = u √(2h / g) | vy = √(2gh) | v = √(u² + 2gh)
Validity Scope: Valid for all horizontal launches from height h under uniform gravity.
🔍 Worked Conceptual Anchor
Problem: Source Worked Example 5 (P11): Projectile leaves horizontally at u = 98 m/s from a hill of height h = 490 m (g = 9.8 m/s²). Find flight time, horizontal distance, and impact velocity.
  • Vertical fall time: t = √(2h / g) = √(2 × 490 / 9.8) = √(100) = 10 s.
  • Horizontal distance: x = u · t = 98 × 10 = 980 m.
  • Vertical velocity at ground: vy = -gt = -9.8 × 10 = -98 m/s.
  • Impact speed: v = √(98² + (-98)²) = 98√2 ≈ 138.6 m/s.
  • Impact direction: tan α = |vy| / vx = 98 / 98 = 1 → α = 45° below horizontal.
Result: Flight time is 10 s, horizontal distance is 980 m, impact speed is 98√2 m/s at 45° below horizontal.
👁️ Staged Visual Anchor
Leaves cliff horizontally at u = 98 m/s, uy = 0.
⚠️ Easy Mistake to Make
Thinking a faster horizontal launch speed makes the object stay in the air longer.
Diagnostic: If you fire a high-speed bullet horizontally and drop a bullet from the same height simultaneously, which hits first?
Repair: Both hit the ground at the EXACT same instant! Because vertical acceleration is g for both and both start with uy = 0, their vertical motions are identical.

The Outfielder Sees an Angle, Not a Range

D3 CHALLENGE CU-PHY-KIN-2D-09
⚡ Inferential Leap / Pivot
A fielder at the landing zone does not directly measure x and y; the player perceives the elevation angle φ(t) = arctan[y / (R - x)]. The rate of change of this angle guides the fielder's running strategy.
🎯 Model Contract & Assumptions
Angular perception from an observer's line of sight.
🛠️ Stepwise Construction (Action → Physical License → Output)
1
Position the observer on the ground at distance d from launch.
Why physically valid: Coordinates are measured relative to the observer's position.
State output: Observer at (d, 0).
2
Write the line-of-sight elevation angle: tan φ(t) = y(t) / (d - x(t)).
Why physically valid: Right-triangle geometry from observer's eye to the ball in flight.

What Controls Crossing Time and Drift?

⚡ Inferential Leap / Pivot

Why physically valid: Motion parallel to banks continues for duration t.

D3 CHALLENGE CU-PHY-REL-2D-04
⚡ Inferential Leap / Pivot

🎯 Model Contract & Assumptions

Why physically valid: To land directly opposite, ground velocity must have zero parallel component.
State output: v_bw sin θ = v_stream → sin θ = v_stream / v_bw.

Calculate across-river speed under zero drift.
Why physically valid: Pythagorean theorem yields v_across = √(v_bw² - v_stream²).
State output: t_zero_drift = d / √(v_bw² - v_stream²) > t_min.

Why physically valid: If stream speed exceeds boat speed, sin θ > 1 (impossible to cancel current).

Since the dot product is identically zero, the velocity vector is strictly perpendicular to the position vector, proving it is tangent to the circle.

Non-Uniform Circular Motion Has Two Accelerations

D3 CHALLENGE CU-PHY-CIRC-2D-06
⚡ Inferential Leap / Pivot
When speed changes along a circle, two perpendicular accelerations act simultaneously: radial a_r = v²/r turns the velocity direction, while tangential a_t = dv/dt alters the speed.
🎯 Model Contract & Assumptions
Particle whose speed changes while traversing a curved path.
🛠️ Stepwise Construction (Action → Physical License → Output)
1
Identify radial component a_r = v²/r directed toward center.
Why physically valid: Required to maintain curved trajectory.
State output: Radial acceleration a_r.
2
Identify tangential component a_t = dv/dt = r α along tangent.
Why physically valid: Rate of change of scalar speed.
State output: Tangential acceleration a_t.
3
Vectorially add components: a_net = a_r + a_t.
Why physically valid: Radial and tangential unit vectors are orthogonal (r̂ ⊥ t̂).
State output: Total acceleration vector.
4
Compute net magnitude: a = √(a_r² + a_t²).
Why physically valid: Pythagorean theorem for perpendicular components.
State output: Total acceleration magnitude.
5
Compute angle with tangent: tan φ = a_r / a_t.
Why physically valid: Right-triangle orientation.
State output: Direction φ.
📐 Mathematical Definition & Validity
a⃗_net = a⃗_r + a⃗_t | |a⃗_net| = √(a_r² + a_t²) = √((v²/r)² + (rα)²) | tan φ = a_r / a_t
Validity Scope: Valid for any 2D planar circular or curved motion.
🔍 Worked Conceptual Anchor
Problem: A car moves on a circular track of radius r = 100 m. At a given instant, its speed is v = 10 m/s and its speed is increasing at 1 m/s². Find its total acceleration.
  • Radial acceleration: a_r = v² / r = 10² / 100 = 100 / 100 = 1 m/s².
  • Tangential acceleration: a_t = dv/dt = 1 m/s².
  • Since a_r ⊥ a_t, total acceleration magnitude is: a = √(a_r² + a_t²) = √(1² + 1²) = √2 ≈ 1.414 m/s².
  • Direction: tan φ = a_r / a_t = 1/1 = 1 → φ = 45° to the tangent.
Result: The total acceleration is √2 m/s² (approx 1.41 m/s²) directed at 45° inward from the tangent.
👁️ Staged Visual Anchor
a_r turns the direction; a_t increases/decreases speed.
⚠️ Easy Mistake to Make
Adding radial and tangential accelerations as simple scalars (e.g. a_tot = a_r + a_t = 1 + 1 = 2 m/s²).
Diagnostic: Are radial acceleration and tangential acceleration pointing in the same direction?
Repair: No! They are strictly perpendicular at every point on the path. You must add them via Pythagorean sum: √(1² + 1²) = √2 m/s².
