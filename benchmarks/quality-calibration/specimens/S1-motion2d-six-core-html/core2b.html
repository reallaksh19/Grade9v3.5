Changed demand

Protected DECIDE move

R-EXT-2B-VEL-DECIDE — Choose derivative components, not position ratios, to represent instantaneous direction.

Complete reasoning route

  1. REPRESENT — List x(t), y(t) and the requested instant without selecting a direction formula.
    Why valid: This keeps the decision open.
  2. DECIDE — Choose derivative components, not position ratios, to represent instantaneous direction.
    Why valid: The demand asks for velocity direction.
  3. TRANSFORM — Evaluate v=(7,10) and obtain the angle.
    Why valid: Both components use t=1.
  4. VERIFY — Check tangent consistency.
    Why valid: Velocity is tangent.

Answer

At t=1 s, v_x=7 and v_y=10 m/s, so theta=tan^-1(10/7) above +x.

  1. First decide that both position laws must be differentiated.
  2. Evaluate both derivatives at t=1 s.
  3. Use the signed component ratio.

A projectile returns to level ground with speed U. After impact it retains 36% of its kinetic energy, and its rebound velocity has vertical:horizontal component ratio 3:4. Find its next maximum height in terms of U and g.

Post-attempt review and solution

Changed demand

The rebound direction is supplied as a component ratio rather than an angle; the learner must still protect the energy-to-speed-squared decision before translating the new representation.

Protected DECIDE move

R-EXT-2B-BOUNCE-DECIDE — Convert the energy fraction to a speed-squared fraction before using the component ratio.

Complete reasoning route

  1. REPRESENT — List retained energy and the 3:4 direction information separately.
    Why valid: This does not hand over how energy maps to speed.
  2. DECIDE — Convert the energy fraction to a speed-squared fraction before using the component ratio.
    Why valid: K is proportional to v².
  3. TRANSFORM — Use the 3:4 ratio to get v_y² and then height.
    Why valid: A 3-4-5 ratio sets the vertical speed fraction.
  4. VERIFY — Check scaling against retained energy.
    Why valid: Height cannot exceed retained-energy vertical bound.

Answer

H=81U²/(1250g).

  1. Protected choice: 36% KE means v_after²=0.36U², not v_after=0.36U.
  2. A 3:4 component ratio gives v_y=(3/5)v_after, so v_y²=(9/25)(0.36U²)=81U²/625.
  3. H=v_y²/(2g)=81U²/(1250g).

Justification rubric

  • Selects the model or representation before calculating. — The governing decision is explicit and justified.
  • Carries the reasoning to a checked result. — The solution closes without tutor input.

Independent check: The result uses both the energy fraction and component-ratio squared, so it is much less than U²/(2g).

R-EXT-2B-POINT-DECIDE — Compare P with the straight-line term before attempting to solve for u.

    Why valid: This exposes the changed representation but not the key comparison.

No finite speed can make it pass through P.

Commit before support

Post-attempt review and solution

Changed demand

The drag parameter is given through a velocity half-life rather than c/m; the learner must recognize and translate the exponential representation before integrating.

Protected DECIDE move

R-EXT-2B-DRAG-DECIDE — Translate the halving statement into an exponential velocity law before computing distance.

Complete reasoning route

  1. REPRESENT — Record the empirical half-life statement without assuming constant horizontal velocity.
    Why valid: A halving law is incompatible with v_x constant.
  2. DECIDE — Translate the halving statement into an exponential velocity law before computing distance.
    Why valid: Linear drag produces exponential velocity decay.
  3. TRANSFORM — Integrate the exponential law.
    Why valid: Displacement is the time integral of velocity.
  4. VERIFY — Take the short-time limit.
    Why valid: It approaches v_x0 t.

Answer

x=(v_x0 τ/ln2)[1-2^{-t/τ}], so x=v_x0 t is not valid except as a short-time/zero-drag limit.

  1. Protected choice: infer exponential decay v_x=v_x0 2^{-t/τ}=v_x0 exp[-(ln2)t/τ].
  2. Integrate that velocity from 0 to t.
  3. The integral gives v_x0 τ/ln2 times (1-2^{-t/τ}).

Justification rubric

  • Selects the model or representation before calculating. — The governing decision is explicit and justified.
  • Carries the reasoning to a checked result. — The solution closes without tutor input.

Independent check: At small t the expression expands to approximately v_x0 t.
