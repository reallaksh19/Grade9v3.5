AUTHORED_PRACTICE

A particle has x(t)=10t and y(t)=18t-2t^2 in metres. Find the direction of its instantaneous velocity at t=2 s.

Conditions

  • x and y use the same time and fixed frame.
Optional authored support
  1. Write the representation or event boundary explicitly before substituting values. (REPRESENT, supports R-EXT-2A-VEL-REP)

Your independent attempt

Reasoning route and full solution
  1. REPRESENT — Identify velocity direction as the target and write v_x=dx/dt, v_y=dy/dt.
    Why valid: Instantaneous velocity is the derivative of position.
  2. TRANSFORM — Differentiate and evaluate both components at t=2 s.
    Why valid: Both belong to the same instant.
  3. CONNECT — Use the signed velocity-component ratio to obtain the direction.
    Why valid: A vector direction follows from its components.
  4. VERIFY — Confirm the arrow is tangent to the trajectory.
    Why valid: Velocity must be tangent.

Crux move: R-EXT-2A-VEL-REP

Answer: The velocity components at 2 s are (10,10) m/s, so the direction is 45° above +x.

  1. Differentiate: v_x=10 and v_y=18-4t.
  2. At t=2, v_y=10.
  3. Use tan(theta)=10/10 and check the first quadrant.

Independent check: The position ratio y/x is not used; the tangent/velocity check gives 45°.

AUTHORED_PRACTICE

A projectile launched at 45° reaches 80 m maximum height. On returning to the ground it retains 60% of its kinetic energy and rebounds at 30° above horizontal. Find its new maximum height.

Conditions

  • Ground is level.
  • The rebound creates a new gravity-only launch.
Optional authored support
  1. Write the representation or event boundary explicitly before substituting values. (REPRESENT, supports R-EXT-2A-BOUNCE-REP)

Your independent attempt

Reasoning route and full solution
  1. REPRESENT — Mark impact as a new launch event and separate pre/post states.
    Why valid: The collision changes velocity discontinuously.
  2. DECIDE — Convert retained kinetic energy into retained speed squared before resolving the new angle.
    Why valid: K is proportional to v squared for fixed mass.
  3. TRANSFORM — Resolve post-bounce vertical speed and compute rise height.
    Why valid: Gravity-only height uses v_y²/(2g).
  4. VERIFY — Check energy/component scaling bounds.
    Why valid: Loss and smaller vertical fraction must reduce height.

Crux move: R-EXT-2A-BOUNCE-ENERGY

Answer: The new maximum height is 24 m.

  1. Original u^2=4gH=320g for a 45° launch.
  2. After impact v^2=0.60u^2=192g.
  3. Post-bounce v_y^2=192g×sin^2 30°=48g, so H'=48g/(2g)=24 m.

Independent check: The result is below the original height and scales with retained energy and sin²30°.

AUTHORED_PRACTICE

An ordinary projectile would have range R. At the apex it enters a region where downward gravity becomes 4g while horizontal velocity is unchanged. Express the new range as a fraction of R.

Conditions

  • The gravity change occurs exactly at the apex.
  • Launch and landing heights are equal.
Optional authored support
  1. Write the representation or event boundary explicitly before substituting values. (REPRESENT, supports R-EXT-2A-G-REP)

Your independent attempt

Reasoning route and full solution
  1. REPRESENT — Split the event at the apex and carry the apex state into stage 2.
    Why valid: The model changes only there.
  2. DECIDE — Apply 4g only to the post-apex descent.
    Why valid: The first stage already occurred under g.
  3. TRANSFORM — Convert the time ratio into horizontal distance and add stage distances.
    Why valid: Horizontal speed is continuous.
  4. VERIFY — Bound the result between R/2 and R.
    Why valid: Only post-apex range shrinks.

Crux move: R-EXT-2A-G-DECIDE

Answer: The new range is 3R/4.

  1. The apex is at R/2 in the ordinary flight.
  2. From the same apex height, multiplying gravity by 4 halves the descent time.
  3. The post-apex horizontal distance becomes R/4, giving R/2+R/4=3R/4.

Independent check: The result lies between R/2 and R because stronger gravity changes only the descent segment.

AUTHORED_PRACTICE

A projectile launched at 45° passes through P=(4 m,1 m). Find u²/g and decide whether the projectile is rising or falling at P.

Conditions

  • Uniform gravity acts downward.
  • Launch point is the origin.
Optional authored support
  1. Write the representation or event boundary explicitly before substituting values. (REPRESENT, supports R-EXT-2A-POINT-REP)

Your independent attempt

Reasoning route and full solution
  1. REPRESENT — License the ideal projectile trajectory and mark P on x-y axes.
    Why valid: The path relation requires the gravity-only model.
  2. TRANSFORM — Substitute P into y=x tanθ-gx²/[2u²cos²θ].
    Why valid: Point membership imposes a launch-parameter constraint.
  3. DECIDE — Use local slope or vertical velocity at P to decide the branch.
    Why valid: Point membership alone does not identify rising/falling.
  4. VERIFY — Substitute back and confirm the slope sign.
    Why valid: Independent substitution closes both decisions.

Crux move: R-EXT-2A-POINT-BRANCH

Answer: u²/g=16/3 m and the projectile is already descending at P.

  1. For theta=45 degrees, the trajectory relation becomes y=x-gx^2/u^2.
  2. At P=(4,1), 1=4-16g/u^2, so 16g/u^2=3 and therefore u^2/g=16/3 m.
  3. The local slope is dy/dx=1-2gx/u^2=1-8/(16/3)=-1/2, so the projectile is descending at P.

Independent check: Substituting u²/g=16/3 back into the path gives y=1 and the negative slope locates P after the apex.

AUTHORED_PRACTICE

A projectile has horizontal launch component 120 m/s. In a medium with linear drag, m/c=2 s. Find its horizontal displacement after 2 s using e≈2.718.

Conditions

  • Horizontal drag obeys F_x=-cv_x.
  • Use the supplied time constant.
Optional authored support
  1. Write the representation or event boundary explicitly before substituting values. (REPRESENT, supports R-EXT-2A-DRAG-REP)

Your independent attempt

Reasoning route and full solution
  1. REPRESENT — Reject constant horizontal velocity and write the linear-drag horizontal law.
    Why valid: Drag acts against v_x.
  2. DECIDE — Use exponential velocity decay rather than a constant-acceleration formula.
    Why valid: Acceleration depends on velocity.
  3. TRANSFORM — Integrate v_x over 0 to 2 s.
    Why valid: Displacement is the integral of velocity.
  4. VERIFY — Compare with v_x0 t.
    Why valid: Drag must reduce distance.

Crux move: R-EXT-2A-DRAG-SOLVE

Answer: The displacement is about 152 m.

  1. Use v_x=v_x0 e^{-t/τ} with τ=m/c=2 s.
  2. Integrate: Δx=v_x0 τ(1-e^{-t/τ}).
  3. At t=2 s, Δx=120×2×(1-e^{-1})≈151.7 m.

Independent check: The distance is less than the no-drag value 240 m and approaches it as c→0.
