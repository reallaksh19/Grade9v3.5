Horizontal launch from a height

Key idea

For a horizontal launch the vertical motion starts from rest, so the fall time comes from h = ½gt², and the horizontal distance is the launch speed times that time.

Understand it

When an object leaves a height h moving horizontally at u, its initial vertical velocity is zero. The fall time is therefore t = √(2h/g), exactly as for a dropped object.

During that time the horizontal velocity stays u, so the horizontal distance is x = u × t = u√(2h/g).

At landing the velocity has components u horizontally and gt vertically, so the landing speed is √(u² + (gt)²).

Common slip: Using u in the vertical equation is a common slip; the launch speed is entirely horizontal.

Worked example

A ball rolls off a 5 m high wall at 4 m/s. Find where it lands and its landing speed. Take g = 10 m/s².

  1. Fall time from the vertical motion: 5 = ½ × 10 × t², so t = 1 s.
  2. Horizontal distance: x = 4 × 1 = 4 m from the foot of the wall.
  3. Vertical velocity at landing: v_y = gt = 10 m/s; landing speed = √(4² + 10²) ≈ 10.8 m/s.

Result: 4 m from the wall, at about 10.8 m/s.

Rebuild it yourself

Explain how you would find where a horizontally thrown object lands if you know only the launch speed and the height.

Compare with a model answer

Use the height with h = ½gt² to find the fall time, because the vertical motion starts from rest. Then multiply the unchanged horizontal speed by that time to get the horizontal distance.

Practice

A stone is thrown horizontally at 10 m/s from the top of a cliff 45 m high. Find the time to reach the ground and the horizontal distance travelled. Take g = 10 m/s².

Hint

The vertical motion starts from rest.

Worked answer
  1. 45 = ½ × 10 × t², so t² = 9 and t = 3 s.
  2. Horizontal distance = 10 × 3 = 30 m.

Answer: 3 s and 30 m

Source: Fixture Source Paper A (2020), Q7

A package is released from an aeroplane flying horizontally at 50 m/s at a height of 80 m. How far ahead of the release point does it land? Take g = 10 m/s².

Hint

The package keeps the aeroplane's horizontal velocity.

Worked answer
  1. Fall time: 80 = ½ × 10 × t², so t = 4 s.
  2. Horizontal distance = 50 × 4 = 200 m.

Answer: 200 m
