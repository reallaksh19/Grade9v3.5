Perpendicular components are independent

Key idea

Horizontal and vertical motions share the same clock but do not affect each other. Gravity changes only the vertical velocity.

Understand it

When a ball moves under gravity alone, the horizontal velocity stays constant because no horizontal force acts, while the vertical velocity grows at g every second.

Because both parts run on the same clock, the time found from the vertical motion is the same time used in the horizontal motion. That shared time links the two parts.

This is why a dropped ball and a ball thrown sideways from the same height land together.

Common slip: A faster sideways throw does not keep the ball in the air longer; the fall time depends only on the vertical motion.

Worked example

A ball leaves a shelf 1.25 m high moving sideways at 2 m/s. How far from the shelf does it land? Take g = 10 m/s².

  1. Use only the vertical motion to find the time: 1.25 = ½ × 10 × t², so t² = 0.25 and t = 0.5 s.
  2. Use the same time in the horizontal motion, where velocity is constant: x = 2 × 0.5 = 1 m.
  3. Check: the horizontal speed was never used to find the time, as independence requires.

Result: 1 m from the shelf.

Rebuild it yourself

Explain why the time of fall for a horizontally thrown ball does not depend on how fast it was thrown.

Compare with a model answer

The time comes from the vertical motion alone: it starts with zero vertical velocity and accelerates at g through the same height. The horizontal speed is a separate, independent component, so it changes where the ball lands, not when.

Practice

Two balls are 20 m above the ground. Ball A is dropped and ball B is thrown horizontally at 5 m/s at the same instant. Which reaches the ground first, and when? Take g = 10 m/s².

Hint

Compare only the vertical motions.

Worked answer
  1. Both start with zero vertical velocity and fall 20 m: 20 = ½ × 10 × t².
  2. t² = 4, so t = 2 s for both; they land together.

Answer: They land together after 2 s.

A ball rolls off a table top at 3 m/s and lands 0.4 s later. Find the horizontal distance from the table edge to the landing point.

Hint

Horizontal velocity is constant.

Worked answer
  1. Horizontal velocity stays 3 m/s throughout the flight.
  2. Distance = 3 × 0.4 = 1.2 m.

Answer: 1.2 m
