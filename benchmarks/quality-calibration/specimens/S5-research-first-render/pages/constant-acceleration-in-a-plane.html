Constant acceleration in a plane

Key idea

With constant acceleration, apply the one-dimensional equations of motion separately to x and to y, using the same time t.

Understand it

For each direction, v = u + at and s = ut + ½at² hold with that direction's own components of initial velocity and acceleration.

Solve the x-equations and the y-equations separately, then combine the results into a position vector or a velocity vector.

Only the time is shared between the two directions; every other quantity carries its own component.

Common slip: Using the total acceleration in the x-equation mixes directions; each equation takes only its own component.

Worked example

A puck starts at the origin with velocity (2 m/s, 0) and acceleration (0, 3 m/s²). Find its position after 2 s.

  1. Horizontal: x = uₓt + ½aₓt² = 2 × 2 + 0 = 4 m.
  2. Vertical: y = u_yt + ½a_yt² = 0 + ½ × 3 × 4 = 6 m.
  3. Combine the two components into one position: (4 m, 6 m), at a distance √(16 + 36) ≈ 7.2 m from the origin.

Result: (4 m, 6 m).

Compare with a model answer

Split the initial velocity and the acceleration into x and y components, apply v = u + at to each direction with the same t, then combine the two velocity components using Pythagoras to get the speed and the direction.

Practice

A particle starts from rest at the origin with constant acceleration 2 m/s² along x and 1 m/s² along y. Find its position after 4 s.

Hint

Use s = ½at² for each direction.

Worked answer
  1. x = ½ × 2 × 4² = 16 m.
  2. y = ½ × 1 × 4² = 8 m, so the position is (16 m, 8 m).

Answer: (16 m, 8 m)

A particle has initial velocity 3 m/s along x and constant acceleration 2 m/s² along y. Find its speed at t = 2 s.

Hint

Only the y-velocity changes.

Worked answer
  1. vₓ stays 3 m/s; v_y = 0 + 2 × 2 = 4 m/s.
  2. Speed = √(3² + 4²) = 5 m/s.

Answer: 5 m/s
