Uniform circular motion

Key idea

Moving in a circle at constant speed still needs an acceleration of size v²/r, pointed towards the centre.

Understand it

In uniform circular motion the speed is constant, but the direction of the velocity turns continuously, so the velocity vector changes and there is an acceleration.

That acceleration always points to the centre of the circle and has magnitude a = v²/r. It is called centripetal acceleration.

A faster speed or a tighter circle both need a larger centripetal acceleration.

Common slip: Constant speed does not mean zero acceleration when the path curves.

Worked example

A toy car moves round a circular track of radius 0.5 m at 1 m/s. Find its centripetal acceleration.

  1. Identify the speed and radius: v = 1 m/s and r = 0.5 m.
  2. Apply a = v²/r = 1² / 0.5 = 2 m/s².
  3. State the direction: towards the centre of the track at every instant.

Result: 2 m/s² towards the centre.

Rebuild it yourself

Explain in your own words why a body moving round a circle at constant speed is accelerating.

Compare with a model answer

Its velocity keeps changing direction, and acceleration measures any change in velocity, not just a change in speed. The change always points towards the centre.

Practice

A body moves in a circle of radius 2 m with a constant speed of 4 m/s. Find its centripetal acceleration.

Hint

Use a = v²/r.

Worked answer
  1. a = v²/r = 4² / 2.
  2. a = 8 m/s², directed towards the centre.

Answer: 8 m/s²
