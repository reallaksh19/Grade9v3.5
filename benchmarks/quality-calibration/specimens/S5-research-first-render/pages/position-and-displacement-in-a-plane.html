Position and displacement in a plane

Key idea

A position in a plane needs two numbers, x and y. Displacement is the arrow from the start point to the end point, found by subtracting coordinates.

Understand it

Choose an origin and two perpendicular axes. Every point then has coordinates (x, y), and the position vector is the arrow from the origin to that point.

Displacement is the change in position: Δx = x₂ − x₁ and Δy = y₂ − y₁. Its magnitude is √(Δx² + Δy²), and it does not depend on the path actually followed between the two points.

Distance travelled counts every metre of the path, so it is never smaller than the magnitude of displacement.

Common slip: Adding the path lengths gives distance, not displacement. Displacement only compares the start and end points.

Worked example

A robot moves from (1 m, 1 m) to (4 m, 5 m). Find its displacement.

  1. Subtract the x-coordinates to get the horizontal part: Δx = 4 − 1 = 3 m.
  2. Subtract the y-coordinates to get the vertical part: Δy = 5 − 1 = 4 m.
  3. Join the two perpendicular parts with Pythagoras: |Δr| = √(3² + 4²) = 5 m.
  4. State the direction as well: tan θ = 4/3, so θ ≈ 53° above the x-axis.

Result: 5 m at about 53° above the x-axis.

Rebuild it yourself

Explain in your own words why two different paths between the same two points have the same displacement.

Compare with a model answer

Displacement is defined only by the start and end positions: Δr = r₂ − r₁. The path does not appear in the formula, so every path between the same two points gives the same arrow.

Practice

A particle moves from point A (2 m, 3 m) to point B (8 m, 11 m). Find the magnitude of its displacement.

Hint

Subtract coordinates first.

Worked answer
  1. Δx = 8 − 2 = 6 m and Δy = 11 − 3 = 8 m.
  2. |Δr| = √(6² + 8²) = 10 m.

Answer: 10 m

A student walks 30 m east and then 40 m north in 50 s. Find the magnitude of the displacement and the average velocity.

Hint

The two legs are perpendicular.

Worked answer
  1. Displacement = √(30² + 40²) = 50 m.
  2. Average velocity = displacement / time = 50 / 50 = 1 m/s, directed about 53° north of east.

Answer: 50 m; 1 m/s at about 53° north of east
