Attempt first
Two equal masses m lie on a smooth horizontal surface and are joined by a light string of total length 2a. The midpoint of the initially taut string is pulled vertically upward by a small constant force F. When the masses are separated by 2x, determine the magnitude of the acceleration of either mass.
(A)
(F/2m)·a/sqrt(a^2-x^2)
(B)
(F/2m)·x/sqrt(a^2-x^2)
(C)
(F/2m)·x/a
(D)
(F/2m)·sqrt(a^2-x^2)/x
Conditions. Horizontal surface is frictionless. String is light and remains taut. Each half-string has length a.
Common wrong route. Taking T=F/2 without resolving the angled string segments.
RepresentationString Geometry · question-aligned schematic
Hint ladder · reveal only what you need
H0 · Represent / First Move SOURCE
Sketch one half-string as a right triangle with horizontal projection x and vertical rise y.
H1 · Connect SOURCE
At the pulled midpoint, which components of the two equal tensions balance F?
H2 · Crux SOURCE
Determine the tension from the massless midpoint geometry before applying Newton II to a mass.
H3 · Equation / Formal Model EXPANSION
Let the half-string rise be y. From the right triangle, x²+y²=a², so y=√(a²−x²), with sinθ=y/a and cosθ=x/a.
H4 · Assembly Checkpoint EXPANSION
At the pulled midpoint, F=2T sinθ, hence T=Fa/(2y).
Teacher solution · complete derivation
1
Geometry. Let the half-string rise be y. From the right triangle, x²+y²=a², so y=√(a²−x²), with sinθ=y/a and cosθ=x/a.
2
Massless midpoint balance. At the pulled midpoint, F=2T sinθ, hence T=Fa/(2y).
3
Newton II on one mass. The horizontal component is T cosθ: m ax=T(x/a).
4
Eliminate geometry. Substitute T and y: ax=Fx/[2m√(a²−x²)], matching option B.
Answer: Option (B): a_mass = F x / [2m sqrt(a^2-x^2)].
Check: As x approaches zero, the horizontal acceleration approaches zero because the string segments become nearly vertical.

A bead can slide without friction on a wire shaped as the parabola y=kx^2, with y vertical. The wire is accelerated horizontally with constant acceleration a. Find the distance from the y-axis of the new equilibrium position where the bead can remain at rest relative to the wire.
(A)
a/(gk)
(B)
a/(2gk)
(C)
2a/(gk)
(D)
a/(4gk)
Conditions. The bead is frictionless on the wire. The required equilibrium is relative to the accelerating wire.
Common wrong route. Applying only gravity and leaving the equilibrium at the vertex despite the accelerating support.
RepresentationParabola Frame · question-aligned schematic
Hint ladder · reveal only what you need
H0 · Represent / First Move SOURCE
Work in the wire frame, where the bead is stationary at the desired equilibrium.
H1 · Connect SOURCE
Add the pseudo-force opposite the wire acceleration.
H2 · Crux SOURCE
Introduce the horizontal pseudo-force in the wire frame before minimizing the effective potential.
H3 · Equation / Formal Model EXPANSION
In the wire frame add the pseudo-force ma opposite the wire acceleration.
H4 · Assembly Checkpoint EXPANSION
With y=kx², an effective potential can be written U(x)=mgkx²+max up to a sign convention for the x-axis.
Teacher solution · complete derivation
1
Accelerating frame. In the wire frame add the pseudo-force ma opposite the wire acceleration.
2
Effective potential. With y=kx², an effective potential can be written U(x)=mgkx²+max up to a sign convention for the x-axis.
3
Equilibrium. Set dU/dx=0: 2mgkx+ma=0. The distance from the y-axis is therefore |x|=a/(2gk), option B.
Answer: Option (B): a/(2gk).
Check: As a approaches zero, the equilibrium returns to the parabola's lowest point x=0.

A light inextensible string passes over a smooth fixed pulley and connects masses 0.36 kg and 0.72 kg. The system is released from rest. Taking g=10 m/s^2, find the work in joules done by the string on the 0.36 kg block during the first second.
Conditions. String is light and inextensible. Pulley is smooth. The system starts from rest. Use g=10 m/s^2.
Common wrong route. Using T=mg or using the heavy block's weight difference directly as the work-producing force.
RepresentationAtwood · question-aligned schematic
Hint ladder · reveal only what you need
H0 · Represent / First Move SOURCE
Solve the two-mass Atwood acceleration for the common string motion.
H1 · Connect SOURCE
Use Newton II on the 0.36 kg block to determine T.
H2 · Crux SOURCE
Find the actual string tension first; the work asked is by tension, not by the net force.
H3 · Equation / Formal Model EXPANSION
a=(0.72−0.36)g/(0.72+0.36)=10/3 m s⁻².
H4 · Assembly Checkpoint EXPANSION
For the 0.36 kg mass moving upward, T−mg=ma, so T=0.36(10+10/3)=4.8 N.
Teacher solution · complete derivation
1
Atwood acceleration. a=(0.72−0.36)g/(0.72+0.36)=10/3 m s⁻².
2
Tension. For the 0.36 kg mass moving upward, T−mg=ma, so T=0.36(10+10/3)=4.8 N.
3
Displacement in 1 s. Starting from rest, s=½at²=5/3 m.
4
Work by string. W_T=Ts=4.8×5/3=8 J.
Answer: 8 J.
Check: The work is positive on the lighter block because its displacement is in the same direction as the tension force.

A block takes time t to slide from rest down a 45° inclined plane. When the same plane is made frictionless, the time becomes t/2. If the original coefficient of friction is alpha/100, find alpha.
Conditions. The block starts from rest in both cases and traverses the same distance along the plane.

RepresentationIncline Friction · question-aligned schematic
Hint ladder · reveal only what you need
H0 · Represent / First Move SOURCE
Write the down-slope acceleration once with friction and once without friction.
H1 · Connect SOURCE
For equal distance from rest, compare times through s=(1/2)at^2 rather than through speed.
H2 · Crux SOURCE
Same-distance motion from rest makes t proportional to 1/sqrt(a), so t/2 means four times the acceleration.
H3 · Equation / Formal Model EXPANSION
At 45°, a_r=g(sin45°−μcos45°)=(g/√2)(1−μ); when smooth, a_s=g/√2.
H4 · Assembly Checkpoint EXPANSION
From s=½at², for the same distance t∝1/√a. Since the smooth time is t/2, a_s=4a_r.
Teacher solution · complete derivation
1
Accelerations. At 45°, a_r=g(sin45°−μcos45°)=(g/√2)(1−μ); when smooth, a_s=g/√2.
2
Same-distance timing. From s=½at², for the same distance t∝1/√a. Since the smooth time is t/2, a_s=4a_r.
3
Solve. 1=4(1−μ), so μ=0.75.
4
Requested value. α=100μ=75.
Answer: 75.
Check: With mu=0.75, the rough acceleration is one quarter of the smooth acceleration, so its travel time is twice as large, exactly as stated.

Inside a train accelerating uniformly along a straight track, a boy throws a ball forward at 10 m/s relative to the train and 60 degrees above the horizontal. To catch it again at the launch height he moves 1.15 m forward inside the train. Using g=10 m/s^2, find the train acceleration in m/s^2.
Conditions. Throw speed is relative to the train. Train acceleration is constant. Catch occurs at the original vertical level.
Common wrong route. Treating the train as constant-velocity and using only the ball's horizontal component.
RepresentationTrain Projectile · question-aligned schematic
Hint ladder · reveal only what you need
H0 · Represent / First Move SOURCE
Use the vertical return-to-launch-height condition to find the one common catch time.
H1 · Connect SOURCE
Write ball-minus-train horizontal displacement over that same interval.
H2 · Crux SOURCE
Use the vertical projectile event to get time, then include the train's accelerated displacement in the relative x equation.
H3 · Equation / Formal Model EXPANSION
u_y=10sin60°=5√3. Returning to launch height gives T=2u_y/g=√3 s.
H4 · Assembly Checkpoint EXPANSION
In the train frame the ball has initial horizontal speed u_x=5 m/s and relative acceleration −a, so x_rel=5T−½aT².
Teacher solution · complete derivation
1
Vertical event time. u_y=10sin60°=5√3. Returning to launch height gives T=2u_y/g=√3 s.
2
Relative horizontal motion. In the train frame the ball has initial horizontal speed u_x=5 m/s and relative acceleration −a, so x_rel=5T−½aT².
3
Use the 1.15 m catch displacement. 1.15=5√3−½a(3).
4
Solve. This gives a≈5.0 m/s².
Answer: 5 m/s^2.
Check: For a=5, the relative displacement is about 1.16 m, consistent with the stated 1.15 m after rounding.

sqrt(52) m/s

Teacher solution · complete derivation

Block position at 1 s. The extension is x=0.2cos(ωt)=0.2cos(π/3)=0.1 m. With natural length 4.9 m, the block is at 5.0 m from the spring support.

Projectile vertical condition. At 45° and t=1 s, meeting at ground level requires 0=(u/√2)(1)−½(10)(1)², so u/√2=5.
3
Horizontal check. The horizontal displacement is also (u/√2)×1=5 m, exactly the required block position.

Result. u=5√2=√50 m/s, option A.
Answer: Option (A): sqrt(50) m/s.
Check: With v/sqrt(2)=5 m/s, y(1)=5-5=0 and the horizontal coordinate is exactly the block coordinate.

A 0.2 kg ball rests on top of a 5 m vertical post. A 0.01 kg bullet moving horizontally with speed V strikes the ball centrally. After the collision the ball and bullet move independently and land 20 m and 100 m, respectively, from the foot of the post. Find the bullet's initial speed.
(A)
250 m/s
(B)
250sqrt(2) m/s
(C)
400 m/s
(D)
500 m/s
Conditions. Both bodies begin their post-collision projectile motion from the same 5 m height. Use g=10 m/s^2 for the numerical evaluation consistent with the source options.
Common wrong route. Treating the ranges as velocities without first establishing the common fall time.
RepresentationBullet Ball Post · question-aligned schematic
Hint ladder · reveal only what you need
H0 · Represent / First Move SOURCE
Find the common time to fall 5 m.
H1 · Connect SOURCE
Convert each horizontal range into its post-collision horizontal speed.
H2 · Crux SOURCE
Exploit the shared projectile fall time before applying horizontal momentum conservation at the collision.
H3 · Equation / Formal Model EXPANSION
Both bodies leave the 5 m height horizontally, so 5=½gt² gives t=1 s.
H4 · Assembly Checkpoint EXPANSION
Their horizontal speeds are therefore v_ball=20/1=20 m/s and v_bullet=100/1=100 m/s.
Teacher solution · complete derivation
1
Common fall time. Both bodies leave the 5 m height horizontally, so 5=½gt² gives t=1 s.
2
Post-collision speeds. Their horizontal speeds are therefore v_ball=20/1=20 m/s and v_bullet=100/1=100 m/s.
3
Collision momentum. Horizontal momentum is conserved: 0.01V=0.20(20)+0.01(100).
4
Solve. 0.01V=5, so V=500 m/s, option D.
Answer: Option (D): 500 m/s.
Check: The two bodies have the same fall time because they leave from the same height with zero vertical velocity; their different ranges therefore directly encode different horizontal speeds.
