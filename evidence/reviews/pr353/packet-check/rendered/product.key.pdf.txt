Two equal masses m lie on a smooth horizontal surface and are joined by a light string of total length 2a. The midpoint
of the initially taut string is pulled vertically upward by a small constant force F. When the masses are separated by 2x,
determine the magnitude of the acceleration of either mass.
Conditions
Horizontal surface is frictionless.
String is light and remains taut.
Each half-string has length a.
(A) (F/2m)·a/√𝑎2 −𝑥2
(B) (F/2m)·x/√𝑎2 −𝑥2
(C) (F/2m)·x/a
(D) (F/2m)·√𝑎2 −𝑥2/x
Hints from the source
1. How does the vertical displacement relate to half-string length and half-separation?
2. At the pulled midpoint, which components of the two equal tensions balance the applied force?
3. What horizontal force acts on either mass, and how does Newton’s second law determine its acceleration?
Authored learning support
1. Sketch one half-string as a right triangle with horizontal projection x and vertical rise y.
2. At the pulled midpoint, which components of the two equal tensions balance F?
3. After finding T, resolve only its horizontal component on either mass.
Answer and working
Option (B): 𝑎𝑚𝑎𝑠𝑠 = F x / [2m √𝑎2 −𝑥2].
Authored reasoning route
1. REPRESENT Let the midpoint rise by y. String geometry gives x^2 + y^2 = a^2, so y = sqrt(a^2-x^2).
Why valid: This follows from the stated physical or chemical model and the quantities preserved in the source-
matched task.

Establishes: intermediate result 1
2. CONNECT At the massless midpoint, vertical force balance gives 2T(y/a)=F, hence T=Fa/(2y).

3. TRANSFORM The horizontal force on either mass is T(x/a), so its acceleration is F x/[2m sqrt(a^2-x^2)].
Why valid: This follows from the stated physical or chemical model and the quantities preserved in the source-
matched task.
Uses: stated source quantities
Establishes: Option (B): a_mass = F x / [2m sqrt(a^2-x^2)].
1. Let the midpoint rise by y. String geometry gives 𝑥2 + 𝑦2 = 𝑎2, so y = √𝑎2 −𝑥2.
2. At the massless midpoint, vertical force balance gives 2T(y/a)=F, hence T=Fa/(2y).
3. The horizontal force on either mass is T(x/a), so its acceleration is F x/[2m √𝑎2 −𝑥2].
Independent check
As x approaches zero, the horizontal acceleration approaches zero because the string segments become nearly
vertical.
