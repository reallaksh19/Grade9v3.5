Why this section matters for the question set

This unit builds toward

  • Q5 · D3
    The move learners miss: Use CO2 or NH3 as a counterexample. Try it in Core2
  1. List the supplied local inventories beside equivalence.
    Why valid: CO2 C has two domains; nitrate N three; NH3 N four although each case has equivalent central bonds.
    Result: Two, three and four local totals with equivalence held true.
  2. The step the hard question turns onUse CO2 or NH3 as a counterexample.
    Why valid: One case satisfying the premise but failing the claimed sp2 consequence refutes a universal implication.
    Result: CO2 uses introductory sp; NH3 sp3-like; the claim is false.
  3. Separate model labels from observable bond equality.
    Why valid: Domain count and orbital allocation are different layers from measured equivalence.
    Result: Only nitrate's three-direction planar framework supports the local sp2 description here.
  4. Change to CH4 as an independent test.
    Why valid: Equivalent C-H bonds coexist with four local directions and an sp3 model.
    Result: The rejected equivalence-only rule fails a fresh fourth case.

The key step

Count local sigma directions and lone pairs, conserve the valence basis, check residual p orientation and neighbouring compatibility, then qualify applicability. Propyne/allene classifications are atom-local.

Worked explanation

Apply the proposed equality-implies-sp2 rule to CH4 and CO2, then compare it with a domain inventory.

Follow the operation, its justification and its result.
