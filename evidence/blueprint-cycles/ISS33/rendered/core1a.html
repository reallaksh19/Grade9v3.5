Chemical bonding & hybridisation — Issue #33 candidate: Construction

Contents and concept route

Concept Book

Hybridisation, sigma/pi overlap and molecular shape

Learning promise

Introductory localized hybrid-orbital descriptions for the supplied BF3/NH3, H2CO, ethene, CO2, CH3F, HCN, methylamine and CH4/NH3/H2O cases.

Concept route

  1. Domain count before hybridisation label
    1. Test whether three sigma bonds are sufficient for the proposed label.
    2. Separate number of bonding directions from bond identity or strength.
    3. Choose the local-domain ledger before the hybrid label
  2. Sigma/pi orbital inventory without double-counting
    1. Conserve carbon orbitals while separating domains from sigma/pi components.
    2. Construct the relation between the molecular plane and the pi orbitals.
    3. The central carbon inventory must support two directions and two pi components.
    4. Keep local inventories separate from the triple-bond component inventory.
    5. Avoid spending a carbon basis function twice.
  3. Electron-domain geometry versus molecular shape
    1. Compare full local inventories, not only the common three sigma bonds.
    2. Count local domains and bonded nuclei at two different centres.
    3. Test whether domain geometry and nuclear shape are interchangeable.

Outside this book

  • Delocalised molecular-orbital treatments, hypervalent models, radicals and amide resonance beyond the supplied questions.

Test whether three sigma bonds are sufficient for the proposed label.

Medium concept
Section 1 of 11Next →

Conditions for this construction

  • Can draw simple Lewis structures.
  • Can count central electron domains.
  • Knows introductory 2/3/4-domain geometry rules.
Why this section matters for the question set

This unit builds toward

  • Q5 · D2 · the hardest question in this set
    The move learners miss: Use nitrogen as the counterexample. Try it in Core2
  1. Hold the claimed three-sigma condition fixed.
    Why valid: Both supplied centres satisfy it, so an omitted lone-pair condition can test the implication.
    Result: BF3 and NH3 both have three sigma bonds.
  2. The step the hard question turns onUse nitrogen as the counterexample.
    Why valid: NH3 also has one central lone pair; its local total is four.
    Result: Three sigma bonds alone do not imply three domains/sp2.
  3. Replace the claim with a conditional local rule.
    Why valid: Within localized introductory classification, bonds plus central lone pairs determine the domain total.
    Result: Use the full inventory: three domains -> sp2 model; four -> sp3-like model.
  4. Separate shape observations from a unique orbital basis.
    Why valid: Nuclear geometry constrains a description but does not measure hybrid coefficients.
    Result: The counterexample rejects the bond-only rule without proving unique physical hybrid orbitals.

The key step

BF3 has three B domains; NH3 has three bonds plus one N lone pair. Introductory sp2/planar versus sp3-like domain/pyramidal shape follows the local model.

Worked explanation

Would a three-bond nitrogen with a lone pair and a three-bond boron without one require identical domain inventories? Explain.

Follow the operation, its justification and its result.

  1. A counterexample must hold the claimed condition while changing an omitted relevant condition.

  2. No: four versus three. Keeping the answer identical despite the changed lone-pair condition suggests the misconception.

  3. NH3 falsifies three-sigma-implies-sp2: its local lone pair makes four domains. Geometry constrains a model but does not uniquely prove a hybrid basis.

Result

No: four versus three. Keeping the answer identical despite the changed lone-pair condition suggests the misconception.

Check

Changed check: BF3 and NH3 share three bonds but differ in local lone pairs; the proposed rule predicts the same result incorrectly.

1

Conserve carbon orbitals while separating domains from sigma/pi components.

Hard concept
Section 4 of 11← PreviousNext →

Conditions for this construction

  • Can count electron domains.
  • Knows introductory 2/3/4-domain geometry rules.
Why this section matters for the question set

This unit builds toward

  • Q2 · D2
    The move learners miss: Allocate the four carbon valence functions. Try it in Core2
  1. Count C neighbours in HCHO.
    Why valid: Two H and one O are three connections; bond multiplicity is a separate inventory.
    Result: Three domains; no C lone pair.
  2. The step the hard question turns onAllocate the four carbon valence functions.
    Why valid: One 2s plus two 2p form three sp2 hybrids, leaving the third 2p unused in that mixing.
    Result: Three sigma-direction hybrids plus one residual p.
  3. Resolve the C=O bond components.
    Why valid: A localized double-bond account has one axial sigma and one lateral pi component.
    Result: C=O: one sigma + one pi; residual C p supplies the pi role.
  4. Close the basis ledger using an sp comparison.
    Why valid: sp gives two hybrids and two residual p functions from the same four inputs.
    Result: Both allocations total four; no p function is spent twice.

The key step

sp2 accounting is three hybrid orbitals plus one residual p. A p function already mixed into a hybrid is not simultaneously counted as an additional residual orbital.

Worked explanation

Inventory sp2 carbon and sp carbon; can a p orbital used in a hybrid also be counted as a separate residual p?

Follow the operation, its justification and its result.

  1. Three sigma directions do not consume the remaining perpendicular p orbital.

  2. No double use. Totals remain four in both cases.

  3. HCHO carbon has three-domain sp2 accounting, three sp2 sigma directions and one unhybridised p orbital; C=O has one sigma plus one pi.

Result

No double use. Totals remain four in both cases.

Check

Changed check: for an sp carbon, two hybrids plus two residual p orbitals still total four basis functions.

Conserve carbon orbitals while separating domains from sigma/pi components.

Construct the relation between the molecular plane and the pi orbitals.

Why this section matters for the question set

This unit builds toward

  • Q3 · D2
    The move learners miss: Align the p functions relative to the molecular plane. Try it in Core2
  1. Build the two local carbon sigma frames.
    Why valid: Each ethene carbon has three sigma directions and one remaining p function in sp2 accounting.
    Result: Two in-plane three-direction frames; one perpendicular p at each C.
  2. The step the hard question turns onAlign the p functions relative to the molecular plane.
    Why valid: A residual p is perpendicular to its carbon sigma plane.
    Result: Parallel perpendicular p directions in planar ethene.
  3. Draw both axial and lateral overlap.
    Why valid: The C-C sigma component is axial; lateral p overlap lies above and below the plane.
    Result: A sigma framework plus a pi component with a node in the molecular plane.
  4. Compare rigid rotation with relative CH2-group rotation.
    Why valid: Only the latter changes relative p alignment.
    Result: Whole-picture rotation preserves overlap geometry; relative twisting can reduce pi alignment.

Worked explanation

Rotate the whole ethene picture, then rotate only one CH2 group. Which operation changes relative p alignment?

Follow the operation, its justification and its result.

  1. Three sigma directions lie in each sp2 carbon plane; residual p is perpendicular to that plane.

  2. Only the relative-group rotation changes alignment. A whole-page-orientation answer exposes a representation error.

  3. Ethene has a coplanar sigma framework; the two residual p directions are parallel and perpendicular to it in the introductory model.

Result

Only the relative-group rotation changes alignment. A whole-page-orientation answer exposes a representation error.

Check

Changed check: rotate one CH2 group while preserving C-C connectivity; ask which overlap is sensitive to the relative orientation.
