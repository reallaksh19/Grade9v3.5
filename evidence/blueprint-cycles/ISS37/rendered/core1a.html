Section 1 of 1

The key step

A local count can be a useful first model without being a complete physical explanation: structural connectivity and orbital alignment decide whether a delocalisation-sensitive model is needed, and a formula only warrants claims about the quantity it actually defines.

What you need before this

Prerequisites

  • Can recall simple sp/sp2/sp3 labels.
  • Can distinguish sigma and pi overlap.
  • Knows electron-domain versus molecular geometry.
  • Knows introductory resonance contributors.

Formula reference · meaning and conditions

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it

supplied overlap factor = cos(theta); model scope does not automatically extend to total molecular energy

A mathematical relation is evidence only about the quantity it defines. At 0°, 60° and 90° the supplied overlap-factor values are 1, 1/2 and 0; a total-energy law requires an additional energetic model or evidence.

  • Use the relation only for the overlap factor named in Q9.
  • Keep atom connectivity and formal electron count distinct from orbital orientation in the torsion comparison.
  • Do not infer donation magnitude or total molecular energy without an additional specified relation/model.

Execute a supplied orbital model while preventing an unsupported transfer from overlap to total energy

Mistake clinic · diagnose and repair

A tempting wrong path

  • Four Lewis entries overrule supplied delocalisation conditions.
  • Torsion changes the formal electron inventory.
  • A changed hybrid label is a causal explanation of donor strength.
  • Spatial proximity equals an orbital-connected p pathway.
  • A hybrid label uniquely determines every angle and physical orbital basis.
  • Recalling a local rule proves its applicability.
  • Three sigma bonds always imply an empty residual p orbital.
  • Geometry alone uniquely measures donation and a rotational barrier.
  • The given overlap factor is the total molecular energy.
  • A local count and label explain every geometry and reactivity difference.

Diagnose

  • Compare adjacent amide N with a saturated-spacer amine N. Does the same four-entry Lewis inventory establish the same donation pathway?
  • Count valence and total electrons for CH3NO before and after torsion. Identify which geometric relation changes.
  • If two sketches have the same label but different donor alignment, must their donation be identical? If the label changes alone, has a mechanism been supplied?
  • Would folding the saturated-spacer molecule closer create the same continuous p pathway as removing the spacer?
  • Can one four-domain label predict the same nuclear shape for CH4 and H2O? Does observing a shape uniquely establish an orbital basis?
  • Both sites have three sigma bonds and a lone pair. Is that alone sufficient to choose identical localized orbital-role descriptions?
  • Compare CH3 radical (7 valence electrons) with a model CH3+ inventory (6). Must their residual p occupancy be the same?
  • Does observed planar geometry alone measure donor strength? What extra information would a numerical rotational barrier require?
  • If theta stays at 60 degrees while an independent energy term changes, does overlap change? Does total energy necessarily stay fixed?
  • Would one bond/lone-pair count plus a label explain NH3 shape, allene terminal-plane relation and amide donation by the same mechanism?

Repair

  • Approximately planar amide N can use an introductory sp2-like sigma framework plus an aligned p-like lone-pair donor. Four Lewis entries do not override the supplied delocalised-model conditions; the basis is not unique observed reality.
  • Formamide CH3NO has 18 valence electrons and 24 total electrons. The supplied three-centre donor/carbonyl pi model has four electrons. A 90-degree twist changes alignment, not these inventories.
  • Twisting reduces relevant alignment and the supplied delocalisation contribution. A changing hybrid label alone explains neither mechanism nor a quantitative barrier; total torsional energy has additional contributions.
  • Amide A has the supplied direct donor/carbonyl path. Saturated-spacer amine B lacks that ordinary contiguous path. Spatial proximity alone is insufficient, and absence of this path is not an absolute zero-interaction theorem.
  • CH4/NH3/H2O share common sp3-like introductory descriptions while their nuclear shapes/angles differ. Geometry constrains models but does not uniquely measure a hybrid decomposition; lone pairs and delocalisation matter.
  • Four-domain counting is useful for localized introductory classification. Adjacent pi acceptors, aligned donor orbitals, planarity/donation evidence or radical occupancy can require a richer model.
  • Supplied planar CH3 radical has three sigma directions and a perpendicular p-like SOMO with one electron. Three sigma bonds alone do not determine an empty residual p orbital.
  • Planarity/bond metrics and condition-specific spectroscopic/dynamic evidence can test localized versus delocalised accounts. Geometry alone does not uniquely measure donation, hybrid coefficients or a rotational barrier; exact empirical numbers need species/conditions/source.
  • Given factors at 0,60,90 degrees are 1,1/2,0. Both cosine and cosine-squared are even. A degenerate two-orbital Hamiltonian with coupling t0 cos(theta) gives epsilon ± |t0 cos(theta)|, so no universal cosine-squared total-energy law follows.
  • Use local count as a bounded starting model, then check residual p directions, occupancy, adjacency/alignment and evidence. A label alone explains neither every geometry nor all reactivity. NH3, allene and amide stress different limitations.

Keep alignment separate from total energy

Look along the fixed C-N axis. The two centres project to the same origin. The solid donor direction turns relative to the dashed carbonyl direction. This is a direction diagram, not a measured orbital or overlap integral.

θ = 0°; given factor = 1; toy E = -1.

Declared demonstration only: g = cos(θ); E = -g² + c in arbitrary toy units. The square is an explicitly chosen example, not a universal law. Hold θ fixed and vary c: the same g permits different E. Predict this before moving c. No physical barrier is computed.

Repair for Q9

Test the rule on a changed case

If theta stays at 60 degrees while an independent energy term changes, does overlap change? Does total energy necessarily stay fixed?

Expected pattern: Overlap factor unchanged; total energy need not remain fixed. Repeated equality of overlap and total energy identifies variable conflation.
