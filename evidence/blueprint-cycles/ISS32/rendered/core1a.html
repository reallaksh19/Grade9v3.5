Steric electron-domain counting and multiple bonds

Subject: ChemistryTopic: Chemical Bonding and Molecular StructureConcept: Steric electron-domain counting and multiple bondsConcept difficulty: Medium concept
1

Count each bonded atom as one domain and add only central lone pairs

Medium concept
Section 1 of 3Next →

The key step

An electron domain is a spatial direction of repulsive charge; all electrons between two bonded atoms constitute exactly one domain.

What you need before this

Prerequisites

  • Recognises single and multiple bonds in simple Lewis structures.
Why this section matters for the question set

  1. The step the hard question turns onCount each bonded atom as one domain regardless of bond order.
    Why valid: Multiple electron pairs between the same two nuclei share one internuclear direction.
    Result: bonded atom domain count
  2. Include only lone pairs residing directly on the central atom.
    Why valid: Peripheral lone pairs on ligands do not influence the central atom's steric number.
    Result: central lone pair count
  3. Sum bonding domains and central lone pairs to obtain total electron domains.
    Why valid: Total domains determine the underlying electrostatic geometry.
    Result: total electron domains
Formula reference · meaning and conditions

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it

Steric Number = (number of bonded atoms) + (number of lone pairs on central atom)

Each bonded atom constitutes exactly one bonding domain regardless of bond order (single, double, triple); only lone pairs localized on the central atom contribute to its steric environment.

  • Count each attached atom as one domain regardless of single/double/triple bond order.
  • Include only unshared electron pairs on the central atom, excluding terminal ligand lone pairs.

Mistake clinic · diagnose and repair

A tempting wrong path

  • Each line of a double bond is a separate local direction.
  • Terminal-atom lone pairs add domains at the central atom.
  • Bond multiplicity equals neighbour-direction multiplicity.

Diagnose

  • At carbon in HCN, count bonded-atom directions and C-N shared pairs separately. Repeat the two counts for HCHO.
  • Count N-centred domains in NH3 and NF3. Then state where the F lone pairs are located.
  • A learner counts HCN carbon as four directions from one C-H line plus three C-N lines. Repair the operation and apply it to HCHO.

Repair

  • HCHO carbon has three bonded-atom directions. C=O contributes one local domain, although its double bond contains one sigma and one pi component. Pi density is not confined to the internuclear axis.
  • NF3 has three nitrogen-to-fluorine directions and one nitrogen lone pair: four N-centred domains. Terminal F lone pairs do not add N-centred domains.
  • Counting both C=O lines as separate domains confuses bond components with one bonded-atom direction. HCHO carbon has three domains.

Hybridisation, orbital conservation, and overlap symmetry

Subject: ChemistryTopic: Chemical Bonding and Molecular StructureConcept: Hybridisation, orbital conservation, and overlap symmetryConcept difficulty: Medium concept
1

Conserve total orbital count when mapping electron domains to hybrid sets and classifying overlap

Medium concept
Section 2 of 3← PreviousNext →

The key step

The number of hybrid orbitals produced strictly equals the number of pure atomic orbitals mixed; end-on overlap gives cylindrical sigma symmetry, while lateral overlap produces a nodal plane.

What you need before this

Prerequisites

  • Understands basic s and p atomic orbital shapes.
  • Inventory central-atom steric electron domains by grouping multiple bonds into single domains and including only central lone pairs
Why this section matters for the question set

  1. Match the steric domain count to the required number of hybrid orbitals.
    Why valid: Electrons repel into symmetric 3D directions requiring equivalent hybrid orbitals.
    Result: domain-hybrid requirement
  2. The step the hard question turns onCombine one s orbital with the necessary p orbitals (sp, sp2, sp3) conserving total orbital count.
    Why valid: Orbital conservation dictates that mixing n atomic orbitals yields n hybrid orbitals.
    Result: conserved hybrid orbital set
  3. Classify bond overlaps: coaxial end-on as sigma (cylindrical) and lateral side-on as pi (nodal plane).
    Why valid: Symmetry criteria confirm bond type.
    Result: verified overlap and bond class
Formula reference · meaning and conditions

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it

n atomic orbitals mixed -> n hybrid orbitals produced (sp -> 2, sp2 -> 3, sp3 -> 4)

Linear combination of valence atomic orbitals conserves total orbital count; the number of directional hybrid orbitals formed equals the steric number of electron domains.

  • Hybridisation occurs between orbitals of comparable energy in the valence shell of the central atom.
  • Number of hybrid orbitals formed strictly equals the number of pure atomic orbitals hybridized.

σ bond: coaxial end-on overlap with cylindrical symmetry; π bond: lateral side-on overlap with nodal plane on internuclear axis

Covalent bonds are classified by orbital overlap geometry: sigma bonds have electron density concentrated directly along the internuclear axis; pi bonds have electron density above and below that axis.

  • Internuclear axis is conventionally taken as the z-axis.
  • Atomic orbitals must have matching symmetry and favorable phase overlap.

Mistake clinic · diagnose and repair

A tempting wrong path

  • The superscript is the total number of hybrid orbitals.
  • The page layout is the molecular geometry.
  • Turning a drawing on the page changes sigma into pi.
  • sp2 has two hybrids and sp3 has three.

Diagnose

  • An sp2 set is proposed to contain two hybrids. How many input and output orbitals should it have? Then apply your method to an sp set.
  • Count domains at C in CH3Cl and C in CH4. Does redrawing either as a cross change its count?
  • Rotate the whole end-on sketch by 90 degrees on paper. Does it become pi? Compare that with rotating one p orbital relative to its neighbour.
  • Compare proposed totals sp=1, sp2=2, sp3=3 against basis-function conservation. Give the corrected sequence.

Repair

  • For second-period atoms, one 2s plus three 2p orbitals gives four sp3 hybrids. The superscript counts p inputs, not total outputs.
  • CH4 has four bonding domains, no carbon lone pair, and a tetrahedral introductory sp3 description. A Lewis layout encodes connectivity.
  • Sigma overlap is end-on/axial in the supplied sketch; pi overlap is lateral with a nodal plane containing the axis.
  • BF3: three domains and three sp2 hybrids. CH4: four domains and four sp3 hybrids. For these centres use valence 2s/2p language rather than ambiguous 1s + 2p shorthand.

3D Stereochemical representation and qualitative models

1

Depict non-coplanar tetrahedral domains with wedge-dash notation and evaluate qualitative model boundaries

Medium concept
Section 3 of 3← Previous

The key step

Flat Lewis structures show connectivity, not 3D shape; wedge-dash conventions capture non-coplanar angles (109.5° / 107°), and VSEPR predicts geometry from domain repulsion without solving orbital wavefunctions.

What you need before this

Prerequisites

  • Understands that molecules occupy 3D space.
  • Inventory central-atom steric electron domains by grouping multiple bonds into single domains and including only central lone pairs
  • Map steric electron domain counts to introductory hybrid-orbital sets (sp, sp2, sp3) and classify coaxial vs lateral orbital overlaps

Depict non-coplanar tetrahedral domains with wedge-dash notation and evaluate qualitative model boundaries

Mistake clinic · diagnose and repair

A tempting wrong path

  • Adding depth marks permits an extra bond or domain.
  • A familiar chapter keyword selects the model.
  • A Lewis cross proves a square-planar molecule.

Diagnose

  • Draw NH3 and a tetrahedral four-bond NH4+ comparison. Mark which slot differs and recount the H atoms in each.
  • Task A asks for atom/domain arrangement; task B asks which orbitals overlap along the bond axis. State what information each model must supply.
  • Does a cross-shaped Lewis CH4 drawing assert square-planar CH4? Does adding wedge/dash notation change the number of bonds?

Repair

  • NH3 has N at the centre, exactly three N-H directions and one distinguishable lone-pair domain. Tetrahedral domain arrangement and trigonal-pyramidal molecular shape describe different inventories.
  • VSEPR fits qualitative electron-domain arrangement. An orbital-overlap model addresses orbital participation. VSEPR does not itself calculate an exact experimental angle or full electronic structure.
  • For CH4 a wedge/dash projection represents four tetrahedral bond directions. Projected page angles are not all physical 109.5-degree angles.
