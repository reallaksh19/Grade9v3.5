Distinguish the hybrid-set name from the number of orbitals in that set.

Medium concept
Section 1 of 11Next →

Conditions for this construction

Why this section matters for the question set

This unit builds toward

  • Q1 · D1
    The move learners miss: Select one valence s and three valence p inputs. Try it in Core2
  1. Read the requested four equivalent domain directions.
    Why valid: The question supplies a tetrahedral four-direction introductory model.
    Result: Four model directions must be represented.
  2. The step the hard question turns onSelect one valence s and three valence p inputs.
    Why valid: For a second-period centre, these four independent basis functions can be recombined without changing the basis dimension.
    Result: 2s + 2px + 2py + 2pz: four inputs.
  3. Name the recombined set and count its members.
    Why valid: The name sp3 records one s and three p inputs; the total number is 1+3.
    Result: sp3; four hybrid orbitals.
  4. Compare the count with an sp allocation.
    Why valid: One s plus one p has two outputs, so the superscript alone cannot be the set size.
    Result: sp has two outputs; the four-member sp3 inventory is consistent.

The key step

For second-period atoms, one 2s plus three 2p orbitals gives four sp3 hybrids. The superscript counts p inputs, not total outputs.

Formula reference · meaning and conditions

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it

3 electron-domain directions ↔ sp2 ↔ 3 hybrids; 4 directions ↔ sp3 ↔ 4 hybrids

In the introductory valence-bond model, the number of hybrid orbitals equals the number of atomic orbitals combined and matches the modelled electron-domain directions.

  • Use this as an introductory local bonding model, not as a complete quantum description.

Worked explanation

An sp2 set is proposed to contain two hybrids. How many input and output orbitals should it have? Then apply your method to an sp set.

Follow the operation, its justification and its result.

  1. A change of orbital basis preserves the number of independent orbitals.

  2. Three for sp2, two for sp. Repeating the superscript-as-total rule in both cases suggests a held counting rule; correct changed cases after an isolated slip suggest an execution error.

  3. For second-period atoms, one 2s plus three 2p orbitals gives four sp3 hybrids. The superscript counts p inputs, not total outputs.

Result

Three for sp2, two for sp. Repeating the superscript-as-total rule in both cases suggests a held counting rule; correct changed cases after an isolated slip suggest an execution error.

Check

Changed check: an sp set has two hybrids because one s plus one p supplies two basis functions; use this conservation rule on the requested set.

1

Separate a bonded-atom direction from the number of shared pairs.

Medium concept
Section 2 of 11← PreviousNext →

Conditions for this construction

  • Can read supplied atoms, single/double/triple bonds, and shared versus lone pairs in a simple Lewis structure.
Why this section matters for the question set

This unit builds toward

  • Q2 · D2
    The move learners miss: Count C-to-O as one local VSEPR domain. Try it in Core2
  1. Make separate neighbour and shared-pair inventories for HCHO.
    Why valid: Two H atoms and one O are attached to C; a C=O double bond contains two shared pairs.
    Result: Three neighbours; two pairs in C=O.
  2. The step the hard question turns onCount C-to-O as one local VSEPR domain.
    Why valid: A multiple bond connects one pair of nuclei and supplies one bonded-neighbour direction in local domain counting. Sigma and pi density have different spatial symmetry.
    Result: One C=O domain; this does not place pi density on the internuclear axis.
  3. Add the two C-H directions.
    Why valid: Each of the two distinct C-H connections adds one domain and C has no lone pair.
    Result: 1+1+1 = three C-centred domains.
  4. Recount carbon directions in HCN.
    Why valid: H-C and C-N connect carbon to two neighbours although C-N has three shared pairs.
    Result: Two domains in HCN; shared-pair count and neighbour count are different.

The key step

HCHO carbon has three bonded-atom directions. C=O contributes one local domain, although its double bond contains one sigma and one pi component. Pi density is not confined to the internuclear axis.

Formula reference · meaning and conditions

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it

electron-domain count = bonded-neighbour regions + lone-pair regions on the chosen central atom

VSEPR domain counting is local to the selected centre. A single, double or triple bond to one neighbour occupies one region/direction.

  • Choose the central atom before counting.
  • Treat each bonded neighbour as one region regardless of bond order.

Worked explanation

At carbon in HCN, count bonded-atom directions and C-N shared pairs separately. Repeat the two counts for HCHO.

Follow the operation, its justification and its result.

  1. The sigma component and pi component of a multiple bond connect the same atom pair.

  2. HCN: two carbon directions, three C-N pairs. HCHO: three directions, two C-O pairs.

  3. HCHO carbon has three bonded-atom directions. C=O contributes one local domain, although its double bond contains one sigma and one pi component. Pi density is not confined to the internuclear axis.

Result

HCN: two carbon directions, three C-N pairs. HCHO: three directions, two C-O pairs.

Check

Changed check: a triple bond to N in HCN still supplies one C-to-N direction; carbon also has the H direction.

Separate a bonded-atom direction from the number of shared pairs.

Translate three bonds and one lone pair without adding a fifth direction.

Medium concept

Why this section matters for the question set

This unit builds toward

  • Q4 · D2
    The move learners miss: Project the four domain directions with a declared depth convention. Try it in Core2
  1. Count exactly three N-H connections and one N lone pair.
    Why valid: The question supplies the inventory at nitrogen.
    Result: Four domains: three bonds and one lone pair.
  2. The step the hard question turns onProject the four domain directions with a declared depth convention.
    Why valid: Two ordinary lines may denote in-plane directions; a solid wedge denotes toward and a hashed wedge away. The lone-pair slot must replace one bond slot.
    Result: N at centre; exactly three labelled H endpoints and one LP direction.
  3. Distinguish domain arrangement from nuclear shape.
    Why valid: Domain geometry counts all four slots; molecular shape counts the three bonded H nuclei.
    Result: Tetrahedral domain model; trigonal-pyramidal molecular shape, without an exact-angle inference.
  4. Compare with the four-bond NH4+ inventory.
    Why valid: Replacing the LP slot with a fourth N-H bond adds a bonded nucleus but no fifth domain.
    Result: NH3: three H + LP; NH4+: four H. The NH3 drawing must not contain a fourth H.

The key step

NH3 has N at the centre, exactly three N-H directions and one distinguishable lone-pair domain. Tetrahedral domain arrangement and trigonal-pyramidal molecular shape describe different inventories.

Formula reference · meaning and conditions

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it

A flat Lewis structure records connectivity/electron pairs but does not by itself encode the three-dimensional domain geometry.

  • Four regions around a centre are arranged tetrahedrally in the introductory VSEPR model.

Worked explanation

  2. NH3 has three H plus a lone pair; NH4+ has four H and no N lone pair in the local Lewis model.

  3. NH3 has N at the centre, exactly three N-H directions and one distinguishable lone-pair domain. Tetrahedral domain arrangement and trigonal-pyramidal molecular shape describe different inventories.

Result

NH3 has three H plus a lone pair; NH4+ has four H and no N lone pair in the local Lewis model.
