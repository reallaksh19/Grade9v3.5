Two-dimensional kinematics and projectile model: Orientation map

Split one plane-motion event into independent x/y components with one clock

Subject: PhysicsTopic: Motion in 2 DConcept: Split one plane-motion event into independent x/y components with one clockConcept difficulty: Medium concept

Hard transition

Perpendicular components may be solved separately because each axis equation contains only that axis's quantities, but they still describe one object at one physical time.

Governing relation

Δx=uxt+0.5axt2;Δy=uyt+0.5ayt2

Each perpendicular displacement component follows the 1D constant-acceleration displacement relation over one common interval.

  • a_x and a_y are constant over the interval.
  • Both component equations use the same elapsed time and frame.

Compact anchor

A particle leaves the origin with u = (2, 0) m/s and constant a = (0, 6) m/s². Where is it at t = 1 s?

Δx = 2 × 1 = 2 m and Δy = 0.5 × 6 × 1² = 3 m, both read at the same t = 1 s, so it is at (2, 3) m.

A particle has x-velocity 6 m/s and y-velocity -8 m/s at t=3 s. Explain what 'independent components' means and what must remain shared between the x and y descriptions.

Apply constant-acceleration kinematics component by component

Subject: PhysicsTopic: Motion in 2 DConcept: Apply constant-acceleration kinematics component by componentConcept difficulty: Medium concept

Hard transition

The one-dimensional constant-acceleration equations can be applied to each perpendicular component independently when a_x and a_y are each constant over the same interval.

Governing relation

vx=ux+axt;vy=uy+ayt

Each perpendicular velocity component follows the 1D constant-acceleration relation over one common interval.

  • a_x and a_y are constant over the interval.
  • Both component equations use the same elapsed time and frame.
Δx=uxt+0.5axt2;Δy=uyt+0.5ayt2

Each perpendicular displacement component follows the 1D constant-acceleration displacement relation over one common interval.

  • a_x and a_y are constant over the interval.
  • Both component equations use the same elapsed time and frame.

Compact anchor

u = (1, 2) m/s and a = (2, −2) m/s², both constant. What is the velocity at t = 2 s?

v_x = 1 + 2 × 2 = 5 m/s and v_y = 2 − 2 × 2 = −2 m/s on the same clock, so v = (5, −2) m/s.

Select the gravity-only projectile model before using projectile consequences

Subject: PhysicsTopic: Motion in 2 DConcept: Select the gravity-only projectile model before using projectile consequencesConcept difficulty: Medium concept

Hard transition

After release in the ideal near-Earth model, projectile motion is not a new set of laws: it is the same component kinematics with a_x=0 and a_y=-g (for +y upward).

Governing relation

ax=0;ay=−g;vx=ux;vy=uy−gt;Δx=uxt;Δy=uyt−0.5gt2

With +y upward, air resistance neglected and near-Earth constant g, projectile motion is 2D constant acceleration specialized to zero horizontal and downward vertical acceleration.

  • Near-Earth region with approximately constant g.
  • Air resistance neglected.
  • +y is declared upward for the displayed signs.
  • The object is in free flight after release.

Compact anchor

An ideal projectile has u_x = 10 m/s and u_y = 5 m/s, with +y up and g = 10 m/s². Where is it, and how is it moving, 1 s after release?

Δx = 10 m and Δy = 5 × 1 − 5 × 1² = 0 m, so it is back at launch height; v = (10, 5 − 10) = (10, −5) m/s.

Before you go on

A stone is launched horizontally from a cliff with speed u. With +y upward and air resistance neglected, write its component acceleration, velocity and displacement relations after release and explain why one common t is used.
