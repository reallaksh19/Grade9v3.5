Newton's Laws & Impulse-Momentum Theorem
4. When we catch a cricket ball, we catch it at the front and make the hands move with the ball backwards. Why is that?
  • (a) To reduce the impulse
  • (b) To increase the impulse
  • (c) To increase the time of contact, thereby increase the force
  • (d) To increase the time of contact, thereby decrease the force
Official Key: (d) To increase the time of contact, thereby decrease the force
Independent Derivation: Impulse J = F_avg * Delta_t = Delta_p. Bringing the ball of fixed mass m and velocity v to rest involves a fixed change in momentum Delta_p. By moving the hands backward, contact duration Delta_t increases, which reduces the average impulsive force F_avg = Delta_p / Delta_t experienced by the hands. Option (d).

25. A machine gun fires a bullet of mass 40g with a velocity of 1200 m/s. The man holding it can exert a maximum force of 144 N on the gun. How many bullets can he fire per second at the most?

  • (b) 2
  • (c) 3

12. Two blocks of masses m and M are placed on a horizontal frictionless table connected by a light spring. Mass M is pulled to the right with a force F. If the acceleration of mass m is a, the acceleration of mass M will be:
  • (a) (F + ma)/M
  • (b) (F - ma)/M
  • (c) am/M
  • (d) F/M
Official Key: (b) (F - ma)/M
Independent Derivation: The light spring transmits a force F_s that accelerates mass m with acceleration a, so F_s = m*a to the right. By Newton's third law, the spring exerts an equal and opposite force F_s = m*a on mass M pulling it to the left. Net force on M is F_net = F - F_s = F - m*a. Acceleration of M is a_M = (F - ma) / M. Option (b).

24. A uniform rope of length L and mass M is placed on a frictionless horizontal table. A force F is applied horizontally at one end. The tension at a distance x from the pulled end is:
  • (a) F(1 - x/L)
  • (b) F*x/L
  • (c) F(1 + x/L)
  • (d) F
Official Key: (a) F(1 - x/L)

18. A block of mass m is placed on an inclined plane. With increase in inclination angle theta, the contact force exerted by the plane on the block (before slipping):
  • (a) Decreases
  • (b) Increases
  • (c) Remains constant equal to mg
  • (d) First increases then decreases
Official Key: (c) Remains constant equal to mg
Independent Derivation: The contact force R is the vector resultant of normal reaction N = mg*cos(theta) and static friction f_s = mg*sin(theta). Since the block is stationary in static equilibrium, vector sum R + mg = 0 => |R| = sqrt(N^2 + f_s^2) = sqrt((mg*cos(theta))^2 + (mg*sin(theta))^2) = mg * sqrt(cos^2(theta) + sin^2(theta)) = mg = constant. Option (c).

28. The minimum force required to slide a block of mass m on a horizontal plane having friction coefficient mu is:
  • (a) \mu mg
  • (b) \frac{\mu mg}{\sqrt{1 + \mu^2}}
  • (c) \mu mg \sqrt{1 + \mu^2}
  • (d) \frac{mg}{\sqrt{1 + \mu^2}}
Official Key: (b) \frac{\mu mg}{\sqrt{1 + \mu^2}}
Independent Derivation: Pulling at an upward angle theta: F*cos(theta) = mu * N = mu * (mg - F*sin(theta)) => F = (mu * mg) / (cos(theta) + mu*sin(theta)). Denominator is maximized when tan(theta) = mu, where max denominator value is sqrt(1 + mu^2). Hence minimum pulling force is F_min = (mu * mg) / sqrt(1 + mu^2). Option (b).

48. When a body slides down an inclined plane with coefficient of friction mu and inclination theta > lambda, its acceleration is given by:

2. A chain of length L is placed on a horizontal table. If the coefficient of static friction is mu, the maximum length of the chain that can hang over the edge without sliding is:
  • (a) \frac{\mu L}{1 + \mu}
  • (b) \frac{L}{1 + \mu}
  • (c) \frac{\mu L}{1 - \mu}
  • (d) \mu L
Official Key: (a) \frac{\mu L}{1 + \mu}
Independent Derivation: Let hanging length be y. Weight of hanging section = (M/L)*y*g. Weight of portion on table = (M/L)*(L - y)*g. Max static friction force f_s = mu * N = mu * (M/L)*(L - y)*g. At verge of sliding: (M/L)*y*g = mu * (M/L)*(L - y)*g => y = mu*(L - y) => y*(1 + mu) = mu*L => y = (mu * L) / (1 + mu). Option (a).

7. A boy pulls a 5kg block along a 20m long horizontal surface at a constant velocity by applying a horizontal force F. If the coefficient of kinetic friction is 0.2, how much work does the boy do on the block? (take g = 10 ms^-2):

Circular Dynamics & Pseudo Forces
TH-1. In a death well or rotor of radius R, the minimum angular speed omega at which a person of mass m can remain pinned to the vertical cylindrical wall with static friction coefficient mu is:
  • (a) \sqrt{g/(\mu R)}
  • (b) \sqrt{\mu g/R}
  • (c) \sqrt{g/R}
  • (d) \sqrt{\mu R/g}
Official Key: (a) \sqrt{g/(\mu R)}
Independent Derivation: Normal reaction N from the cylinder wall provides centripetal force: N = m * omega^2 * R. Vertical upward static friction f_s balances gravitational weight: f_s = m*g. To prevent slipping downward: f_s <= mu * N => m*g <= mu * m * omega^2 * R => omega^2 >= g / (mu * R) => omega_min = sqrt(g / (mu * R)). Option (a).

TH-2. On a banked circular track of radius R and banking angle theta, the optimum speed to round the curve without any reliance on lateral friction is:
  • (a) \sqrt{R g \tan\theta}
  • (b) \sqrt{R g \cot\theta}
  • (c) \sqrt{R g \sin\theta}
  • (d) R g \tan\theta
Official Key: (a) \sqrt{R g \tan\theta}
Independent Derivation: When friction is absent, normal force N components provide vertical balance and centripetal acceleration: N*cos(theta) = m*g and N*sin(theta) = m*v^2 / R. Dividing the two equations: tan(theta) = v^2 / (R*g) => v = sqrt(R*g*tan(theta)). Option (a).
