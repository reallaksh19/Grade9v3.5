### Q-PHY-11-MIP-P16-L1-06 (PDF Page 16 / Book 103)
- **Section**: LEVEL-I | **Type**: SINGLE_CHOICE
- **Stem**: A person projects a bottle into a dustbin at the same height as he is 2m away at an angle of 45°. The velocity of projection is (in m/s):
- **Options**: (a) g, (b) \sqrt{g}, (c) 2g, (d) \sqrt{2g}
- **Official Answer Key**: `(d) \sqrt{2g}`
- **Independent Derivation**: Range formula on horizontal ground is R = (u^2 * sin(2*theta)) / g. For theta = 45°, sin(90°) = 1, so R = u^2 / g. Given R = 2m, we have 2 = u^2 / g => u^2 = 2g => u = sqrt(2g). Option (d).
- **Hardness**: EASY (C1, R0, M0, A0, S0)

### Q-PHY-11-MIP-P16-L1-07 (PDF Page 16 / Book 103)
- **Section**: LEVEL-I | **Type**: SINGLE_CHOICE
- **Stem**: A ball of mass 'm' is thrown vertically upwards. Another ball of mass '2m' is thrown up making an angle 'theta' with the vertical. Both of them stay in air for the same time. Their maximum heights are in the ratio:
- **Options**: (a) 2:1, (b) 1:2, (c) 1:cos\theta, (d) 1:1
- **Official Answer Key**: `(d) 1:1`
- **Independent Derivation**: Time of flight depends purely on vertical velocity component: T = 2*u_y / g. Since T1 = T2, u_1y = u_2y. Maximum height H = u_y^2 / (2g) also depends strictly on u_y. Hence H1 : H2 = u_1y^2 : u_2y^2 = 1:1, independent of mass or launch angle. Option (d).
- **Hardness**: EASY (C1, R1, M0, A0, S1)

### Q-PHY-11-MIP-P16-L1-08 (PDF Page 16 / Book 103)
- **Section**: LEVEL-I | **Type**: SINGLE_CHOICE
- **Stem**: From the top of a building 80m high, a ball is thrown horizontally which hits the ground at a distance. The line joining the top of the building to the point where it hits the ground makes an angle of 45° with the ground. Initial velocity of projection of the ball is (g=10 m/s^2):
- **Options**: (a) 10 m/s, (b) 15 m/s, (c) 20 m/s, (d) 30 m/s
- **Official Answer Key**: `(c) 20 m/s`
- **Independent Derivation**: Line from top of building to impact point makes 45° with ground => tan(45°) = height / range => 1 = 80 / x => x = 80m. Time to fall height h = 80m is t = sqrt(2h/g) = sqrt(160/10) = 4s. Horizontal velocity u = x / t = 80 / 4 = 20 m/s. Option (c).
- **Hardness**: MEDIUM (C1, R1, M1, A0, S1)

### Q-PHY-11-MIP-P16-L1-09 (PDF Page 16 / Book 103)
- **Section**: LEVEL-I | **Type**: SINGLE_CHOICE
- **Stem**: A stone is projected from the top of a tower with velocity 20 m/s making an angle of elevation of 30° with the horizontal. If the total time of flight is 5s and g=10 m/s^2, then:
- **Options**: (a) the height of the tower is 75m, (b) the maximum height of the stone from the ground is 80m, (c) both the above are true, (d) the height of the tower is 120m
- **Official Answer Key**: `(c) both the above are true`
- **Independent Derivation**: u_y = 20 * sin(30°) = 10 m/s. Vertical displacement upon reaching ground: -h = u_y*t - 0.5*g*t^2 = 10(5) - 5(25) = 50 - 125 = -75m => h = 75m. Maximum height above tower: h_max = u_y^2 / (2g) = 100 / 20 = 5m. Height above ground = 75 + 5 = 80m. Thus both statements (a) and (b) are true. Option (c).
- **Hardness**: MEDIUM (C2, R1, M1, A1, S0)

### Q-PHY-11-MIP-P16-L1-10 (PDF Page 16 / Book 103)

- **Options**: (a) 0°, (b) 30°, (c) 45°, (d) 60°
- **Official Answer Key**: `(a) 0°`

### Q-PHY-11-MIP-P17-L1-21 (PDF Page 17 / Book 104)

- **Stem**: A particle of mass 1 kg is projected at an angle 45° to the horizontal with an initial velocity of 20 m/s. Change in momentum during its time of flight is:
- **Options**: (a) 10\sqrt{2} kg m/s, (b) 20\sqrt{2} kg m/s, (c) 30\sqrt{2} kg m/s, (d) 40\sqrt{2} kg m/s
- **Official Answer Key**: `(b) 20\sqrt{2} kg m/s`

### Q-PHY-11-MIP-P18-L1-29 (PDF Page 18 / Book 105)
- **Section**: LEVEL-I | **Type**: SINGLE_CHOICE
- **Stem**: From the top of a tower of height 78.4m two stones are projected horizontally with 10 m/s and 20 m/s in opposite directions. On reaching the ground, their separation is:
- **Options**: (a) 60 m, (b) 100 m, (c) 120 m, (d) 200 m
- **Official Answer Key**: `(c) 120 m`

### Q-PHY-11-MIP-P21-L2-06 (PDF Page 21 / Book 108)
- **Section**: LEVEL-II | **Type**: SINGLE_CHOICE
- **Stem**: A swimmer can swim in still water at 4 km/h. To cross a river of width 1 km flowing at 2 km/h along the shortest possible path, in which direction should he swim?
- **Options**: (a) At an angle 120° to the water current, (b) At an angle 150° to the water current, (c) At an angle 90° to the water current, (d) None of these
- **Official Answer Key**: `(a) At an angle 120° to the water current`
- **Independent Derivation**: Shortest path requires zero drift: swimmer must head upstream at angle theta with the normal such that v_s * sin(theta) = v_r => 4 * sin(theta) = 2 => sin(theta) = 1/2 => theta = 30° upstream from normal. Total angle with respect to downstream water current = 90° + 30° = 120°. Option (a).
- **Hardness**: MEDIUM (C2, R2, M1, A1, S1)

### Q-PHY-11-MIP-P21-L2-07 (PDF Page 21 / Book 108)
- **Section**: LEVEL-II | **Type**: SINGLE_CHOICE
- **Stem**: A river flows with a speed greater than the maximum speed with which a person can swim in still water (v_r > v_s). He intends to cross the river by the shortest possible path. Which of the following is correct?
- **Options**: (a) He should start normal to the river bank, (b) He can reach the opposite point directly, (c) He should swim at sin(theta) = v_s/v_r upstream, (d) The man cannot reach the directly opposite point without drift
- **Official Answer Key**: `(d) The man cannot reach the directly opposite point without drift`
- **Independent Derivation**: To reach the directly opposite point, zero net drift is required, meaning v_s * sin(theta) = v_r => sin(theta) = v_r / v_s. But v_r > v_s implies sin(theta) > 1, which is impossible for any physical heading angle. Therefore, drift cannot be eliminated and the swimmer cannot reach the directly opposite point. Option (d).
- **Hardness**: MEDIUM (C2, R1, M1, A2, S1)

### Q-PHY-11-MIP-P22-L2-21 (PDF Page 22 / Book 109)
- **Section**: LEVEL-II | **Type**: SINGLE_CHOICE
- **Stem**: A projectile can have the same range R for two angles of projection. If t1 and t2 are the times of flight in the two cases, then:
- **Options**: (a) t1 * t2 \propto R, (b) t1 * t2 \propto R^2, (c) t1 * t2 \propto 1/R, (d) t1 * t2 \propto 1/R^2

### Q-PHY-11-MIP-P24-L2-43 (PDF Page 24 / Book 111)

- **Stem**: A particle is projected from the ground with an initial speed of u at an angle theta with horizontal. The average velocity between its launch and apex is:

### Q-PHY-11-MIP-P25-L2-47 (PDF Page 25 / Book 112)
- **Section**: LEVEL-II | **Type**: SINGLE_CHOICE
- **Stem**: The horizontal range and maximum height attained by a projectile are R and H, respectively. If a constant horizontal acceleration a_x = g/4 is imparted by wind, the new range is:
- **Options**: (a) R + H, (b) R + 2H, (c) R + 4H, (d) R + H/2
- **Official Answer Key**: `(a) R + H`
- **Independent Derivation**: Flight time is governed strictly by vertical gravity: T = 2*u_y / g. Additional horizontal displacement due to wind acceleration: Delta_x = 0.5 * a_x * T^2 = 0.5 * (g/4) * (2*u_y / g)^2 = (g/8) * (4 * u_y^2 / g^2) = u_y^2 / (2g) = H. Therefore, new total range R_new = R + Delta_x = R + H. Option (a).
