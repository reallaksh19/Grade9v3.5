### Q-PHY-11-NLM-P26-L1A-04 (PDF Page 26 / Book 163)
- **Section**: LEVEL-IA | **Type**: SINGLE_CHOICE
- **Stem**: When we catch a cricket ball, we catch it at the front and make the hands move with the ball backwards. Why is that?
- **Options**: (a) To reduce the impulse, (b) To increase the impulse, (c) To increase the time of contact, thereby increase the force, (d) To increase the time of contact, thereby decrease the force
- **Official Answer Key**: `(d) To increase the time of contact, thereby decrease the force`
- **Independent Derivation**: Impulse J = F_avg * Delta_t = Delta_p. Bringing the ball of fixed mass m and velocity v to rest involves a fixed change in momentum Delta_p. By moving the hands backward, contact duration Delta_t increases, which reduces the average impulsive force F_avg = Delta_p / Delta_t experienced by the hands. Option (d).
- **Hardness**: EASY (C1, R0, M0, A0, S0)

### Q-PHY-11-NLM-P32-L1B-12 (PDF Page 32 / Book 169)
- **Section**: LEVEL-1B | **Type**: SINGLE_CHOICE
- **Stem**: Two blocks of masses m and M are placed on a horizontal frictionless table connected by a light spring. Mass M is pulled to the right with a force F. If the acceleration of mass m is a, the acceleration of mass M will be:
- **Options**: (a) (F + ma)/M, (b) (F - ma)/M, (c) am/M, (d) F/M
- **Official Answer Key**: `(b) (F - ma)/M`
- **Independent Derivation**: The light spring transmits a force F_s that accelerates mass m with acceleration a, so F_s = m*a to the right. Option (b).

### Q-PHY-11-NLM-P33-L1B-16 (PDF Page 33 / Book 170)
- **Section**: LEVEL-1B | **Type**: SINGLE_CHOICE
- **Stem**: Two masses m1 and m2 (m1 > m2) are connected by a light inextensible string passing over a smooth massless pulley. The acceleration of the system is:
- **Options**: (a) \frac{m1 - m2}{m1 + m2}g, (b) \frac{m1 + m2}{m1 - m2}g, (c) \frac{2m1 m2}{m1 + m2}g, (d) \frac{m1 m2}{m1 + m2}g

### Q-PHY-11-NLM-P34-L1B-24 (PDF Page 34 / Book 171)

### Q-PHY-11-NLM-P42-L1C-18 (PDF Page 42 / Book 179)
- **Section**: LEVEL-1C | **Type**: SINGLE_CHOICE
- **Stem**: A block of mass m is placed on an inclined plane. With increase in inclination angle theta, the contact force exerted by the plane on the block (before slipping):
- **Options**: (a) Decreases, (b) Increases, (c) Remains constant equal to mg, (d) First increases then decreases
- **Official Answer Key**: `(c) Remains constant equal to mg`
- **Independent Derivation**: The contact force R is the vector resultant of normal reaction N = mg*cos(theta) and static friction f_s = mg*sin(theta). Since the block is stationary in static equilibrium, vector sum R + mg = 0 => |R| = sqrt(N^2 + f_s^2) = sqrt((mg*cos(theta))^2 + (mg*sin(theta))^2) = mg * sqrt(cos^2(theta) + sin^2(theta)) = mg = constant. Option (c).

### Q-PHY-11-NLM-P43-L1C-28 (PDF Page 43 / Book 180)
- **Section**: LEVEL-1C | **Type**: SINGLE_CHOICE
- **Stem**: The minimum force required to slide a block of mass m on a horizontal plane having friction coefficient mu is:
- **Options**: (a) \mu mg, (b) \frac{\mu mg}{\sqrt{1 + \mu^2}}, (c) \mu mg \sqrt{1 + \mu^2}, (d) \frac{mg}{\sqrt{1 + \mu^2}}
- **Official Answer Key**: `(b) \frac{\mu mg}{\sqrt{1 + \mu^2}}`
- **Independent Derivation**: Pulling at an upward angle theta: F*cos(theta) = mu * N = mu * (mg - F*sin(theta)) => F = (mu * mg) / (cos(theta) + mu*sin(theta)). Denominator is maximized when tan(theta) = mu, where max denominator value is sqrt(1 + mu^2). Hence minimum pulling force is F_min = (mu * mg) / sqrt(1 + mu^2). Option (b).
- **Hardness**: HARD (C2, R1, M2, A2, S1)

### Q-PHY-11-NLM-P44-L1C-48 (PDF Page 44 / Book 181)

- **Stem**: When a body slides down an inclined plane with coefficient of friction mu and inclination theta > lambda, its acceleration is given by:

### Q-PHY-11-NLM-P45-L1D-02 (PDF Page 45 / Book 182)

- **Stem**: A chain of length L is placed on a horizontal table. If the coefficient of static friction is mu, the maximum length of the chain that can hang over the edge without sliding is:
- **Options**: (a) \frac{\mu L}{1 + \mu}, (b) \frac{L}{1 + \mu}, (c) \frac{\mu L}{1 - \mu}, (d) \mu L

### Q-PHY-11-NLM-P03-TH-01 (PDF Page 3 / Book 140)
- **Section**: THEORY_CIRCULAR | **Type**: SINGLE_CHOICE
- **Stem**: In a death well or rotor of radius R, the minimum angular speed omega at which a person of mass m can remain pinned to the vertical cylindrical wall with static friction coefficient mu is:
- **Options**: (a) \sqrt{g/(\mu R)}, (b) \sqrt{\mu g/R}, (c) \sqrt{g/R}, (d) \sqrt{\mu R/g}
- **Official Answer Key**: `(a) \sqrt{g/(\mu R)}`
- **Independent Derivation**: Normal reaction N from the cylinder wall provides centripetal force: N = m * omega^2 * R. Vertical upward static friction f_s balances gravitational weight: f_s = m*g. ### Q-PHY-11-NLM-P06-TH-02 (PDF Page 6 / Book 143)

- **Stem**: On a banked circular track of radius R and banking angle theta, the optimum speed to round the curve without any reliance on lateral friction is:

## 5. Ranked Question Clinics

### Rank 1: Q-PHY-11-NLM-P32-L1B-12 — Connected Blocks with Light Spring Under External Pull
- **Pedagogical Bottleneck**: Failing to recognize that spring force F_s = ma acts as an internal constraint force opposing the pulled mass M.

### Rank 2: Q-PHY-11-NLM-P45-L1D-07 — Work Done Against Kinetic Friction at Constant Velocity
- **Pedagogical Bottleneck**: Assuming zero net acceleration implies zero work done by the external pulling agent.

### Rank 3: Q-PHY-11-NLM-P41-L1C-07 — Self-Adjusting Nature of Static Friction on Abrupt Stop
- **Pedagogical Bottleneck**: Believing friction remains at its maximum limiting value rather than vanishing when no tending force exists.

### Rank 4: Q-PHY-11-NLM-P03-TH-01 — Rotor / Death Well Minimum Angular Frequency
- **Pedagogical Bottleneck**: Inverting the roles of normal reaction (providing centripetal acceleration) and static friction (balancing gravity).
