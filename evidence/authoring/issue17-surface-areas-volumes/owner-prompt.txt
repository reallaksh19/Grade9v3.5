# THE 10 QUESTIONS

---

## Q1 — Cube: total surface area

A wooden cube has edge length 7 cm.

Find its total surface area.

---

## Q2 — Cylinder: diameter trap

A cylindrical water bottle has diameter 14 cm and height 20 cm.

Find its curved surface area.

---

## Q3 — Open cylindrical container

A cylindrical bucket is open at the top.

Its internal radius is 7 cm and its height is 18 cm.

Find the area of metal sheet required to make the bucket, ignoring the thickness of the sheet.

---

## Q4 — Cone: slant height required

A conical tent has radius 7 m and vertical height 24 m.

Find:

1. its slant height;
2. the area of canvas required to make the tent.

The base of the tent is open.

---

## Q5 — Hemisphere: curved versus total surface

A hemispherical bowl has radius 10.5 cm.

Find the area of its inner surface.

Explain why the circular base is not included in your calculation.

---

## Q6 — Composite solid with hidden surface

A toy is made by mounting a cone on a hemisphere.

The cone and hemisphere have the same radius, 3.5 cm.

The vertical height of the cone is 12 cm.

Find the total **exposed** surface area of the toy.

The circular face where the cone and hemisphere join is not exposed.

---

## Q7 — Hollow/open container reasoning

A cylindrical vessel of radius 7 cm and height 25 cm is open at the top.

It is to be painted:

- on its entire outer curved surface; and
- on the outside of its circular base.

The inside is not painted.

Find the area to be painted.

Before calculating, state exactly which surfaces are included.

---

## Q8 — Volume and capacity with unit conversion

A rectangular water tank is 2 m long, 1.5 m wide and 1.2 m deep.

It is filled to 75% of its total capacity.

How many litres of water are in the tank?

Show the unit conversion clearly.

---

## Q9 — Recasting using conservation of volume

A solid metallic sphere of radius 6 cm is melted and recast into identical solid spheres of radius 2 cm.

Assume that no metal is lost.

How many smaller spheres are formed?

Explain why surface area is not conserved in this process.

---

## Q10 — Same radius and height: reasoning without unnecessary calculation

A cone and a cylinder have the same base radius and the same height.

The volume of the cylinder is 924 cm³.

Find the volume of the cone.

Then explain why the radius and height do **not** need to be calculated separately.
