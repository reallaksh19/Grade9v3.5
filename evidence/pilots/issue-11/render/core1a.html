Surface Areas and Volumes

Concept route

  1. Choose the surface before choosing the formula
    1. Surface-area formulas are not selected by the solid's name alone
  2. Build slant height before cone canvas area
    1. The cone's vertical height is not the length that appears in πrl; a right-triangle cross-section must first construct the slant height.
  3. Expose the outside; hide the join
    1. Joining two solids changes the boundary
  4. Keep volume, percentage and capacity units in sequence
    1. A capacity problem is safest as a unit-preserving chain
  5. Conserve material volume, not exposed surface
    1. The conservation equation is about material volume; surface area is a boundary measure and can change dramatically when one object becomes many.

    1. When two formulas share the same πr²h, the unknown dimensions do not need to be recovered; compare the coefficients directly.

Choose the surface before choosing the formula

Subject: MathematicsTopic: Surface Areas and VolumesConcept: Choose the surface before choosing the formulaConcept difficulty: Medium concept
1

Surface-area formulas are not selected by the solid's name alone

Medium concept
Section 1 of 6Next →

The key step

Surface-area formulas are not selected by the solid's name alone: first inventory the actual material or exposed boundary, then assign one area term to each included piece.

You need

  • Can compute square and circle areas.
  • Can distinguish radius from diameter.
  • Can substitute into a known formula with units.
  • Compute areas of squares and rectangles from dimensions. (Mathematics)
  • Relate a circle's radius and diameter. (Mathematics)
  • Use circle area and circumference formulas. (Mathematics)

  1. Inventory every physical boundary piece before writing an area formula.
    Why valid: Openings and internal joins are not material/exposed surfaces, so boundary selection must precede arithmetic.
    Result: A named list of included and excluded surfaces.
  2. The step the hard question turns onMatch each included piece to its familiar area relation.
    Why valid: A surface sum is valid only when each term represents one distinct included region.
    Result: One formula term per included surface.
  3. Re-read the wording and check that the formula has exactly the same number and type of surface terms.
    Why valid: This catches closed-vs-open and curved-vs-total errors independently of arithmetic.

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it

Surface area is the sum of the areas of the material or exposed pieces that the situation includes.

  • Count each included external/material surface exactly once.
  • Openings and hidden joining faces contribute no exposed sheet or painted area.

Watch one

Q3 · Owner-supplied question, verbatim

A cylindrical bucket is open at the top. Its internal radius is 7 cm and its height is 18 cm. Find the area of metal sheet required to make the bucket, ignoring the thickness of the sheet.

  1. Include the curved wall and one circular base; exclude the open top.

    Why valid: Metal sheet is needed only where material exists; the top opening has no circular sheet.

    Result: included surfaces: curved wall + bottom circle

  2. Add 2πrh + πr² = 2π(7)(18) + π(7²).

    Why valid: Areas of distinct sheet pieces add without overlap.

    Result: 301π cm²

  3. Recount the boundary: one lateral surface, one base, zero top.

    Why valid: The physical boundary description independently verifies the formula terms.

    Result: 301π cm² = 946 cm² if π = 22/7

Result

301π cm², which is 946 cm² if π = 22/7.

Check

The included pieces are 2πrh + πr² = 252π + 49π = 301π cm²; there is exactly one base because the top is open.

A tempting wrong path

  • The solid's standard total-surface-area formula should always be used unchanged.

Diagnose

  • If the top is open or a joining face is hidden, which physical surface would that extra formula term represent?

Repair

  • Trace the actual boundary first; add only terms that correspond to present or exposed material.

  1. 1 · CheckSay aloud which surfaces are included before looking at any formula.
  2. 2 · ApplyFor an open cylinder, write the area expression without substituting numbers.
  3. 3 · ConnectUse the same boundary-first rule for paint, metal sheet, canvas, or exposed-area questions.

Build slant height before cone canvas area

Subject: MathematicsTopic: Surface Areas and VolumesConcept: Build slant height before cone canvas areaConcept difficulty: Medium concept
1

The cone's vertical height is not the length that appears in πrl; a right-triangle cross-section must first construct the slant height.

Medium concept
Section 2 of 6← PreviousNext →

The key step

The cone's vertical height is not the length that appears in πrl; a right-triangle cross-section must first construct the slant height.

You need

  • Can use the Pythagorean theorem.
  • Can identify radius and vertical height in a right cone.
  • Can use curved cone area πrl once l is known.
  • Use the Pythagorean theorem in a right triangle. (Mathematics)
  • Use circle area and circumference formulas. (Mathematics)

This unit builds toward

  • Q4 · D3
    The move learners miss: Compute l = √(7² + 24²) = 25 m. Try it in Core2
  1. Take an axial cross-section and name r, h and the sloping side l.
    Why valid: The centre-to-rim radius is perpendicular to the vertical axis in a right cone.
    Result: A right triangle with legs r and h and hypotenuse l.
  2. Write l² = r² + h².
    Why valid: Pythagoras applies to the axial right triangle.
    Result: A relation that determines l.
  3. Evaluate the slant height from the given r and h.
    Why valid: The positive square root is the physical length.
    Result: The cone's slant height.
  4. The step the hard question turns onUse l, not h, in πrl and omit πr² when the base is open.
    Why valid: Curved canvas follows the sloping surface; an open base contributes no canvas.
    Result: A boundary-correct cone canvas model.

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it
l2=r2+h2;cone curved area=πrl

In an axial cone cross-section, radius and vertical height are perpendicular and determine the slant height used by lateral area.

  • The cone is right circular.
  • Use πrl for curved area; add a base only when a material base is included.

Watch one

Q4 · Owner-supplied question, verbatim

A conical tent has radius 7 m and vertical height 24 m. Find: 1. its slant height; 2. the area of canvas required to make the tent. The base of the tent is open.

  1. Form the right triangle with radius 7 m, vertical height 24 m, and slant height l.

    Why valid: A cross-section through the cone axis gives a right triangle from centre, rim and apex.

    Result: l² = 7² + 24²

  2. Compute l = √(7² + 24²) = 25 m.

    Why valid: Pythagoras applies to the right-triangle cross-section.

    Result: l = 25 m

  3. Use only curved cone area πrl for the canvas.

    Why valid: The tent base is open, so the circular base is not canvas.

    Result: canvas area = 175π m²

  4. Check both the 7-24-25 triangle and the open-base boundary.

    Why valid: 25² = 7² + 24², and the area expression has no πr² base term.

    Result: 175π m² = 550 m² if π = 22/7

Result

Slant height = 25 m; canvas area = 175π m², which is 550 m² if π = 22/7.

Check

7² + 24² = 625 = 25², and the open tent uses πrl = π(7)(25) with no πr² base term.

The cone's vertical height is not the length that appears in πrl; a right-triangle cross-section must first construct the slant height.

A tempting wrong path

  • Vertical height h can be substituted for slant height l in πrl.

Diagnose

  • In an axial cross-section, which segment lies along the canvas: the vertical axis or the sloping side?

Repair

  • Construct l with Pythagoras first, then use l in the lateral-area relation.

1-2-3 quick check

  1. 1 · CheckVerify that a positive-radius cone has slant height greater than vertical height.
  2. 2 · ApplyFor r=3 and h=4, find l before writing the curved-area expression.
  3. 3 · ConnectUse the same slant-height bridge whenever a cone problem supplies r and h but asks for lateral area.

Try it with less support

A right cone has r=5 cm and h=12 cm. What slant height must be used in πrl?

Expose the outside; hide the join

Subject: MathematicsTopic: Surface Areas and VolumesConcept: Expose the outside; hide the joinConcept difficulty: Hard concept
1

Joining two solids changes the boundary

Hard concept
Section 3 of 6← PreviousNext →

The key step

Joining two solids changes the boundary: the coincident contact faces stop being exposed, so component total areas cannot simply be added.

You need

  • Can find cone slant height with Pythagoras.
  • Can use cone and hemisphere curved-area formulas.
  • Can distinguish external from internal surfaces.
  • Use the Pythagorean theorem in a right triangle. (Mathematics)
  • Use circle area and circumference formulas. (Mathematics)

This unit builds toward

  • Q6 · D3
    The move learners miss: Include cone curved area and hemisphere curved area, but omit the common circular join. Try it in Core2
  1. Mark the common circular contact face as internal.
    Why valid: A point on the join cannot be reached from outside the assembled toy without passing through material.
    Result: The join is excluded from exposed area.
  2. Construct the cone's slant height from its radius and vertical height.
    Why valid: The cone lateral area depends on slant height, not vertical height.
    Result: A usable l for the cone.
  3. Write one term for each exposed curved component: πrl and 2πr².
    Why valid: The external boundary consists of the cone lateral surface and hemisphere curved surface.
    Result: An exposed-area sum with no interface term.
  4. The step the hard question turns onTrace the assembled outside and match it term-for-term to the area expression.
    Why valid: Boundary tracing independently detects a hidden-face double count.
    Result: A verified exposed-surface model.

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it
Aexposed=sum of visible external surface areas

When solids are joined, coincident contact faces become internal and are omitted from exposed surface area.

  • The joining face is fully covered by the other component.
  • Only surfaces visible from outside are counted.
l2=r2+h2;cone curved area=πrl

In an axial cone cross-section, radius and vertical height are perpendicular and determine the slant height used by lateral area.

  • The cone is right circular.
  • Use πrl for curved area; add a base only when a material base is included.
cube TSA=6a2;cylinder curved=2πrh;circle=πr2;hemisphere curved=2πr2

Surface area is the sum of the areas of the material or exposed pieces that the situation includes.

  • Count each included external/material surface exactly once.
  • Openings and hidden joining faces contribute no exposed sheet or painted area.

Watch one

Q6 · Owner-supplied question, verbatim

A toy is made by mounting a cone on a hemisphere. The cone and hemisphere have the same radius, 3.5 cm. The vertical height of the cone is 12 cm. Find the total **exposed** surface area of the toy. The circular face where the cone and hemisphere join is not exposed.

  1. Find the cone slant height l = √(12² + 3.5²) = 12.5 cm.

    Why valid: Radius and vertical height form perpendicular legs in the axial cross-section.

    Result: l=12.5 cm

  2. Include cone curved area and hemisphere curved area, but omit the common circular join.

    Why valid: The join lies inside the composite toy and is not exposed to the outside.

    Result: exposed pieces: cone lateral + hemisphere curved

  3. Add πrl + 2πr² = π(3.5)(12.5) + 2π(3.5)².

    Why valid: The two exposed curved surfaces are disjoint and their areas add.

    Result: 68.25π cm²

  4. Check that no πr² join term appears and that the slant height exceeds the vertical height.

    Why valid: A hidden interface contributes no exposed area, and l=12.5 cm > 12 cm is geometrically consistent.

    Result: 68.25π cm² = 214.5 cm² if π = 22/7

Result

68.25π cm², which is 214.5 cm² if π = 22/7.

Check

The slant height is √(12² + 3.5²) = 12.5 cm; exposed area is π(3.5)(12.5) + 2π(3.5)² = 68.25π cm², with the common circle hidden.

Joining two solids changes the boundary

A tempting wrong path

  • Add the full surface areas of both component solids, including the circular face where they touch.

Diagnose

  • Can the joining circle be touched from outside the assembled toy without separating the solids?

Repair

  • Remove every coincident interface from the exposed boundary; count only surfaces visible from outside.

1-2-3 quick check

  1. 1 · CheckPoint to each term in the exposed-area sum on the outside of the assembled solid.
  2. 2 · ApplyIf two cylinders are glued base-to-base, identify which circular faces disappear from exposed area.
  3. 3 · ConnectUse the same hidden-interface rule for composite solids built from any joined shapes.

Try it with less support

A cone is fixed to a circular face of another solid. What happens to that common circle in exposed surface area?

Keep volume, percentage and capacity units in sequence

Subject: MathematicsTopic: Surface Areas and VolumesConcept: Keep volume, percentage and capacity units in sequenceConcept difficulty: Medium concept
1

A capacity problem is safest as a unit-preserving chain

Medium concept
Section 4 of 6← PreviousNext →

The key step

A capacity problem is safest as a unit-preserving chain: full volume in m³ → filled fraction in m³ → litres.

You need

  • Can calculate rectangular-prism volume.
  • Can apply a percentage as a fraction.
  • Knows or can use 1 m³ = 1000 L.
  • Compute rectangular-prism volume. (Mathematics)
  • Interpret a percentage as a multiplicative fraction. (Mathematics)
  • Convert cubic metres to litres. (Mathematics)

This unit builds toward

  • Q8 · D2
    The move learners miss: Take 75% to get 2.7 m³, then use 1 m³ = 1000 L. Try it in Core2
  1. Compute full tank volume in cubic metres.
    Why valid: All three dimensions are in metres, so lwh yields m³.
    Result: Full capacity in m³.
  2. The step the hard question turns onApply the fill fraction to the full volume.
    Why valid: A 75% fill contains 0.75 of total volume.
    Result: Filled volume in m³.
  3. Convert m³ to litres and reverse-check the conversion.
    Why valid: 1 m³ = 1000 L, so dividing the litre result by 1000 must recover the filled m³.
    Result: Capacity stated and checked in litres.

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it
V=lwh;filled volume=fraction×V;1m3=1000L

Tank capacity is volume; a fill percentage scales that volume before or after an equivalent unit conversion.

  • Dimensions are in compatible units before multiplication.

Watch one

Q8 · Owner-supplied question, verbatim

A rectangular water tank is 2 m long, 1.5 m wide and 1.2 m deep. It is filled to 75% of its total capacity. How many litres of water are in the tank? Show the unit conversion clearly.

  1. Compute the tank's full volume: 2 × 1.5 × 1.2 = 3.6 m³.

    Why valid: A rectangular tank's capacity is length × width × depth.

    Result: 3.6 m³

  2. Take 75% to get 2.7 m³, then use 1 m³ = 1000 L.

    Why valid: The fill fraction scales volume, and cubic-metre-to-litre conversion is multiplicative.

    Result: 2700 L

  3. Convert back: 2700 L = 2.7 m³, which is three-quarters of 3.6 m³.

    Why valid: The reverse conversion and fraction check test both transformations independently.

    Result: 2.7 / 3.6 = 0.75

Result

2700 litres.

Check

The tank volume is 3.6 m³; 75% is 2.7 m³, and 2.7 × 1000 = 2700 L.

A capacity problem is safest as a unit-preserving chain

A tempting wrong path

  • Treat 75% as multiplication by 75 or convert 1 m³ to 100 L.

Diagnose

  • What decimal represents 75%, and how many 1-litre cubes fit in a 1 m × 1 m × 1 m cube?

Repair

  • Use 0.75 as the fill factor and 1000 L per m³; keep units written at each step.

1-2-3 quick check

  1. 1 · CheckState the unit after l×w×h before applying any conversion.
  2. 2 · ApplyConvert 0.5 m³ to litres and back.
  3. 3 · ConnectUse the same chain for partially filled tanks, pools and containers.

Try it with less support

A 4 m³ tank is 25% full. How many litres does it contain?

Conserve material volume, not exposed surface

Subject: MathematicsTopic: Surface Areas and VolumesConcept: Conserve material volume, not exposed surfaceConcept difficulty: Hard concept
1

The conservation equation is about material volume; surface area is a boundary measure and can change dramatically when one object becomes many.

Hard concept
Section 5 of 6← PreviousNext →

The key step

The conservation equation is about material volume; surface area is a boundary measure and can change dramatically when one object becomes many.

You need

  • Can use sphere volume and surface-area formulas.
  • Can simplify a ratio of powers.
  • Understands that no-loss recasting preserves amount of material.
  • Use the sphere volume formula. (Mathematics)
  • Simplify multiplicative ratios. (Mathematics)

This unit builds toward

  • Q9 · D3 · the hardest question in this set
    The move learners miss: Use conservation of volume, not surface area, because the same metal is melted and recast with no loss. Try it in Core2
  1. Choose volume as the conserved quantity under no-loss recasting.
    Why valid: Melting and reshaping do not create or remove material, so occupied volume is preserved.
    Result: V_before = V_after.
  2. Write V_large = nV_small and cancel the common sphere-volume constant.
    Why valid: Both sides contain the factor 4π/3.
    Result: n = R³/r³.
  3. Evaluate the cubic radius ratio to get the number of spheres.
    Why valid: Volume scales with the cube of every linear scale factor.
    Result: A recast count n.
  4. The step the hard question turns onCompare total surface area separately and then recheck total volume.
    Why valid: Surface area is not the conserved equation; a separate calculation exposes the change while the volume balance must remain exact.
    Result: Volume conserved; surface area free to change.

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it
Vbefore=Vafter;n=(Rr)3

With no material loss, recasting conserves volume; sphere volume scales with the cube of radius.

  • No metal is lost.
  • All smaller spheres have the same radius.

Watch one

Q9 · Owner-supplied question, verbatim

A solid metallic sphere of radius 6 cm is melted and recast into identical solid spheres of radius 2 cm. Assume that no metal is lost. How many smaller spheres are formed? Explain why surface area is not conserved in this process.

  1. Use conservation of volume, not surface area, because the same metal is melted and recast with no loss.

    Why valid: Recasting changes shape and exposed boundary but not the amount of material.

    Result: V_large = n V_small

  2. Cancel the common 4π/3 factor to get n = (6/2)³ = 27.

    Why valid: Sphere volume is proportional to the cube of radius.

    Result: n = 27

  3. Compare surface areas to explain why surface area is not conserved.

    Why valid: Surface area measures exposed boundary, and splitting one object into many adds boundary.

    Result: new total surface 432π cm² versus original 144π cm²

  4. Substitute n=27 into the volume equation.

    Why valid: A valid recast count must reproduce the original material volume exactly.

    Result: 27 × (4/3)π(2³) = (4/3)π(6³)

Result

27 smaller spheres; surface area is not conserved because recasting preserves material volume, while creating more pieces changes the total exposed surface.

Check

27 × (4/3)π(2³) = (4/3)π(6³), while total new surface area is 27 × 4π(2²) = 432π cm² versus 144π cm² originally.

The conservation equation is about material volume; surface area is a boundary measure and can change dramatically when one object becomes many.

A tempting wrong path

  • Surface area should be conserved because the same metal is used.

Diagnose

  • If one cube is cut into eight smaller cubes, does the material amount change? Does the total area of all exposed faces change?

Repair

  • Conserve volume for no-loss recasting; treat total surface area as a separate boundary quantity that generally changes.

1-2-3 quick check

  1. 1 · CheckSubstitute the sphere count back into nV_small = V_large.
  2. 2 · ApplyIf every small radius is half the original, predict the number from cubic scaling.
  3. 3 · ConnectUse volume conservation for melting/recasting regardless of the new solid's shape.

Try it with less support

A sphere is recast into spheres with half its radius. How many are formed if no material is lost?

Cancel shared dimensions before solving for them

Subject: MathematicsTopic: Surface Areas and VolumesConcept: Cancel shared dimensions before solving for themConcept difficulty: Medium concept
1

Medium concept
Section 6 of 6← Previous

The key step

When two formulas share the same πr²h, the unknown dimensions do not need to be recovered; compare the coefficients directly.

You need

  • Knows cone and cylinder volume formulas.
  • Can compare expressions with a common nonzero factor.
  • Can use a one-third multiplicative relation.
  • Use a fixed multiplicative relationship between quantities. (Mathematics)

This unit builds toward

  • Q10 · D2
    The move learners miss: Take one-third of 924 cm³. Try it in Core2
  1. Write both volume formulas using the same symbols r and h.
    Why valid: The problem states the radius and height are shared.
    Result: Two expressions with common factor πr²h.
  2. The step the hard question turns onFactor out the common πr²h and compare coefficients 1 and 1/3.
    Why valid: Equal nonzero common factors do not affect the ratio.
    Result: V_cone = V_cyl/3.
  3. Use the known cylinder volume directly and reverse-check by multiplying the cone result by 3.
    Why valid: The fixed ratio is sufficient; separate r and h values are unnecessary.
    Result: A checked cone volume.

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it
Vcylinder=πr2h;Vcone=13πr2h=13Vcylinder

For the same base radius and height, cone and cylinder volumes differ only by the factor 1/3.

  • Cone and cylinder have the same base radius and the same height.

Watch one

Q10 · Owner-supplied question, verbatim

A cone and a cylinder have the same base radius and the same height. The volume of the cylinder is 924 cm³. Find the volume of the cone. Then explain why the radius and height do **not** need to be calculated separately.

  1. Write the two volume relations with the shared radius and height: V_cyl = πr²h and V_cone = (1/3)πr²h.

    Why valid: The cone and cylinder have the same base radius and height, so πr²h is the same factor in both.

    Result: V_cone = (1/3)V_cyl

  2. Take one-third of 924 cm³.

    Why valid: The common πr²h factor cancels the need to determine r and h separately.

    Result: V_cone = 308 cm³

  3. Multiply the cone volume by 3 and recover the cylinder volume.

    Why valid: The fixed 1:3 volume ratio is an independent check under the same-radius, same-height condition.

    Result: 3 × 308 = 924 cm³

Result

308 cm³; the radius and height are unnecessary because the cone and cylinder share the same πr²h factor.

Check

3 × 308 = 924, exactly the cylinder volume, matching V_cone = (1/3)V_cylinder for equal radius and height.

When two formulas share the same πr²h, the unknown dimensions do not need to be recovered; compare the coefficients directly.

A tempting wrong path

  • A numerical radius and height must be found before any cone volume can be calculated.

Diagnose

  • How many equations are available for r and h separately, and what common factor appears in both volume formulas?

Repair

  • Compare the formulas first; the shared πr²h cancels from the ratio, so no separate dimensions are needed.

1-2-3 quick check

  1. 1 · CheckExplain in one sentence why πr²h is identical for both solids.
  2. 2 · ApplyIf a matching cylinder has volume 300 cm³, state the cone volume without finding r or h.
  3. 3 · ConnectLook for shared multiplicative factors before solving unnecessary intermediate variables in other formula comparisons.
