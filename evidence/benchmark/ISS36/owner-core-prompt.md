## A — Question set Q1–Q10

Grade 11 Chemistry: chemical bonding and hybridisation. Questions involving amide or radical models include advanced extensions.

### Q1

Nitrate, NO3−, is planar and has three equivalent N–O bonds. Its three conventional Lewis contributors place one N=O bond in different positions. Choose between a permanently localised single/double-bond picture and a delocalised pi description. Explain which observations discriminate between them, and state separately the introductory sigma-framework hybridisation model at nitrogen.

### Q2

Use the supplied nitrate description: three planar sigma bond directions around nitrogen and a pi system delocalised across the N–O framework. Translate the three Lewis contributors into one qualitative orbital diagram. Distinguish the sigma framework from the delocalised pi system, and explain what is preserved and what must not be interpreted as a molecule switching between drawings.

### Q3

For propadiene (allene), H2C=C=CH2, apply local electron-domain counting at each of the three carbon atoms. Assign the introductory hybridisation of each carbon and show how the two mutually perpendicular unhybridised p orbitals on the central carbon constrain the relative planes of the terminal CH2 groups.

### Q4

Ethene is planar; propadiene has terminal CH2 groups in perpendicular planes. Build a connected account from each carbon's local sigma framework, the required pi overlaps and the p-orbital directions. Explain why copying the planar ethene sketch to allene fails.

### Q5

For the benchmark, use these supplied simplified observations, not an exam-source claim: CO2 is linear with two equivalent C–O bonds; NO3− is planar with three equivalent N–O bonds; NH3 is pyramidal with three equivalent N–H bonds. Assess the claim 'Equivalent bonds imply sp2 at the central atom.' Use counterexamples and valid warrants, and distinguish equivalence, electron-domain count and hybridisation as a model description.

### Q6

For propyne, CH3–C≡CH, assign the introductory hybridisation of each carbon and count the sigma and pi bonds in the entire molecule. Show a check that distinguishes the triple bond's components from the local domain count.

### Q7

The benchmark supplies that carbonate, CO3²−, is planar and has three equivalent C–O bonds; its three conventional Lewis contributors place one C=O bond in different positions. Construct one qualitative description of its sigma framework and delocalised pi system. State why no single contributor should be read as the complete physical picture.

### Q8

Compare rotation about the central C–C bond in ethane with twisting about the C=C bond in ethene. Use the sigma and pi overlap arrangements to explain which overlap is altered by each motion. Do not infer a numerical energy barrier from a sketch.

### Q9

For CH2=CH–CH=CH2 in an approximately planar conformation, identify the continuous sequence of p orbitals and contrast it with CH2=CH–CH2–CH=CH2. Explain which structural feature interrupts that pathway in the second molecule.

### Q10

A student assigns one hybridisation label to an entire molecule. Use propyne and allene to test this procedure and devise a more reliable local procedure. State the electron-domain information and orbital-consistency checks your procedure needs.
