## A — Question set Q1–Q10

Grade 11 Chemistry: chemical bonding and hybridisation. Questions involving amide or radical models include advanced extensions.

### Q1

In the introductory valence-bond model, name the hybrid-orbital set used for four equivalent electron-domain directions around a central atom, and state how many hybrid orbitals it contains.

### Q2

The Lewis structure H2C=O has a double bond from carbon to oxygen and one single bond from carbon to each hydrogen. Explain why the C=O double bond counts as one electron domain around carbon.

### Q3

For CH4, use the known rule for four bonding domains and no central lone pair to assign the carbon hybridisation and electron-domain geometry. Show the domain count.

### Q4

NH3 has three N–H single bonds and one lone pair on nitrogen. Translate that information into a labelled diagram showing all four electron domains; distinguish the lone pair from the three bond directions. Do not treat the planar Lewis sketch as the molecular shape.

### Q5

A model is needed only to describe the overall three-dimensional arrangement of electron domains in NH3; it need not describe the detailed orbital overlap. Choose between VSEPR and a hybrid-orbital overlap diagram, and identify what your chosen model predicts and what it leaves unresolved.

### Q6

For a qualitative orbital sketch, overlap A is end-on along the internuclear axis and overlap B is side-on on opposite sides of that axis. Classify each as sigma or pi overlap and identify the defining feature you used.

### Q7

For CH4, sketch a three-dimensional arrangement of the four C–H bond directions using a wedge/dash convention or another clearly defined convention. Explain how your drawing differs from a flat cross-shaped Lewis sketch.

### Q8

A nitrogen atom in NF3 has three N–F single bonds and one lone pair. Each fluorine also has lone pairs. Count the electron domains around nitrogen and state which lone pairs belong in that central-atom count.

### Q9

A student counts the double bond in H2C=O as two separate directions and predicts four domains around carbon. Locate the counting error and give the corrected domain count.

### Q10

The benchmark supplies this table: BF3 has three bonding domains and no central lone pair; CH4 has four bonding domains and no central lone pair. Match each central atom to the introductory sp2 or sp3 model, and check that the number of hybrid-orbital directions agrees with the domain count.
