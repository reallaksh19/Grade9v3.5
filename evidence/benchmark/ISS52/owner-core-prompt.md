### Q1

Exact data give $p(0)=1$, $p(1)=2$, $p(2)=5$ and $p(3)=10$. First assume $p$ is a real polynomial of degree at most three: determine it and justify uniqueness. Then remove the degree bound and characterise every real polynomial consistent with the same four data points. State precisely which inference depended on the bound.

### Q2

A nonzero real polynomial has degree at most four and zeros at $-2$, $0$ and $2$. It changes sign across each of $-2$ and $2$, but has the same nonzero sign on the two sides sufficiently close to $0$. Also $p(1)=-3$. Construct the polynomial, justify the multiplicity information you use, and establish whether these conditions determine it uniquely. These are exact benchmark conditions, not measurements from a graph.

### Q3

Find the least possible degree of a real polynomial whose distinct real zeros are exactly $-1$ and $1$, and which satisfies $p(0)=p(2)=1$. Exhibit one such polynomial, verify that it has no other real zeros, and prove that every smaller degree is impossible. You may use continuity of real polynomial functions and the factor theorem. This is an advanced competition extension.

### Q4

For real $a$, consider $F_a(x)=(a-1)x^4+2(a-1)x^3+(a+2)x^2+2(a+2)x$. Develop a complete classification of its degree and number of distinct real zeros as $a$ varies. Identify all exceptional parameter values where the degree drops or zeros coincide, and account for multiplicities at those values. Justify that your cases are exhaustive.

### Q5

An open box is formed by cutting squares of side $x$ cm from the corners of a $10$ cm square sheet and folding the sides up, ignoring thickness. An exact calculation table gives volumes $0$, $64$ and $72$ cubic centimetres at $x=0,1,2$ respectively; $x=0$ denotes the flat limiting configuration. An analyst fits $Q(x)=-28x^2+92x$ to these three entries and treats it as the volume model. Derive the model from the geometry, state the domain for a non-degenerate box, assess the fitted model over that domain, and choose an additional admissible input that discriminates between the two accounts. Explain why matching the recorded entries is insufficient.

### Q6

For real $a$, let $R_a(x)=[x^2-(a+1)x+a]/(x-a)$ with its original domain $x\neq a$. A student cancels a factor and says that every $R_a$ is the same polynomial function on all real numbers, with zero $1$. Determine which parts of that statement hold on the original domains, classify the real zeros as $a$ varies, and describe the polynomial extension separately. Explain why extending a function and preserving its original domain are different operations.

### Q7

A real polynomial $p$ has degree at most four, satisfies $p(-x)=p(x)$ for every real $x$, and has $p(1)=p(2)=0$ and $p(0)=4$. Classify the following claims as necessary, possible but not necessary, or impossible under these conditions: its degree is two; it has exactly four distinct real zeros; it also has zero $3$; its leading coefficient is $1$; the data determine a unique polynomial. Justify each verdict. Reassess the claims when the degree bound is removed, while all other conditions are retained.

### Q8

Real polynomials $p$ and $q$ each have degree at most three. They agree at $x=-1,0,1$, but $p(2)-q(2)=12$. Characterise every possible difference polynomial, determine $p(3)-q(3)$, and decide whether the evidence identifies either polynomial individually. Use explicit examples to separate uniqueness of a difference from uniqueness of its two components.

### Q9

Characterise all monic real polynomials of degree at most four whose distinct real zeros are exactly $-1$ and $1$ and whose value at zero is $1$. Explain how repeated factors and any remaining quadratic factor affect completeness of your list. Prove both that every item in your list works and that no allowed polynomial has been omitted.

### Q10

A solver uses four rules: cancellation makes a rational expression a polynomial on all real numbers; the largest printed exponent is always the degree; a degree-$n$ polynomial has exactly $n$ distinct real zeros; agreement at three inputs proves an identity. Develop a qualified decision procedure and apply it to: $A_t(x)=(t-1)x^3+x^2-1$ with real $t$; $B(x)=(x^2-1)/(x-1)$ on its original domain; $C(x)=(x-2)^2(x^2+1)$; and the pair $D(x)=x^2+1$, $E(x)=x^2+1+x(x-1)(x-2)$ compared at $x=0,1,2$. State the relevant conditions and valid conclusions in each case, then create one fresh counterexample to an unqualified rule. Preserve the difference between expressions, functions, identities and finite observations.
