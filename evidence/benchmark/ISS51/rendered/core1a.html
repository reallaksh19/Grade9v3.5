The key step

A degree bound becomes useful only after the right auxiliary object is built; for identity, agreement must become zeros of a difference polynomial and the zero-polynomial branch must remain explicit.

Formula reference · meaning and conditions

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it

r=p-q; if deg r<=n and r has n+1 distinct zeros, nonzero r is impossible

Agreement becomes a root-count problem for the difference polynomial, with the zero-polynomial branch handled explicitly.

  • Agreement inputs are distinct.
  • The root-count theorem is invoked only after assuming r is nonzero.

p(r)=0 iff (x-r) is a factor of p(x)

For a polynomial, a real zero and the corresponding real linear factor are equivalent statements.

  • Use the factor theorem on the polynomial under discussion.

Worked explanation

Suppose f and g are real polynomials of degree at most one and agree at two distinct real inputs. Must they agree everywhere?

Follow the operation, its justification and its result.

  1. Define h=f-g.

  2. Translate the two agreements into h-zeros.

  3. Assume h nonzero and derive the degree-one contradiction.

  4. Interpret h identically zero.

Result

Yes. Let h=f-g; if h were nonzero it would have too many distinct zeros, so h is the zero polynomial.

Check

A nonzero degree-at-most-one polynomial has at most one distinct root.

A degree bound becomes useful only after the right auxiliary object is built; for identity, agreement must become zeros of a difference polynomial and the zero-polynomial branch must remain explicit.

Mistake clinic · diagnose and repair

A tempting wrong path

  • Every polynomial of degree at most n has at most n zeros, including the zero polynomial.

Diagnose

  • State the nonzero hypothesis before invoking the root-count theorem.

Repair

  • Use contradiction only for a nonzero difference; then interpret the identically-zero case.

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it

remaining area = whole area - removed area; degree is read after simplification

Leading polynomial terms can cancel when component quantities are combined.

  2. Expand and cancel x^2.

Result

The key step

A parameter condition constrains specific coefficients or values, while an auxiliary substitution is a mapping whose image must travel through the algebra.

Formula reference · meaning and conditions

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it

y=x^2 with real x implies y>=0

An auxiliary variable inherits the image of its defining substitution.

  • Original x is real.
  • Only y>=0 can map back to real x.

p(r)=0 iff (x-r) is a factor of p(x)

For a polynomial, a real zero and the corresponding real linear factor are equivalent statements.

  • Use the factor theorem on the polynomial under discussion.

Worked explanation

Solve x^4+3x^2-4=0 over the reals using y=x^2, preserving the auxiliary-variable condition.

Follow the operation, its justification and its result.

  1. Set y=x^2 with y>=0.

  2. Solve (y+4)(y-1)=0.

  3. Reject y=-4 and map y=1 to x=+/-1.

Result

The real zeros are +/-1.

Check

Direct substitution gives zero.

A parameter condition constrains specific coefficients or values, while an auxiliary substitution is a mapping whose image must travel through the algebra.

Repair
