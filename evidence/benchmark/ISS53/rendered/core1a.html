Worked explanation

Q1 · Owner-supplied question, verbatim

For $p(x)=4x^3-2x+7$, state its degree, leading coefficient, constant term and coefficient of $x^2$. Explain how the missing $x^2$ term is recorded.

Follow the operation, its justification and its result.

  1. Identify degree from highest power

    Why valid: The degree of a polynomial is the highest power of the variable with a non-zero coefficient. In 4x^3 - 2x + 7, the highest power is 3.

    Result: Degree = 3

  2. Extract leading coefficient and constant term

    Why valid: The leading coefficient is the coefficient of the highest power term (4 for x^3), and the constant term is the term independent of x (7).

    Result: Leading coeff = 4, Constant term = 7

  3. Evaluate missing x^2 term coefficient

    Why valid: The power x^2 does not appear explicitly in the polynomial. Its coefficient is recorded as 0 because 0 * x^2 = 0 contributes nothing to the sum.

    Result: Coefficient of x^2 = 0

  4. Confirm complete power sequence

    Why valid: Writing p(x) = 4x^3 + 0x^2 - 2x + 7 shows all integer powers 3, 2, 1, 0 are accounted for.

    Result: Verified complete representation

Result

Degree: 3; Leading coefficient: 4; Constant term: 7; Coefficient of $x^2$: 0. The missing $x^2$ term is recorded with coefficient 0 because $0x^2 = 0$, contributing nothing to the polynomial sum while preserving positional power sequencing.

Check

Reconstruct: $4x^3 + 0x^2 - 2x + 7 = 4x^3 - 2x + 7$; highest power is 3 with coeff 4, constant is 7.

Record missing powers with coefficient 0 and group like terms by exponent

The key step

An expression is a polynomial if and only if all powers of the variable are whole numbers; reciprocals (x^-1) and radicals (x^1/2) fail this condition.

Q2 · Owner-supplied question, verbatim

Which of $3x^2-2x+1$, $1/x+3$, $\sqrt{x}+1$ and $-5$ are polynomials in $x$ with real coefficients? For each accepted expression state its degree; for each rejected expression identify the defining condition it fails.

Follow the operation, its justification and its result.

  1. State polynomial defining criteria

    Why valid: A polynomial in x with real coefficients requires all exponents of x to be non-negative integers (whole numbers W = {0, 1, 2, ...}) and all coefficients to be real numbers.

    Result: Defining condition: exponents in W, coefficients in R

  2. Express each candidate expression in index power form

    Why valid: Rewrite: 3x^2 - 2x + 1 has powers 2, 1, 0; 1/x + 3 = x^-1 + 3 has power -1; sqrt(x) + 1 = x^(1/2) + 1 has power 1/2; -5 = -5x^0 has power 0.

    Result: Index forms: x^2, x^-1, x^(1/2), x^0

  3. Classify accepted polynomials and state degrees

    Why valid: 3x^2 - 2x + 1 is a polynomial of degree 2 (highest power 2); -5 is a constant polynomial of degree 0 (power 0 in W).

    Result: Accepted: 3x^2-2x+1 (deg 2), -5 (deg 0)

  4. Identify defining condition failures for rejected expressions

    Why valid: 1/x + 3 fails because exponent -1 is negative (-1 not in W); sqrt(x) + 1 fails because exponent 1/2 is a fraction (1/2 not in W).

    Result: Rejected: 1/x+3 (negative exponent -1), sqrt(x)+1 (fractional exponent 1/2)

Result

Accepted: $3x^2-2x+1$ (degree 2) and $-5$ (degree 0). Rejected: $1/x+3$ (fails condition because exponent of $x$ is $-1$, a negative integer) and $\sqrt{x}+1$ (fails condition because exponent of $x$ is $1/2$, a fraction/non-integer).

Check

Check exponent sets: $\{2,1,0\} \subset \mathbb{W}$ (valid); $\{-1,0\} \not\subset \mathbb{W}$ (invalid); $\{1/2,0\} \not\subset \mathbb{W}$ (invalid); $\{0\} \subset \mathbb{W}$ (valid).

Check all exponents against non-negative integers {0, 1, 2, ...} and evaluate with brackets

Mistake clinic · diagnose and repair

A tempting wrong path

Diagnose

Repair

  • 1/x = x^-1 has negative exponent -1 not in W; constant -5 = -5x^0 has valid whole-number exponent 0.

  2. 2 · ApplyAPPLY: Substitute x=2 into p(x) with brackets: (2)^2 - 3(2) + 4 = 2.
  3. 3 · ConnectCONNECT: Connect constant -5 to non-zero constant polynomial of degree 0.

The key step

Identities hold for all real numbers due to algebraic field axioms; linear polynomials are uniquely fixed by two independent constraints (constant term and zero).

Formula reference · meaning and conditions

Equations and validity

EquationWhat it tells youWhen you can use it

(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2

Expansion of a binomial square via the distributive law yields four terms combining to three distinct terms with cross-term 2ab.

  • Holds for all a, b in R

a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)

Difference of two squares factors into the product of conjugate binomials whose cross-terms cancel.

  • Holds for all a, b in R

p(x) = ax + b, a != 0, zero at x = -b/a

A linear polynomial has constant term p(0)=b and a unique zero at x = -b/a.

  • a != 0

Area(x) = (x + a)(x + b) = x^2 + (a + b)x + ab

Physical rectangular area is modeled by the product of length and width binomials in cm^2.

  • x >= 0
  • length > 0
  • width > 0

Worked explanation

Q10 · Owner-supplied question, verbatim

Construct a linear polynomial whose constant term is $2$ and whose zero is $1$. Check both conditions and state whether more than one linear polynomial can satisfy them.

Follow the operation, its justification and its result.

  1. Formulate general linear polynomial

    Why valid: A linear polynomial in one variable has the general form p(x) = ax + b, where a, b in R and leading coefficient a != 0.

    Result: p(x) = ax + b, a != 0

  2. Apply constant term condition to determine b

    Why valid: The constant term is p(0) = a(0) + b = b. Given constant term is 2, so b = 2.

    Result: b = 2

  3. Apply zero condition

    Why valid: A zero at x=1 means p(1) = 0. Substituting x=1 and b=2 gives a(1) + 2 = 0.

    Result: a(1) + 2 = 0

  4. Solve for leading coefficient a

    Why valid: Solving the linear equation a + 2 = 0 yields a = -2, which is non-zero (degree 1 holds).

    Result: a = -2 (non-zero)

  5. Verify both conditions and prove uniqueness

    Why valid: Check: p(0) = -2(0) + 2 = 2; p(1) = -2(1) + 2 = 0; deg = 1. Uniqueness: a+2=0 has a unique solution in R, so exactly ONE linear polynomial satisfies both conditions.

    Result: Constructed p(x) = -2x + 2; unique linear polynomial

Result

The constructed linear polynomial is $p(x) = -2x + 2$. Verification: (1) Constant term is $p(0) = -2(0) + 2 = 2$; (2) Zero is $p(1) = -2(1) + 2 = 0$; (3) Degree is 1 ($a = -2 \ne 0$). Exactly one linear polynomial satisfies these conditions: any linear polynomial has form $ax+b$ ($a \ne 0$); condition $p(0)=2$ forces $b=2$, and condition $p(1)=0$ forces $a(1)+2=0 \implies a=-2$. Since the linear system for coefficients has a unique solution, no other linear polynomial can satisfy them.

Check

Check $p(0) = -2(0) + 2 = 2$ (constant term holds); $p(1) = -2(1) + 2 = 0$ (zero holds); $\deg(p) = 1$ (linear holds). Uniqueness: $b=2$ is fixed by constant term; $a(1)+2=0 \iff a=-2$, unique root in $\mathbb{R}$.

Mistake clinic · diagnose and repair

A tempting wrong path

  • (a+b)^2 = a^2 + b^2 omitting the middle cross-term 2ab, or assuming point substitution proves an identity universally.

Diagnose

  • Evaluate (1+3)^2 versus 1^2 + 3^2, or ask if x^2 = x holding at x=0, 1 makes it an identity.

Repair

  • Distributivity produces two middle terms ab + ba = 2ab; point checks do not establish universal validity.
