Grade 9 Mathematics: polynomials in one variable with real coefficients. Competition/advanced extensions are explicitly marked; no calculus or complex-number prerequisite is assumed. Preserve stated domains and degree bounds.

### Q1

For $p(x)=4x^3-2x+7$, state its degree, leading coefficient, constant term and coefficient of $x^2$. Explain how the missing $x^2$ term is recorded.

### Q2

Which of $3x^2-2x+1$, $1/x+3$, $\sqrt{x}+1$ and $-5$ are polynomials in $x$ with real coefficients? For each accepted expression state its degree; for each rejected expression identify the defining condition it fails.

### Q3

For $p(x)=x^2-3x+4$, calculate $p(2)$. Show the substitution with brackets before simplifying.

### Q4

Add $p(x)=2x^2+3x-5$ and $q(x)=-x^2+4x+2$. Write the result in descending powers and identify which terms can be combined.

### Q5

Translate $p(x)=5x^3-2x+7$ into a coefficient table with one row for each power $3,2,1,0$. Reconstruct the polynomial from your table and explain what a zero entry means.

### Q6

A student writes $(x+3)^2=x^2+9$. Use the product $(x+3)(x+3)$ to locate the error and give the corrected expansion.

### Q7

A rectangle has length $(x+3)$ cm and width $(x+1)$ cm, where $x\geq0$. Write a polynomial for its area, identify the output unit and state which feature of the rectangle your expression represents.

### Q8

Factorise $x^2-9$ using a familiar algebraic identity. Check your factorisation by multiplying the factors.

### Q9

Are $x(x+1)$ and $x^2+x$ equal for every real $x$? Give a reason that applies to every value, rather than relying on one numerical substitution.

### Q10

Construct a linear polynomial whose constant term is $2$ and whose zero is $1$. Check both conditions and state whether more than one linear polynomial can satisfy them.
