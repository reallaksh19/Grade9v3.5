Domain count before hybridisation label

Subject: ChemistryTopic: Chemical bonding and hybridisationConcept: Domain count before hybridisation labelConcept difficulty: Medium concept
1

Choose the local-domain ledger before the hybrid label

Medium concept
Section 1 of 3Next →

The key step

A sigma-bond count is not the local domain count when lone pairs are present; choose the introductory hybrid model only after the full domain inventory.

What you need before this

Prerequisites

  • Can draw simple Lewis structures.
  • Can count central electron domains.
  • Knows introductory 2/3/4-domain geometry rules.
Why this section matters for the question set

  • Q6 · D1
    The move learners miss: Keep the four-direction domain count fixed while distinguishing it from bond-specific properties. Try it in Core2
  1. Separate sigma-bond count from total local electron-domain count.
    Why valid: Lone pairs occupy local electron-density regions even though they are not sigma bonds.
    Result: two distinct ledgers
  2. The step the hard question turns onInventory bond directions and lone-pair domains before choosing the introductory hybrid label.
    Why valid: The introductory mapping is keyed to regions/directions, not merely the number of bonded atoms.
    Result: full local domain count
  3. Check the proposed label against the electron-domain geometry and note that shape is model evidence, not direct orbital measurement.
    Why valid: This prevents a shape-only proof claim.
    Result: bounded model assignment

    Result: crux identified

  2. Predict step 2, then reveal

    Why valid: This is the reviewed learner-relative bridge from demonstrated knowledge to the requested representation/explanation.

    Result: question-specific model

  3. Predict step 3, then reveal

Result and independent check

Result

BF3 is treated by the introductory localized model as three electron domains at B: sp2 with trigonal-planar bonded-atom arrangement. NH3 has four electron domains at N because its lone pair is an additional domain: sp3, tetrahedral electron-domain geometry and trigonal-pyramidal molecular shape. Therefore three sigma bonds alone are insufficient.

Sigma/pi orbital inventory without double-counting

Subject: ChemistryTopic: Chemical bonding and hybridisationConcept: Sigma/pi orbital inventory without double-countingConcept difficulty: Hard concept
1

Translate domain count into sigma-framework and remaining-p orbital jobs

Hard concept
Section 2 of 3← PreviousNext →

The key step

Build the sigma-direction frame first, then allocate only the remaining unhybridised p orbital(s) to pi overlap; never count a pi component as a new sigma/domain direction.

What you need before this

Prerequisites

  • Can count electron domains.
  • Knows introductory 2/3/4-domain geometry rules.
Why this section matters for the question set

This unit builds toward

  • Q3 · D2
    The move learners miss: Place the sigma framework in the molecular plane and then add the two parallel unhybridised p orbitals perpendicular to it for side-on overlap. Try it in Core2
  • Q4 · D2
    The move learners miss: Coordinate the linear two-sp-hybrid sigma framework with two remaining perpendicular p orbitals, one for each pi component. Try it in Core2
  • Q9 · D2
    The move learners miss: Assign each of the three sigma directions to a distinct sp2 hybrid and reserve the unhybridised p orbital for pi overlap. Try it in Core2
  1. Keep the domain-direction ledger separate from the sigma/pi bond-component ledger.
    Why valid: A multiple bond is one local direction even though it contains more than one bond component.
    Result: two named ledgers
  2. The step the hard question turns onConstruct the hybrid sigma framework from the local domain count, then identify the unhybridised p orbital(s) left over.
    Why valid: Hybridisation changes the basis used for the sigma framework while leaving specific p directions available for pi overlap.
    Result: orbital inventory
  3. Orient remaining p orbitals for side-on overlap: perpendicular to an sp2 molecular plane, or as two independent p directions around an sp linear axis.
    Why valid: Pi overlap requires parallel p orbitals and is geometrically distinct from sigma overlap along the axis.
    Result: qualitative overlap model
  4. Close the inventory by checking each local valence orbital has one assigned role and each multiple bond has exactly one sigma component.
    Why valid: This catches double-counting and domain/component conflation.
    Result: closed orbital ledger

Q9 · Owner-supplied question, verbatim

A carbon centre has three sigma-bond directions, no lone pair, and participates in one pi bond. Translate this description into a qualitative local orbital arrangement and check that the orbitals used in the sigma and pi parts have not been counted twice.

Before opening each step, say what you would do next.

  1. Predict step 1, then reveal
    How to allocate one carbon's valence orbitals to three sigma directions plus one pi participation without reusing or double-counting an orbital.

    Why valid: The question turns on identifying the correct counting/model distinction before a label is chosen.

    Result: crux identified

  2. Predict step 2, then reveal
    Assign each of the three sigma directions to a distinct sp2 hybrid and reserve the unhybridised p orbital for pi overlap.

    Why valid: This is the reviewed learner-relative bridge from demonstrated knowledge to the requested representation/explanation.

    Result: question-specific model

  3. Predict step 3, then reveal
    The local valence-orbital inventory closes at four: three sp2 hybrids assigned to sigma directions and one unhybridised p assigned to pi participation.

    Why valid: An independent ledger/domain check tests the conclusion without merely restating it.

    Result: verified response

Result and independent check

Result

Three sigma-bond directions and no lone pair give three local electron domains, so the introductory local model is sp2. Three sp2 hybrid orbitals provide the three coplanar sigma directions. The one remaining unhybridised p orbital is perpendicular to that plane and supplies the atomic orbital for the pi component. It is not a fourth electron-domain direction and is not one of the three sigma hybrids.

Check

The local valence-orbital inventory closes at four: three sp2 hybrids assigned to sigma directions and one unhybridised p assigned to pi participation.

Translate domain count into sigma-framework and remaining-p orbital jobs

Mistake clinic · diagnose and repair

A tempting wrong path

  • A pi bond is an extra electron-domain direction or can reuse a sigma hybrid orbital.

Diagnose

  • After the sigma framework is allocated, which local atomic orbitals remain unused?

Repair

  • Give each local orbital one bonding job: hybrid directions support the sigma framework and separate unhybridised p orbitals support side-on pi overlap.

Electron-domain geometry versus molecular shape

Subject: ChemistryTopic: Chemical bonding and hybridisationConcept: Electron-domain geometry versus molecular shapeConcept difficulty: Medium concept
1

Project an all-domain geometry to the bonded-atom molecular shape

Medium concept
Section 3 of 3← Previous

The key step

Electron-domain geometry names all local regions; molecular shape names bonded-atom positions, so lone pairs can change the latter without changing the former.

What you need before this

Prerequisites

  • Can count central domains.
  • Knows the four-domain tetrahedral rule.
Why this section matters for the question set

This unit builds toward

  • Q1 · D2
    The move learners miss: Count all local electron domains first, then connect 3 domains to the introductory sp2 model and 4 domains to sp3 while separating electron-domain geometry from molecular shape. Try it in Core2
  • Q8 · D2
    The move learners miss: Compare which of the four domain positions are occupied by bonded atoms versus a lone pair at C and N. Try it in Core2
  • Q10 · D2
    The move learners miss: Compare CH4, NH3 and H2O while holding four total domains fixed and varying lone-pair occupancy. Try it in Core2
  1. Name electron-domain geometry and molecular shape as different projections.
    Why valid: One includes all regions; the other records atom positions.
    Result: two geometry meanings
  2. The step the hard question turns onMark bond versus lone-pair occupancy on the common domain scaffold.
    Why valid: Changing occupancy changes which vertices are occupied by atoms.
    Result: occupied atom subset
  3. Check CH4, NH3 and H2O as 0/1/2-lone-pair cases with four total domains.
    Why valid: A shared total domain count with different atom subsets is a direct counterexample to conflating the names.
    Result: shape distinction

Watch one · predict before reveal

Q10 · Owner-supplied question, verbatim

A student says, 'If the electron-domain geometry is tetrahedral, the atoms must form a tetrahedral molecular shape.' Evaluate the statement using CH4, NH3 and H2O, with their respective central lone-pair counts of zero, one and two.

Before opening each step, say what you would do next.

  1. Predict step 1, then reveal
    Whether tetrahedral electron-domain geometry logically entails tetrahedral molecular shape.

    Why valid: The question turns on identifying the correct counting/model distinction before a label is chosen.

    Result: crux identified

  2. Predict step 2, then reveal
    Compare CH4, NH3 and H2O while holding four total domains fixed and varying lone-pair occupancy.

    Why valid: This is the reviewed learner-relative bridge from demonstrated knowledge to the requested representation/explanation.

    Result: question-specific model

  3. Predict step 3, then reveal
    All three examples total four electron domains, but their atom counts around the centre are 4, 3 and 2, producing tetrahedral, trigonal-pyramidal and bent molecular shapes respectively.

    Why valid: An independent ledger/domain check tests the conclusion without merely restating it.

    Result: verified response

Result and independent check

Result

The statement is false. CH4, NH3 and H2O each have four central electron domains and therefore tetrahedral electron-domain geometry in VSEPR. CH4 has 0 lone pairs, so four bonded atoms form a tetrahedral molecular shape; NH3 has 1 lone pair, so three bonded atoms form a trigonal-pyramidal shape; H2O has 2 lone pairs, so two bonded atoms form a bent shape. Electron-domain geometry includes lone pairs; molecular shape names the atom positions.

Check

All three examples total four electron domains, but their atom counts around the centre are 4, 3 and 2, producing tetrahedral, trigonal-pyramidal and bent molecular shapes respectively.

Project an all-domain geometry to the bonded-atom molecular shape

Mistake clinic · diagnose and repair

A tempting wrong path

  • Tetrahedral electron-domain geometry means the bonded atoms must form a tetrahedron.

Diagnose

  • Which domain positions contain atoms and which contain lone pairs?

Repair

  • Keep the tetrahedral all-domain scaffold, then derive molecular shape from only the bonded-atom positions.
