Parameter Cancellation, Degree and the Zero-Polynomial Boundary

Subject: MathematicsTopic: Grade 9 Mathematics — PolynomialsConcept: Parameter Cancellation, Degree and the Zero-Polynomial BoundaryConcept difficulty: Hard concept
1

Branch on coefficient collapse before assigning degree or invoking a zero bound.

Hard concept
Section 1 of 3Next →

Conditions for this construction

  • Can collect polynomial terms and identify degree in a simple nonzero polynomial.
  • Can substitute a real parameter value into coefficients.
Why this section matters for the question set

This unit builds toward

  • Q10 · D3 · the hardest question in this set
    The move learners miss: Branch on whether a-1 is zero before assigning degree. Try it in Core2
  1. Factor or isolate the parameter-dependent coefficient shared by the leading term or all terms.
    Why valid: Degree is defined from nonzero coefficients, so parameter-controlled cancellation is the first structural question.
    Result: The controlling coefficient is visible.
  2. The step the hard question turns onSolve when the controlling coefficient is zero and split exceptional from ordinary parameter values.
    Why valid: The same printed family can represent polynomials of different degree when a coefficient vanishes.
    Result: Separate parameter branches.
  3. Assign degree only after the branch is known to be nonzero.
    Why valid: A leading power contributes to degree only when its coefficient is nonzero; the task stipulates undefined degree for the zero polynomial.
    Result: A valid degree statement or zero-polynomial exception.
  4. Before using a degree-based zero bound, check its nonzero-polynomial hypothesis.
    Why valid: A theorem cannot contradict an object excluded by its hypothesis.
    Result: The zero-count statement is scoped to the nonzero branch.

The key step

Do not assign degree from printed exponents first. Check whether the parameter makes the relevant coefficient zero, and treat the all-zero case separately before using a theorem whose hypothesis says nonzero polynomial.

Worked explanation

For h_t(x)=(t+2)(x-4)^2, determine when h_t is the zero polynomial and determine its degree otherwise, using the same zero-polynomial convention.

Follow the operation, its justification and its result.

  1. Keep the scalar t+2 visibly factored from (x-4)^2.

    Why valid: The scalar controls all coefficients at once.

    Result: One coefficient branch controls zero versus nonzero polynomial.

  2. Branch on t+2=0 before assigning degree.

    Why valid: Degree depends on the highest nonzero coefficient, so the all-zero case must be separated first.

    Result: t=-2 is exceptional; t≠-2 is nonzero.

  3. Substitute one value from each branch.

    Why valid: Direct substitution checks both cases.

    Result: Zero polynomial versus degree-2 polynomial.

Result

If t=-2, h_t is the zero polynomial and its degree is undefined under the convention. If t≠-2, h_t has degree 2.

Check

Test t=-2 to get 0 for every x and t=0 to get 2(x-4)^2.

Branch on coefficient collapse before assigning degree or invoking a zero bound.

Mistake clinic · diagnose and repair

A tempting wrong path

  • The highest printed exponent determines degree even when its coefficient is zero, and the root bound applies to the zero polynomial.

Diagnose

  • For h_b(x)=b(x-2)^2, what changes at b=0 before you count zeros?

Repair

  • Branch on b=0 first. Only a nonzero leading coefficient supports degree 2 and the nonzero-polynomial zero bound.

1-2-3 quick check

  1. 1 · CheckSubstitute the exceptional parameter into every coefficient and verify each becomes zero.
  2. 2 · ApplyChange the scalar to b+1 in (b+1)(x-2)^2 and classify b=-1 versus b=0 before reading degree.
  3. 3 · ConnectBefore using a theorem, state its nonzero-polynomial hypothesis and identify which branch satisfies it.
Question-specific diagnosis and repair

Repair for Q10 · zero-polynomial parameter boundary

Test the rule on a changed case

For h_b(x)=b(x-2)^2, predict the degree and real zero set when b=0 and when b=5. Explain which branch is covered by the degree-two zero bound.

Expected pattern: For b=5 the polynomial has degree 2 and zero x=2 (multiplicity 2); for b=0 it is the zero polynomial, has no defined degree under the stated convention, and every real number is a zero.

This pattern suggests a rule to revisit; it does not diagnose you or certify mastery.

Replacement rule: Degree and the usual finite zero bound apply to a nonzero polynomial. Under this task's convention the zero polynomial has no defined degree and vanishes at every real input.

Fresh application: For j_c(x)=(c+4)(x^3-1), branch on c=-4 versus c≠-4 before naming a degree or using a root-count bound.

Return to the question

Now you do one

For m_c(x)=(c-5)x^3+(c-5), classify the zero-polynomial case and the degree otherwise.

Evaluation, Factors, Zeros and Algebraic Warrants

Subject: MathematicsTopic: Grade 9 Mathematics — PolynomialsConcept: Evaluation, Factors, Zeros and Algebraic WarrantsConcept difficulty: Medium concept
1

Turn evaluation at c into remainder/factor information, then verify by a different operation.

Medium concept
Section 2 of 3← PreviousNext →

Conditions for this construction

  • Can substitute values into a polynomial.
  • Can expand elementary products and collect like terms.
Why this section matters for the question set

This unit builds toward

  • Q7 · D2
    The move learners miss: Translate the factor condition into f(1)=0. Try it in Core2
  1. Write p(x)=(x-c)q(x)+r for division by a linear divisor.
    Why valid: The division algorithm leaves a constant remainder because its degree is less than 1.
    Result: A form in which evaluation at c isolates the remainder.
  2. The step the hard question turns onSet x=c to obtain p(c)=r; when r=0, connect the zero to factor x-c.
    Why valid: The factor term vanishes exactly, establishing the Remainder Theorem and its Factor-Theorem case.
    Result: Evaluation, remainder and factor information are coordinated.
  3. Use expansion, multiplication-back or exact substitution as an independent check.
    Why valid: A different operation can catch a coefficient or scale error that the original route misses.
    Result: A non-circular verification.

The key step

For division by x-c, evaluating at c isolates the remainder; when that value is zero, x-c is a factor. Universal identities need an algebraic warrant, not a finite sample.

Worked explanation

For F(x)=x^3+t x^2-4x+2, find t so that x-2 is a factor, then state how you would verify the factorisation by a route different from the substitution that found t.

Follow the operation, its justification and its result.

  1. Translate x-2 being a factor into F(2)=0.

    Why valid: The Factor Theorem links x-c to the zero c.

    Result: A linear equation for t.

  2. Solve for t and divide by x-2.

    Why valid: A zero remainder yields a polynomial quotient.

    Result: Parameter and quotient.

  3. Multiply the quotient by x-2.

    Why valid: Multiplication-back checks every coefficient without repeating F(2)=0.

    Result: Independent factorisation verification.

Result

Use F(2)=0 to determine t, divide by x-2, then multiply the factor and quotient to verify every coefficient.

Check

Multiplication-back is independent of the factor-theorem substitution.

Turn evaluation at c into remainder/factor information, then verify by a different operation.

Mistake clinic · diagnose and repair

A tempting wrong path

  • A successful sample proves an identity, or one factor condition certifies the whole factorisation.

Diagnose

  • If two expressions agree at x=0, what additional warrant establishes equality for every real x?

Repair

  • A universal identity needs algebra valid for arbitrary x; a proposed factorisation should be expanded or multiplied back.

1-2-3 quick check

  1. 1 · CheckAfter obtaining a factor and quotient, multiply them and compare every coefficient with the original polynomial.
  2. 2 · ApplyFor p(x)=x^2-1, use p(1) to predict the remainder on division by x-1 before factoring.
  3. 3 · ConnectExplain why one counterexample can disprove an identity while agreeing sample points do not prove it.

Now you do one

Let p(x)=x^3-3x+2. Without long division, find the remainder on division by x-1 and decide whether x-1 is a factor. Explain the link.

Modelling, Tables/Graphs and Domain-Preserving Simplification

Subject: MathematicsTopic: Grade 9 Mathematics — PolynomialsConcept: Modelling, Tables/Graphs and Domain-Preserving SimplificationConcept difficulty: Medium concept
1

Preserve the invariant and the original domain while changing representation or simplifying.

Medium concept
Section 3 of 3← Previous

Conditions for this construction

  • Can multiply binomials and collect terms.
  • Can substitute inputs and read ordered pairs.
  • Can identify when a denominator is zero.
Why this section matters for the question set

This unit builds toward

  • Q6 · D3
    The move learners miss: Factor x^2-1 and cancel x-1 only under x≠1. Try it in Core2
  1. Write down the invariant before translating: total area, input-output value or original allowed input set.
    Why valid: A representation is faithful only when the preserved meaning is explicit.
    Result: A named invariant guides the translation.
  2. The step the hard question turns onCarry out the algebraic or graphical translation while keeping the invariant visible.
    Why valid: Expansion, plotting and cancellation change appearance but not valid values under their conditions.
    Result: A second representation of the same valid information.
  3. Translate back or test a boundary/special input.
    Why valid: A reverse or boundary check detects lost signs, areas, points or domain restrictions.
    Result: The translation is checked independently.
  4. Carry the original excluded input into the final function comparison and test that boundary explicitly.
    Why valid: Algebraic simplification preserves values only on the original domain; function equality also requires the domains to agree.
    Result: The final comparison states both the simplified rule and the original domain boundary.

The key step

A changed representation must preserve the same mathematical object and constraints. Cancelling a factor can simplify a formula without enlarging the original function's domain.

Worked explanation

Compare H(x)=(x^2-9)/(x-3) with K(x)=x+3. Simplify H where valid and decide whether H and K are the same real function.

Follow the operation, its justification and its result.

  1. Record x≠3 before simplifying.

    Why valid: The original denominator is zero at x=3.

    Result: domain(H)=R\{3}.

  2. Factor and cancel only for x≠3.

    Why valid: Cancellation divides by x-3 and requires it to be nonzero.

    Result: H(x)=x+3 on its original domain.

  3. Compare the excluded input with K.

    Why valid: Function equality includes domain equality.

    Result: H is undefined at 3 while K(3)=6.

Result

H(x)=x+3 only for x≠3. H is undefined at 3 while K(3)=6, so they are not the same function on all real numbers.

Check

At x=4 both give 7; at x=3 only K is defined.

Preserve the invariant and the original domain while changing representation or simplifying.

Mistake clinic · diagnose and repair

A tempting wrong path

  • Cancelling a common factor permanently removes the original denominator restriction.

Diagnose

  • After simplifying (x^2-4)/(x-2) to x+2, what is the value of the original expression at x=2?

Repair

  • There is no original value at x=2. Record the domain first and keep it after cancellation.

1-2-3 quick check

  1. 1 · CheckTest one ordinary input in both the original and simplified formulas.
  2. 2 · ApplyFor (x^2-25)/(x-5), state the excluded input before cancelling and keep it in the final function description.
  3. 3 · ConnectWhen plotting a value table, identify zeros from y=0 rather than merely from the fact that a point was plotted.
Question-specific diagnosis and repair

Repair for Q6 · cancellation with original domain

Test the rule on a changed case

For h(x)=(x^2-4)/(x-2), compare h with x+2. State the domain first, then decide whether the two are the same function on all real numbers.

Expected pattern: h(x)=x+2 only for x≠2; h is undefined at 2 while x+2 equals 4 there.

This pattern suggests a rule to revisit; it does not diagnose you or certify mastery.

Replacement rule: Cancelling a nonzero factor preserves values on the original domain; it does not add an input at which the original denominator was zero.

Fresh application: Apply the same domain ledger to (x^2-9)/(x-3) before cancelling.

Return to the question

Simplify s(x)=(x^2-16)/(x-4) where valid and compare it with t(x)=x+4 as real functions.
