## A — Question set Q1–Q10

Grade 9 Mathematics: polynomials in one variable with real coefficients. Competition/advanced extensions are explicitly marked; no calculus or complex-number prerequisite is assumed. Preserve stated domains and degree bounds.

### Q1

For $p_k(x)=(k-2)x^3+3x^2-x+4$, where $k$ is real, classify the degree when $k=2$ and when $k\neq2$. Explain why the highest printed exponent is not sufficient to decide the degree.

### Q2

Find the remainder when $p(x)=x^3-4x+1$ is divided by $x-2$, without carrying out long division. Explain why your chosen computation gives the remainder.

### Q3

Factorise $x^2-5x+6$ and find its real zeros. Verify the factors by expansion and each zero by substitution.

### Q4

Two non-overlapping rectangular tiles have dimensions $(x+2)$ cm by $(x+1)$ cm, and $x$ cm by $(x+3)$ cm, with $x>0$. Construct and simplify a polynomial for their total area. Identify which part of your model accounts for adding the two areas.

### Q5

For $p(x)=(x-1)(x+1)$, produce a value table at $x=-2,-1,0,1,2$ and plot those five ordered pairs on labelled axes. Mark the zeros and distinguish a zero of the polynomial from an arbitrary plotted point.

### Q6

Compare the original expression $r(x)=(x^2-1)/(x-1)$ with $q(x)=x+1$. State the domain of $r$, simplify it where valid, and decide whether $r$ and $q$ are the same function on all real numbers. Address $x=1$ explicitly.

### Q7

For $f(x)=2x^3+kx^2-5x+2$, find the real value of $k$ for which $x-1$ is a factor. Then exhibit a quotient and verify the resulting product without merely repeating the same substitution.

### Q8

A student checks $(2x)^2=2x^2$ at $x=0$ and calls it an identity. Decide whether that check is sufficient, give an exact counterexample if needed, and state a corrected identity with a general reason.

### Q9

Construct a quadratic polynomial with real zeros $-2$ and $3$ and with $p(0)=-6$. Explain how the value at zero fixes the scale, then check all three conditions.

### Q10

For $g_a(x)=(a-1)x^2+2(a-1)x+(a-1)$, determine when it is the zero polynomial and determine its degree otherwise. Use the convention that the zero polynomial has no defined degree. Assess whether an arbitrarily large number of zeros in the exceptional case contradicts the usual degree bound for a nonzero polynomial.
